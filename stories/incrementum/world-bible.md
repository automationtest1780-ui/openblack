# How to Use This Document

# 1. Core Concept and Theme

## Elevator Pitch

In the harbor town of Saltreach, the stars have started to move. The Ploughman's Belt, fixed for three hundred years, has shifted a finger's width east, and the town council calls it autumn haze. Four people know better: a surveyor who won't stop counting, a lamplighter who won't let her climb alone, the magistrate who saw it first and buried it, and a girl who fell out of the sky into the salt marsh and grows a little every time the stars shift. Something up there is growing, and it is using the ground as its measuring stick. Incrementum is a 4v4 hero gacha about what grows, what holds still, and what four people do when the sky has to move.

## The Theme: Growth Aimed at the Stars

*Incrementum* is Latin for growth or increase. Poets also used it for offspring, the thing that grows out of something else. Both meanings matter. Something in the sky is growing and pushing the old stars aside. Wren is what grew out of it. Each hero has to grow past a flaw, and the sky is the outside version of that inner change. The ground is the measuring stick: just as a child is measured against a doorframe, the sky's growth is measured against the earth, and Saltreach is the doorframe.

The title line, *"Grow until the sky has to move,"* reads three ways. It is the sky's threat, Wren's destiny, and the player's progression loop.

## The Title Screen

The title screen is as important as the story. Treat it as a story beat.

**Working recommendation:**

- **Image:** The dead lighthouse at dusk, seen from the salt marsh. The lamp room glows faintly (Bram's lantern). Above it, the Ploughman's Belt.

- **Living sky:** As the campaign goes on, the title-screen sky changes to match it. The Belt drifts further east and new light gathers in the gap. A player coming back after a month should see that the sky has moved.

- **Line:** *"Grow until the sky has to move."* in plain serif, with no other tagline.

- **After the ending:** One new fixed light sits low over the lighthouse. It is Wren (see Section 7).

# 2. The World

## Saltreach

Saltreach is a cold-water harbor town of a few thousand people, where a grey river meets the sea, with salt marsh to the north and a stone breakwater around the anchorage. It is practical and proud of being sensible, and it distrusts excitement.

**The Harbor.** A working harbor of herring boats, crab pots, net lofts, smokehouses, and a single customs house. Sera Vant's tide tables are nailed up at every slip.

**The Old Lighthouse (the dead light).** A tall stone tower on the headland, kept by four generations of the Hollis family. Eleven years ago the council built a new light on the breakwater and let the old tower go dark. Inside, a spiral stair climbs to the **lamp room**: a round room of salt-stained glass, an empty lamp cradle, and a stone wall that Sera has nearly covered in chalk, one mark every dusk for the sky's drift. Bram still climbs the stair to oil hinges nobody uses, and that is how he found Sera there the first night. The lamp room is the game's **home base**.

**The New Breakwater Light.** It's squat and efficient, built by the council and run by a hired keeper. It's brighter and safer than the old tower, and nobody loves it.

**The Salt Marsh and the Crater.** North of town lie reed beds, tidal channels, and salt pans. Three nights after the Belt moved, something fell there. The crater is a bowl of fused glass, sand an hour before it fell, still faintly warm. Reeds around it now lean toward the center.

**The Harbor Council.** Seven seats, chaired by Magistrate Ivo Thane. It meets in the customs house and officially has nothing to do with the sky. When Sera presented her measurements, Ivo called them haze in open session, and the council minuted it that way.

**The Bad Year.** Last year the herring didn't come where they should have. Boats were sold, two families left, and the smokehouses ran half empty. That is the ground Ivo is standing on when he chooses the lie.

**Daily Life.** People are up before light for the boats, mend nets in the afternoon, and eat supper early because lamp oil costs money. Children learn the tide tables before they learn their letters. The town copes by understating things, so a crisis gets called "a bit of weather."

**How Townsfolk Talk About the Sky.** Officially it's *weather* or *autumn haze*. Unofficially:

- "Sky's restless."

- "The Belt's slipping," said quietly, and only by old fishermen who steer by it.

- "Don't look up after supper," a saying that spreads in Act II.

- Skipping rhyme: *"Ploughman, Ploughman, where's your belt? / Lost it in the marsh where the glass is felt."*

## The Sky

The sky over Saltreach is a sailor's sky. Its constellations are named for working things, and people steer by them.

| **Constellation**        | **Description**                                                                       | **Status** |
|--------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|------------|
| **The Ploughman's Belt** | Three bright stars in a line. Fixed for 300 years, now shifted a finger's width east. | **Canon**  |
| **The Nail**             | A single steady star low in the north that navigators steer by.                       | Working    |
| **The Net-Mender**       | A hunched figure of faint stars that sailors say is mending the sky.                  | Working    |
| **The Drowned Bell**     | A ring of stars that sets into the sea in winter.                                     | Working    |
| **The Heron**            | A long-legged shape over the marsh in autumn. Its stars thin out as Wren grows.       | Working    |

**What is known (in-world):**

- The Belt has moved east, and it keeps moving, faster each time.

- The movement is regular enough to measure. Sera's wall and Ivo's ledger agree.

- Something fell into the marsh three nights after the first shift.

- The tides are slightly off Sera's tables, and they're getting worse.

**What is unknown (in-world):**

- What is moving the stars.

- Whether it is aware of Saltreach at all.

- Why Wren grows when the sky shifts.

- Whether it can be stopped, and whether it should be.

## Beyond Saltreach (Open / Expandable)

These are hooks for future quartets, not settled places.

- **Ferrow (the Ploughlands).** Inland farm country where the Ploughman myth comes from. Farmers there plant by the Belt, and its movement has thrown off a whole harvest calendar. *Hook:* a quartet of farmers and almanac-makers whose argument is about faith, not truth.

- **Bellwater.** A half-drowned city to the south whose bell tower rings by itself when the Drowned Bell rises. *Hook:* a quartet who have been hiding a moving sky for a generation, an older version of Ivo's lie.

- **Mount Quell.** A cold observatory-monastery whose keepers believe the stars are nails that hold the sky up. *Hook:* scholars who know too much and have decided that measuring is a sacred duty.

# 3. Cosmology and Rules

## Answers Writers Should Know

*This is internal truth. Never state it outright to players before the act listed in the next section.*

**What the stars are.** Stars are not lights. They are **fixed points**, held in place because they have been seen, named, and measured for so long that they have become set.

**What is growing.** A new star is forming in the dark east of the Belt. Writers call it **the Bloom**. Characters should not use that word until Act III. The Bloom is not malicious. It is growing the way a seedling does: blindly, toward room, pushing aside whatever is in the way.

**The ground as measuring stick.** A growing thing needs something fixed to measure itself against. The Bloom measures against the ground, the one thing below that doesn't move. Saltreach is the closest settled mark. As the Bloom grows, it pulls on the ground in small ways: tides come early, fish move, sand turns to glass. The bad fishing year was the first symptom, months before the Belt visibly shifted. Ivo's older readings record that early wobble without understanding it.

**The Plumbs.** The Bloom's measuring lines take shape as tall, thin, faceless figures that come down out of haze and stand very still in fields, on rooftops, and in the marsh. They are the main enemy type. They aren't soldiers. They are rulers, and they lash out at whatever disturbs a measurement. Variants (all working material): Tide-Plumbs (in the harbor), Glass Hounds (from the crater), and Haze-Walkers (Act III onward).

**How Wren connects.** Wren is the Bloom's offspring, its *incrementum*. She is a piece of it that broke loose and fell when the Belt first shifted. She and the Bloom are the same growth in two places. Every time the sky shifts, the Bloom grows and so does Wren: taller, brighter, remembering less about being a child and more about being a star. The Bloom is not trying to destroy her. It is calling her home to finish growing.

**The Rules (Limits):**

> 1\. **Measurement holds things in place.** What is watched, counted, and recorded resists being moved. Sera's chalk wall is actually slowing the drift around Saltreach. *"If I stop counting, it wins"* is literally true, though she doesn't know it.
>
> 2\. **Hidden measurement holds less.** Ivo's private ledger anchors only weakly, because a measurement nobody shares is only half made. When he reveals it in Act III, the two records together hold the sky still long enough to buy the town time.
>
> 3\. **The Bloom cannot take what the ground holds.** Wren can only be called back up if nothing here holds her: no name, no home, no one who has counted her as theirs. Bram naming her was the first anchor.
>
> 4\. **Loss loosens the ground.** Grief makes the ground less of a fixed point, which is why Wren grows faster when allies fall.
>
> 5\. **Growth cannot be undone.** Anything grown can be redirected, but it cannot be shrunk back. This rule shapes the ending options.

## What Players Learn and When

| **Act** | **Players learn**                                                                                                                                                                        |
|---------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| I       | The Belt has moved. The council denies it. Something fell, and it was Wren. Strange tall figures (Plumbs) come down to the crater.                                                       |
| II      | The sky is speeding up. Wren grows with every shift. The Plumbs seem to be measuring something. Sera runs out of wall.                                                                   |

| IV      | Wren is the Bloom's child, and the sky is calling her back. The ground can hold her if it chooses to.                                                                                    |
| V       | Growth cannot be undone, only directed. Wren chooses what she becomes.                                                                                                                   |

# 4. Hero Profiles

All canon details below are preserved exactly. Voice notes, visual notes, extra sample lines, and arc beats are working material.

## Sera Vant, the Star-Measurer

*"If I stop counting, it wins."*

**Role (battle):** Control and setup. She fixes one point in the sky and drags the fight toward it, pulling enemies off their lines to set up her allies.

**Biography.** Sera is the harbor surveyor of Saltreach, and she drew its tide tables. Her mother taught her that the stars are fixed, and that anything moving against them is weather, a ship, or a lie. Sera noticed the Belt move and took it to the harbor council, where Magistrate Ivo Thane publicly called it haze. Every dusk since, she has climbed the dead lighthouse and chalked the sky's drift on the stone wall of the lamp room. The wall is nearly full.

**Signature item:** Her mother's brass sextant, with one notch filed off where the old Belt used to sit.

| **Traits**       | Exact, stubborn, blunt, fiercely loyal once decided                           |
|------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Flaw**         | Treats people like measurements; can't forgive a reading that turns out false |
| **Wants**        | To prove the sky is moving and find what's moving it                          |
| **Fears**        | Being the only one who believes what she sees                                 |
| **Bonds**        | Trusts Bram completely; refuses to forgive Ivo; sees Wren as proof            |
| **Victory line** | *"Mark it. We were right."*                                                   |

**Voice notes.** Short declarative sentences. She uses numbers instead of adjectives, so a thing is "four degrees off," never "very wrong." She doesn't soften anything and doesn't apologize for it. She gets warm only in action, by remembering what you like and bringing it.

**Sample lines:**

- "Don't tell me what it looks like. Tell me where it is."

- (to the player, home base) "You're back. Good. Hold the chalk."

- (death line) "Someone... keep counting."

**Visual / silhouette.** Narrow and upright, in a long surveyor's coat with chalk on the cuffs. Her palette is slate and sea-grey, and the sextant at her hip is the one brass gleam. Her silhouette is a vertical line with one hand raised to sight. Victory pose: she lifts the sextant to her eye toward the sky, then lowers it and nods once.

**Kit.** Her ultimate, *Fixed Point*, sights a star and drags every enemy toward a pinned spot. Her passive builds the longer she stays on the field, because she keeps counting.

**Growth arc: being right isn't the same as being trusted.**

> 1\. *Act I:* She is right and alone, and shuts out anyone who doubts her.
>
> 2\. *Act II:* She treats Wren as evidence instead of a child, and Bram calls her on it.
>
> 3\. *Act III:* The ledger proves her right, and being right feels like nothing.
>
> 4\. *Act IV:* She has to persuade the town instead of just showing them.
>
> 5\. *Act V:* She files a new notch in the sextant and lets someone else make the first mark.

## Bram Hollis, the Lamplighter

*"Somebody's got to keep the light on for when you come down."*

**Role (battle):** Shield. He raises his storm lantern, and its light shields whoever stands inside it. It burns brighter the worse things get.

**Biography.** Four generations of the Hollis family kept the Saltreach light, until the council built a new one on the breakwater and let the old tower go dark. Bram hauls nets now, but he still climbs the old stair out of habit to oil the unused hinges. That's how he found Sera there the first night, and he has brought her supper every night since. He is the warmest man in Saltreach and the worst at bad news, and he jokes about whatever scares him. He believes her, but he wishes she'd look down.

**Signature item:** The last working lamp from the old tower. He has trimmed its wick every night for eleven years.

| **Traits**       | Warm, loud, patient, protective, quick to laugh                                                                 |
|------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Flaw**         | Would rather keep loved ones safe than let them be right                                                        |
| **Wants**        | To get everyone home in one piece                                                                               |
| **Fears**        | Waking at dusk to find Sera has climbed somewhere he can't follow                                               |
| **Bonds**        | Quietly in love with Sera, never said it; gets along with Ivo better than she'd like; first to give Wren a coat |
| **Victory line** | *"Everyone still here? Good. Supper's on me."*                                                                  |

**Voice notes.** Long, rolling sentences full of food, weather, and family. He makes a joke right before anything frightening, and that's the tell. Bad news comes out sideways ("So. Funny thing about the boats."). He calls Wren "Sparrow" when she's in trouble (working material). When he's truly scared he goes quiet.

**Sample lines:**

- "Eat first. The sky'll still be broken after soup."

- (to the player, home base) "There you are. I kept a bowl warm. Don't tell Sera, she thinks I spoil you."

- (death line) "Light's... still on. Go."

**Visual / silhouette.** Broad, in an oilskin and fisherman's knit. His palette is warm amber against sea-dark. The storm lantern is held high and is the brightest thing on screen. His silhouette is a wide triangle with one arm raised. Victory pose: he lowers the lantern, counts heads with a pointing finger, and grins.

**Kit.** His ultimate, *Keeper's Watch*, flares the lantern into a dome over the team. His passive scales with missing team HP.

**Growth arc: learning to let her climb.**

> 1\. *Act I:* He keeps the light, brings supper, and hopes she'll stop.
>
> 2\. *Act II:* He hides a bad tide reading to spare her, a small version of Ivo's lie.
>
> 3\. *Act III:* He's caught between Sera and Ivo and refuses to pick a side.
>
> 4\. *Act IV:* At peak shift Sera climbs the outside of the tower, where he can't follow. He doesn't stop her. He lights the way.
>
> 5\. *Act V:* He keeps the light on and finally says what he never said.

## Ivo Thane, the Unbeliever

*"A frightened town drowns faster than a fooled one."*

**Role (battle):** Tactician. He reads the enemy's next move, marks a weak point, and calls it for the team. He hits hardest when the fight is already going his way.

**Biography.** Ivo was a navigator before he was magistrate. He saw the Belt move two weeks before Sera did, logged it in a private ledger, and told no one. Saltreach was barely holding together after a bad fishing year, and he had watched a panic empty a port before. When Sera stood in council with the same numbers, he called it haze to her face. His ledger is thicker than her wall, and more frightening, because his measurements go further back.

**Signature item:** The hidden ledger. Its last page is always blank and ready.

| **Traits**       | Cold, precise, controlled, dry humor, always counting the cost                                                                             |
|------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Flaw**         | Decides what others can handle, and is usually right, which makes it worse                                                                 |
| **Wants**        | To keep Saltreach standing whatever the sky does                                                                                           |
| **Fears**        | The truth coming out at the worst time, and it being his fault                                                                             |
| **Bonds**        | Respects Sera more than anyone alive and will never tell her; tolerates Bram fondly; thinks Wren is the most dangerous thing in the harbor |
| **Victory line** | *"As calculated. Mostly."*                                                                                                                 |

**Voice notes.** Measured and formal, with full sentences and no contractions under pressure. His humor is so dry it's often missed. He talks in costs and odds ("That buys us a week. Perhaps eight days."). He lowers his voice when he's angry, and he doesn't say "I'm sorry" until Act III.

**Sample lines:**

- "I didn't say it was haze. I said it was haze *publicly*."

- (to the player, home base) "You've returned. Sit. You can be useful or decorative, and I'd prefer useful."

- (death line) "Should have... written that down."

**Visual / silhouette.** Tall and lean, in a magistrate's coat cut like a navigator's jacket, with silver at his temples and gloved hands. The black ledger is under his arm. His palette is ink-black and silver with one red ribbon bookmark. Victory pose: he opens the ledger, writes one line, and closes it without looking up.

**Kit.** His ultimate, *Final Entry*, marks one enemy, and allies deal bonus damage to it. His damage scales with team advantage.

**Growth arc: admitting he lied.**

> 1\. *Act I:* He denies it publicly, then goes out to the crater alone.
>
> 2\. *Act II:* He manages the town's fear brilliantly, and it costs him.
>
> 3\. *Act III:* The ledger comes out at the worst possible time, which is exactly his fear.
>
> 4\. *Act IV:* He uses his authority to tell the truth and gets the town watching the sky together.
>
> 5\. *Act V:* He writes the last entry and hands the ledger to Sera.

## Wren, the Fallen Star

*"I think I used to be up there. I think I was supposed to stay."*

**Role (battle):** Burst. She starts each fight small and grows brighter, ending in a falling-star ultimate that hits the whole enemy line. She grows faster if allies fall.

**Biography.** Three nights after the Belt moved, something fell in the salt marsh north of town. Bram reached the crater first and found a barefoot girl of about ten, curled up in glass that had been sand an hour earlier, with no name and no memory. He named her Wren because she was small and chattered. Every night the sky shifts, she is taller and brighter, and she remembers less about being a child and more about being something else.

**Signature item:** A shard of crater glass, warm and growing.

| **Traits**       | Curious, cheerful, fearless, lonely in a way she can't name                     |
|------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Flaw**         | Trusts too easily; doesn't understand what she can do to people                 |
| **Wants**        | To belong somewhere on the ground                                               |
| **Fears**        | Being called back up                                                            |
| **Bonds**        | Sera is her hero, Bram her family, Ivo the person she's desperate to make smile |
| **Victory line** | *"Did I do good? I did good!"*                                                  |

**Voice notes.** Early on she speaks in fast, run-on questions, using new words slightly wrong. As she grows, her speech slows and gets stranger, as if it's coming from far up. She keeps her cheer. Even her most frightening lines end in a question or a joke. Tag each scene with its Wren stage (see the arc beats).

**Sample lines:**

- (early) "What's a supper? Is it a kind of star?"

- (late) "I can hear the Belt. It's tired. It's been holding still a long time."

- (to the player, home base) "You came back! Everyone always comes back here. That's what makes it a here."

- (death line) "It's okay... I'll just... go up a little."

**Visual / silhouette.** Small and barefoot in Bram's oversized coat, sleeves rolled four times, with the glass shard at her throat. Her hair drifts as if underwater. Her palette is pale gold and white and brightens with progression. Portrait art changes by act: taller, with fewer rolled sleeves. Victory pose: she bounces, then looks up a moment too long before turning back to the team.

**Kit.** Her passive, *Incrementum*, stacks growth every turn, with extra stacks when allies fall. Her ultimate, *Starfall*, hits the whole enemy line. Her model grows during the fight.

**Growth arc: choosing what she becomes.**

> 1\. *Act I (Fledgling):* Child-sized. Nameless, then named.
>
> 2\. *Act II (Kindling):* Teenage height. She hurts someone by accident.
>
> 3\. *Act III (Brightening):* She remembers the sky and hears the Bloom.
>
> 4\. *Act IV (Called):* She has to decide whether she's the sky's child or Saltreach's.
>
> 5\. *Act V (Fixed):* She chooses (see Section 7).

# 5. Relationship Map

*(The Markdown version contains a Mermaid diagram of this map. Summary:)*

| **Pairing**      | **Link**                    |
|------------------|-----------------------------|
| Sera and Bram    | Trust / unspoken love       |
| **Sera and Ivo** | **The fault line: the lie** |
| Sera and Wren    | Hero / proof                |
| Bram and Ivo     | Fond, unlikely friends      |
| Bram and Wren    | Family: the coat            |
| Ivo and Wren     | Danger / "make him smile"   |

The quartet's fault line runs between **Sera and Ivo** and the lie. **Wren** is the decision they eventually have to make together. Bram holds the middle.

## Sera and Bram

- **Dynamic:** Supper every night for as long as the wall has been filling. She trusts him completely. He loves her and has never said so.

- **Tension:** He wants her to look down. She wants him to look up.

- **Pair-bond bark:**

- Bram: "Eyes up there, feet down here, deal?"

- Sera: "Deal. Stay in the light."

## Sera and Ivo (the fault line)

- **Dynamic:** Two people holding the same numbers. She told the truth and was mocked. He lied and was believed. He respects her more than anyone alive. She refuses to forgive him.

- **Tension:** The lie. Before Act III, she resents him for dismissing her. After Act III, she knows he *knew*, which is worse.

- **Pair-bond bark (pre-Act III):**

- Ivo: "Your mark is two degrees off."

- Sera: "Then it's two degrees closer than yours."

- **Pair-bond bark (post-Act III):**

- Sera: "Call it."

- Ivo: "...Thank you. Left."

## Sera and Wren

- **Dynamic:** Wren worships Sera. To Sera, Wren is proof, a walking measurement that she was right.

- **Tension:** Sera's flaw at its sharpest. She keeps measuring Wren's height on the lamp-room wall, and Wren starts to realize she's being recorded rather than loved.

- **Pair-bond bark:**

- Wren: "Did you see? Did you count it?"

- Sera: "I counted. That was well done." *(The second sentence is the growth.)*

## Bram and Ivo

- **Dynamic:** They've known each other for years. Ivo signed the order that closed the old light, and Bram forgave him for it long ago, which Ivo has never understood.

- **Tension:** Ivo might confess to Bram first. When the lie comes out, Bram refuses to pick a side.

- **Pair-bond bark:**

- Bram: "You're doing that face where you've already won."

- Ivo: "It's my only face, Hollis."

## Bram and Wren

- **Dynamic:** He found her, named her, and gave her his coat. She calls him family, the first word she learned for it.

- **Tension:** Each night the coat fits her a little better, and the girl he found slips further away.

- **Pair-bond bark:**

- Wren: "Bram! I'm bigger than the lantern now!"

- Bram: "Then you'd better stay where I can see you, Sparrow."

## Ivo and Wren

- **Dynamic:** He thinks she's the most dangerous thing in the harbor. She is desperate to make him smile.

- **Tension:** He's right that she's dangerous, and he's wrong about what to do about her. He's the one who argues she should be sent up. Her attempts to make him smile are a running comic thread that turns sad later.

- **Pair-bond bark:**

- Wren: "Was that funny? Did you almost smile?"

- Ivo: "I noted it. Under 'almost.'"

# 6. The Quartet Scene

When all four are fielded together, a **quartet scene** unlocks at the home base after each act. Working titles:

> 1\. **"Supper in the Lamp Room"** (Act I). Bram feeds everyone. Ivo arrives uninvited. Sera refuses to speak to him. Wren asks what supper is.
>
> 2\. **"The Last Clean Stone"** (Act II). The wall runs out of space, and they argue about where to write next. Wren offers her arm.
>
> 3\. **"Two Records"** (Act III). Sera and Ivo lay the wall and the ledger side by side. Nobody speaks for a long time. Bram finally does.
>
> 4\. **"Measuring Wren"** (Act IV). They mark her height on the doorframe like a child's. She's too tall for it.
>
> 5\. **"The Blank Page"** (Act V). Ivo's last entry. Varies by ending.

# 7. Campaign Outline

**The campaign clock:** The moving sky is the clock, and it speeds up. Each act covers about half the in-world time of the one before, from weeks in Act I down to a single night in Act V. The title screen, chalk wall, and skybox all show it.

## Opening: First Session (before the gacha)

**Night three. The crater.** After the title screen, a short scene: Sera chalking at dusk, Bram on the stair with supper. Then a streak of light falls into the marsh.

- Bram runs and reaches the crater first. He finds the barefoot girl in the glass.

- Sera arrives with her sextant, reading the fall.

- Ivo arrives last, alone and on foot, which a magistrate has no official reason to be.

- Plumbs step down out of the haze around the crater, tall and silent, measuring.

- **The first fight:** all four heroes, one battle, a guided tutorial. Sera pulls a Plumb off its line. Bram's lantern catches a blow. Ivo calls the weak point. The nameless girl glows, grows, and drops the first *Starfall*.

- **After the fight:** Bram wraps her in his coat. "We'll call you Wren. You chatter." Ivo says nothing. Sera writes a mark on the back of her hand.

- **Then the first summon.** The player's starter roster is these four. The first pull is framed as "someone else in Saltreach who saw the light fall."

**Home base unlock:** After the first mission, the **lamp room** unlocks (see Section 8, Home Base).

## Act I: Haze

*The sky moves; the town says it doesn't.*

- Bram teaches Wren words and stairs, and Sera tests her. Plumbs appear in the marsh, and the council blames "fog-madness."

- **Act close:** The Belt shifts again, visibly this time. Wren wakes up taller. The wall is almost full.

- *Arc focus:* Sera, right and alone.

## Act II: The Wall Fills

*The sky speeds up, and Wren grows with it.*

- The tides fall off the tables, and boats are nearly lost. Bram hides one bad reading from Sera.

- Wren reaches teenage height. She accidentally hurts someone while glowing, and the town starts to notice her.

- **Act close:** Sera runs out of wall. Bram's hidden reading comes out. The two quarrel, and that quarrel is the dress rehearsal for Act III.

- *Arc focus:* Bram, and Wren becoming visible.

## Act III: The Ledger

*The lie comes out.*

- The ledger surfaces. Its measurements go back years and tie the bad fishing year to the sky.

- Sera confronts Ivo. The fault line breaks open. The council fractures, and the town learns at the worst possible time.

- **Act close:** Sera and Ivo put the two records together, and for one night the sky holds still. The town sees it happen.

- *Arc focus:* Ivo admits he lied. Sera learns that being right isn't enough.

## Act IV: Called Up

*Wren is the sky's child, and it wants her back.*

- Wren nearly adult, bright enough to read by. She hears the Bloom and remembers the sky.

- Ivo argues, logically and with real pain, that sending her up might end the drift. Sera and Bram refuse. Wren hears all of it.

- Ivo, now telling the truth, gets the town up on the rooftops counting the sky. At peak shift Sera climbs the outside of the tower to the lamp-room roof, and Bram lets her go.

- **Act close:** The sky calls her, and Wren starts to rise.

- *Arc focus:* Wren's choice approaches. Bram lets go.

## Act V: The New Fixed Point

*One night. Everything moves.*

> **WORKING DIRECTION, NOT LOCKED**
>
> **Wren grows into the new sky.** Growth can't be undone, so Wren can't be made small again, and the Bloom can't be pushed back. But she can choose where she grows. Anchored by her name, by Bram's coat, by Sera's count, and by Ivo's finally written entry, she rises *on her own terms* and becomes the new star the Bloom was trying to grow. She doesn't sit in the east, crowding the Belt. She sits low over the old lighthouse, a new fixed point. The drift stops. The Belt settles in its new place. Sera files a new notch into her mother's sextant. Bram lights the old lamp. Ivo writes on the blank page: *"Fixed. Witnessed by all of Saltreach."*
>
> Wren becomes something new and doesn't go back to being what she was before. She still talks to them, faintly, the way a lighthouse talks to ships.
>
> **Alternative endings considered:**

- **They move the sky back.** The town counts the Bloom back and restores the old Belt, and Wren stays human. This works against the "growth can't be undone" rule and against the title.

- **The sky wins, and they grow anyway.** The Bloom completes, Wren is taken up, and Saltreach learns to navigate under a new sky. It's bittersweet and on theme, but it takes away Wren's choice.

**Post-ending hook:** Skies elsewhere (Ferrow, Bellwater, Mount Quell) start to drift too.

# 8. Gacha-Facing Guidance

**Core rule:** every hero needs a voice, a physical habit (Sera chalking, Bram trimming the wick, Ivo's ledger, Wren looking up), and a named bond with the squad the player actually fields. Their ultimate, death line, and victory pose should all come out of the backstory. The drama inside the four is what players root for. The world plot is the frame around it.

## Quartet Scene

See Section 6. Each quartet scene should move at least one internal quarrel forward, and none should hold plot information that exists nowhere else.

## Pair Bonds

- Two heroes who share wins build bond. At set thresholds they unlock a short conversation (under 300 words) and a battlefield bark that only plays when both are fielded.

- Pair barks should change after major arc beats (for example, Sera and Ivo before and after Act III).

## Same-Person Returns

Favorites come back as **themselves**: the same name, the same voice actor and voice, a changed role, and a return triggered by the story. Never introduce a replacement "alt" with a different personality.

**Suggested Saltreach returns (working material):**

- **Sera Vant, the Witness:** after Act III. Support role. She no longer measures alone; she buffs allies who mark the same target.

- **Bram Hollis, the Climber:** after Act IV. A mobile shield who follows an ally anywhere on the field.

- **Ivo Thane, the Truth-Teller:** after Act III. Reveals enemy intents to the whole team rather than calling them himself.

- **Wren, the Fixed Star:** post-ending. A stationary, field-wide anchor. She doesn't move, she holds.

## The Campaign Fields the Player's Four

- Campaign battles use the player's chosen roster. Saltreach's four are the starter roster.

- Scenes needing an unowned hero are told **by letter or rumor**, such as a note in the lamp room or gossip from the net lofts.

- **Unowned heroes are people the owned ones talk about.** Before a new hero releases, Saltreach heroes should already have mentioned them.

## Bench Anecdotes

- **Every content drop owes one scene to an existing bench hero.** For example, a Ferrow event might include a letter from Sera about how the Belt looks inland.

## Home Base

- After the first mission, the player's favorite (the hero they fielded most, defaulting to Sera) waits in the lamp room and has a **new line each time the player returns**. Line pools are tied to act, time of day, and recent fights.

- The chalk wall shows campaign progress. Bram's lamp shows login streak, burning brighter on longer streaks (working idea).

## Naming Conventions for Future Heroes

- **Format:** *Given Surname, the Epithet* (Sera Vant, the Star-Measurer). Heroes without a family get one name (Wren).

- **Names:** one or two syllables, plain and slightly old-fashioned, easy to say. No apostrophes or invented letter clusters.

- **Epithets:** a plain noun phrase describing what the person *does* or *is* in the story (Lamplighter, Unbeliever). No power words.

- **Regional flavor:** Saltreach names are clipped and coastal. Give each new region its own sound.

## Tone Rules

- Plain speech. People talk like workers, not prophets.

- Understatement is the house style. Fear comes out sideways, as jokes, as numbers, or as silence.

- No villain monologues. The Bloom doesn't speak in words, and the Plumbs don't speak at all.

- Every scene should be about a person first and the sky second.

## Building New Quartets

> 1\. **Start with a place** that experiences the moving sky in its own way (see Beyond Saltreach).
>
> 2\. **Four roles:** cover different battle roles (control, shield, tactician, burst, or new ones) so the quartet is playable as a team.
>
> 3\. **One fault line:** two members on opposite sides of a single moral question. Saltreach's is truth versus protection.
>
> 4\. **One heart:** a member who holds the middle (Bram).
>
> 5\. **One question:** a member who is the thing the others must decide about (Wren).
>
> 6\. **Write the six pairings first**, then the kits, then bench mentions in existing heroes' lines one drop ahead of release.

# 9. Glossary

- **Bloom, the:** Writers' name for the new star growing east of the Belt. Characters first use it in Act III.

- **Chalk wall:** The stone wall in the lamp room where Sera records the sky's drift every dusk. It is nearly full.

- **Crater, the:** A bowl of fused glass in the salt marsh where Wren fell, three nights after the Belt moved.

- **Crater glass:** Glass formed from marsh sand by the fall. Wren's shard is warm and growing.

- **Fixed point:** In-world, a star that does not move. In the cosmology, anything held in place by being measured and witnessed.

- **Harbor council:** Saltreach's seven-seat governing body, chaired by Magistrate Ivo Thane.

- **Haze:** The official explanation for the moving sky.

- **Incrementum:** Latin for growth or increase, and poetically, offspring. It is the game's title and the name of Wren's growth passive.

- **Ledger, the:** Ivo's hidden record of the sky. It is thicker than Sera's wall and goes back further. Its last page is always blank.

- **Ploughman's Belt:** Three stars, fixed for 300 years, now shifted a finger's width east.

- **Plumbs:** The tall, faceless measuring figures that come down from the haze. They are the main enemy type.

- **The bad year:** Last year's failed herring season, now known to be an early symptom of the drift.
