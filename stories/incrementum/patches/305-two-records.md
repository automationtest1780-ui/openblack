## Planning sheet (gacha 14.2)

**Player fantasy:** on the night the sky was due to jump, you held the chalk while two people who hate each other counted it together out loud, and the whole town stayed up and watched it not move.

**Featured heroes: want / obstacle / cost**
- **Sera** wants tonight counted twice, in one room, out loud. Obstacle: the second count is in the hands of the man who lied to her, and she can't say why two counts should matter, only that her column says so. Cost: she walks into the customs house and asks him (she calls it telling), says his name for the first time to hand him the call on the stair, gives the keeper back the chalk, and at dawn finds that being right still weighs nothing. What weighs something is the harbor wall, still lit. She says the first half of the deal to Bram.
- **Ivo** wants Saltreach standing. Obstacle: everything he built to keep it standing (the word, the saying, the ration) is what Sera now needs him to undo. Cost: he tells the town to look up, in red, with his own name under it, lifts his own oil ration, carries the council's book up the stair of the tower whose order he signed, and stands at the glass all night with no word to hide behind. He won't write why it held, because he doesn't know.
- **Bram** wants everyone home and nobody choosing between his two people. Obstacle: one lamp, two books, a stair full of haze, and a woman who needs the light on her page and out of her eye at the same time. Cost: he holds the lantern at the one height that works for two hours without moving, misses his first three o'clock at the slip since the bar, and says out loud what he knows about how she writes.
- **Wren** wants to be useful in the middle now that nobody needs messages carried. Obstacle: the itch is climbing all day, and she can feel something up there lift its foot. Cost: she holds Ivo's watch and calls every minute of the due hour while she itches. She remembers a little more of "up there" than she meant to say.

## Script

### Scene 1 — The lamp room, dusk
[Day 27, dusk. The lamp room. The chalk wall is full from the ceiling to the floor. On the glass beside the second window bar, in Sera's hand, one word in chalk: bloom.]
[Sera sits on the cradle step with her tide book open on one knee and a sheaf of clerk's paper on the other, tied with council tape: the fair copy. Wren stands at the glass. Her sleeves end halfway down her forearms. As the light goes, she gives off a little of her own, like a lamp turned right down.]
[Objective: Take the dusk count.]
SERA: Keeper. Dusk. Sight it with me. Arm straight.
[The Keeper holds one arm out toward the glass. Sera lifts the sextant.]
SERA: Twelve.
KEEPER: Twelve. Same as last night.
SERA: Same as last night.
[She writes it in the tide book. Then she writes it again in the margin of the fair copy, beside the clerk's neat columns.]
WREN: You write everything twice now.
WREN: Is that so the first one isn't lonely?
SERA: It's so one isn't all there is.
WREN: Is two enough?
SERA: Ask me at dawn.
[Wren scratches her forearm through the short sleeve, slowly, the way you scratch something that isn't on the skin.]
WREN: It's itching. Not the little itch. The going-somewhere one.
SERA: Since when?
WREN: Since noon. It's been climbing all afternoon.
WREN: Like when you can hear somebody on the stair, but not which step.
SERA: Noon.
[She writes "noon" in the tide book.]
WREN: Do you write my itches down now?
SERA: I write everything down now. There's no wall left to choose for me.
KEEPER: Is it tonight?
[Sera turns the fair copy so the Keeper can see. The clerk's columns run down the page, one line a night, in Ivo's figures. Sera's own numbers are pencilled beside them.]
SERA: Every shift since the first. Here. Here. Here. Here.
SERA: The gaps shrink. All but one. That one stumbled, then it shrank again.
SERA: The next one's tonight. Before midnight. Eleven, by my sums.
KEEPER: The worst yet?
SERA: The soonest yet. Soonest has been the worst every time.
[She turns a page and lays her tide book beside the copy.]
SERA: And this. Between shifts, it creeps. A hair a night.
SERA: Nights there's only my count, a hair. Nights there's his as well—
[She runs her finger down the two columns.]
SERA: Almost nothing. A hair of a hair.
KEEPER: Why?
SERA: I don't know why. I'm not saying why. I'm saying what's on the page.
WREN: It's on the glass too. Your word. My word, I mean. I said it first.
[She touches the chalk "bloom" on the glass, carefully, without smudging it.]
WREN: It's opening wider tonight. Like the thrift does when you're not looking at it.
WREN: Is it rude to say it's beautiful? When it itches?
SERA: Say what you like. Don't stand in my light.
[On the stair: a heavy tread, and the ninth step's creak. Bram comes up the last turn with his storm lantern and a covered pot. SERA is chalked on the lid.]
BRAM: Evening.
[He says it to the room. He sets the pot on the cradle step and lifts the lid. Steam comes off it.]
BRAM: Hester's pie. Her crust, her fish, my knees.
WREN: Bram. It's itching.
[Bram goes quiet. He looks at Wren's forearm, at her faint light in the dusk.]
BRAM: Then you'll stay where I can see you, Sparrow.
WREN: You can always see me now. I'm the brightest thing on the stair. After you.
BRAM: After the lantern. I'm not bright. Ask anybody in the harbor.
WREN: Is it tonight, Bram? Sera says tonight.
BRAM: If Sera says tonight, it's tonight. She's not been wrong yet. It's very tiring.
SERA: My tables were wrong. They're struck through on every slip.
BRAM: That was the sea's fault.
SERA: My name's on them.
[Bram looks at her. It's nearly the first thing she's said to him straight in days, and it's about the tables.]
BRAM: Eat while it's hot. Hester says. Hester says it every night. I'm only the cart.
[Sera stands. She closes the fair copy and ties the tape.]
SERA: Keeper. Coat. We're going to the customs house.
BRAM: The customs house.
SERA: The book's there.
BRAM: The book. And the man sat next to it.
SERA: I'm not going for the man.
BRAM: You're going to ask Ivo Thane for something. Out loud. In his own room.
SERA: I'm going to tell him.
BRAM: That's how you ask, Sera. That's the only way you've ever asked for anything.
[Sera doesn't answer. She puts the tide book inside her coat.]
WREN: Can I come? I'll be in the middle. I'm good at the middle now.
SERA: Yes.
BRAM: Yes?
SERA: She's been in the middle all week. She can be in the middle down there.
BRAM: And the pie?
SERA: It's hot.
BRAM: It's meant to be hot. That's the trick of it.
[She doesn't eat it. She doesn't tell him to leave it, either. Bram puts the lid back on, picks up the pot and the lantern both, and waits at the stair head.]
[Choice a: "Will he come?"]
[Choice b: "What if it moves anyway?"]
[Choices converge.]
SERA: Then two of us will have seen it.
SERA: I've had one of us since the first night. I know what one's worth.
[Objective: Go to the customs house.]
[Skip summary: At dusk on day 27 the Belt reads twelve, and Sera's figures say the next shift is due tonight, before midnight, the soonest yet. Her column shows the sky barely creeps on nights when Ivo's count sits beside hers. Wren's itch has been climbing since noon. Sera sets off for the customs house to make Ivo count with her, and Bram follows with the lantern and her supper.]

### Scene 2 — The customs house
[The council room of the customs house, just after dusk. A brown tide mark runs round every wall at knee height, where the flood stood two days ago. Seven chairs at the long table. Three are pushed in tight, the way a chair is pushed in by someone who won't be back.]
[The black ledger lies open under the one lamp, a red ribbon across the page. Ivo sits before it with a pen, writing nothing. At the far end, Councillor Gage has the green minute book and a cold cup of tea.]
[Sera comes in with the fair copy under her arm. The Keeper and Wren come in behind her. Bram stops in the doorway with the lantern and the pot.]
[Objective: Bring the magistrate's count.]
GAGE: Surveyor. Keeper. The girl. Hollis, with a pie.
GAGE: That's more people at this table than we've had since the water.
IVO: Surveyor.
SERA: It's due tonight.
IVO: Yes.
SERA: Before midnight.
IVO: A quarter to eleven.
SERA: Eleven.
IVO: Fifteen minutes apart.
[A beat.]
IVO: We have not been fifteen minutes apart on anything since the haze.
SERA: Don't.
IVO: No. Quite right.
[Sera unties the fair copy and lays it open across the ledger's page. Her tide book goes beside it.]
SERA: Your clerk's copy. I've had it since yesterday.
SERA: Between shifts, it creeps. Here. Here. A hair a night.
SERA: Nights there's only my count, a hair. Nights there's yours as well—
[She puts one finger on a line of Ivo's figures and one on her own.]
SERA: Almost nothing.
[Ivo looks at the two columns for a long moment. He doesn't touch them.]
IVO: Some nights. That is not a law, Surveyor. That is a handful of evenings.
SERA: I know what it is. It's a column.
IVO: It may be coincidence.
SERA: Then I'll count a coincidence. Tonight. With you.
IVO: With me.
SERA: Same room. Same minute. Out loud. Up the tower.
IVO: Why aloud?
SERA: So the other one hears it when it's said. Not at breakfast. Not in a drawer.
IVO: We could each count where we stand, and compare at breakfast.
SERA: You counted where you stood for years. I've counted where I stood since it moved.
SERA: I'm done comparing at breakfast.
GAGE: Town's in three lots, Surveyor. Did he say? The fleet's packing for the thirtieth.
GAGE: The pan folk spent two days at the crater with spades. The rest don't know which lot they're in.

IVO: Your column, Surveyor. It has thirty-three more lines in it tonight. In both books.
SERA: It's still a column. It's not a reason.
IVO: It is a longer column. I am a magistrate. I have a weakness for a longer column.
WREN: Did I do good? With the watch? I called every one. Even asleep.
BRAM: You did good, Sparrow. You did the best of any of us.
SERA: Every minute. Exact.
WREN: Exact! That's better than good. Bram, it's better than good.
[Wren holds the watch out to Ivo in both hands. He looks at it, and doesn't take it yet.]
[Sera sits down on the cradle step, with the sextant across her knees, as if her legs have been waiting all night to be asked.]
[Choice a: "You were right."]
[Choice b: "It held."]
[Choices converge.]
SERA: I was right on night three.
SERA: I was right every dusk since. A wall of it. Ceiling to floor.
SERA: I thought being right would weigh something. It never weighed anything. Not once.
KEEPER: And tonight?
[She doesn't answer. She looks down through the glass at the harbor wall. The lamps are still there, all along it, pale, and the people beside them.]
SERA: They stayed up.
SERA: All of them. All night. Looking.
SERA: Keeper. Count the lamps.
KEEPER: All of them?
SERA: All of them.
[The Keeper counts along the wall from the slip. Twenty. Forty. They lose the line by the smokehouse.]
KEEPER: I can't. There's too many.
SERA: Good.
SERA: On night three there was one count on that wall. Mine. Then yours. Then Pell's tag.
SERA: The second time, the whole harbor saw it go. By market day it was beads on a string.
SERA: Tonight they saw it not go. Nobody can make that into beads.
[She doesn't say anything else for a while. Ivo stands at the third bar with the closed ledger under his hand, and lets her not say it.]
IVO: Surveyor. This is not forgiving. I know that.
SERA: It's not.
IVO: I did not take it for that.
SERA: It's counting. That's all it is.
IVO: Yes.
SERA: It weighs more than right. I don't know why. Don't ask me.
IVO: I would not dream of it. I have only just stopped writing things I cannot explain.
[Bram stands. His arm has been in one place since a quarter to eleven, and it shows. He lifts the storm lantern, crosses to the window, and hangs it on its hook, where it hangs every night.]
BRAM: Light's done for the night. Sun's taken over. He's better at it. Don't tell him.
[Down on the harbor wall, a lamp lifts and dips. Twice. Then another. Then the whole wall, lamp after lamp, lifting and dipping twice toward the tower: Bram's signal. Seen you.]
WREN: Bram. They're doing your dips. All of them. Did you teach them?
BRAM: I never taught anybody. They've been watching, that's all.
[Sera's hand goes to the hook by the window, out of habit, to answer. This time the lantern is there.]
[She looks at it. Then she lifts it off the hook and dips it toward the harbor. Once. Twice.]
[Far down on the breakwater, a lamp answers, twice: Jory.]
[Sera hangs the lantern back on its hook. Bram watches her do it, and doesn't say anything at all.]
[Skip summary: At dawn the thirty-third count is twelve, the same as every count since dusk. Ivo writes "Twelve, dusk to dawn. Held. Seen from the harbor wall." in the ledger, and won't write why. Sera says being right never weighed anything; the town staying up all night on the harbor wall does. Bram hangs his lantern back on its hook, the whole harbor wall dips its lamps twice at the tower, and Sera lifts the lantern and answers them.]

### Scene 14 — The harbor wall, dawn
[CG: dawn over Saltreach. The Belt fading into the grey at twelve, over the headland. The harbor wall still lined from end to end with lamps gone pale, people asleep against each other on the coping, children in coats, a pony with its head on a man's shoulder. On the headland, the lamp room, its lantern hanging in the window.]
[Down the wall, people are lowering their lamps after the dips. Mags stands with hers up, counting the window.]
MAGS: Bram. Sera. The keeper. The long one.
MAGS: And him.
HESTER: You're counting the magistrate? You said he wasn't your lot.
MAGS: He's up a tower with my lot all night. Fair's fair.
MAGS: Once. Don't anybody tell him. It'll go to his face.
HESTER: He's only got the one face. It won't fit there.
[Hester sets her basket down by Nell, on the slip, and wipes her hands on her apron, twice.]
HESTER: Still going south?
[Nell has her grandfather's peg-board under her arm. She looks up at where the Belt was, and then east.]
NELL: Where's south, now?
HESTER: That's not an answer, Garrow.
NELL: It's the one I've got. Thirtieth's not here yet. Done.
[She doesn't slap anything.]
[Corra comes down the wall with chalk on her fingers.]
CORRA: Mags. I'm going to the pan-house. I'm putting it on the door.
MAGS: Twelve again?
CORRA: Twelve. And "all night". Under it. There's room for two more.
MAGS: You'll want a bigger door.
CORRA: I'll want a bigger everything. Keep's got the chalk now. I saw from here. Sera gave it back.
MAGS: You can't see that from here.
CORRA: I know what giving back looks like.
[Abel is asleep sitting up against the wall, his head tipped back, his face still turned to the sky. Patch has her nose in his collar.]
MAGS: Abel. Abel Thorn.
ABEL: ...I'm looking.
MAGS: It's morning, Abel.
ABEL: Then I'll look at the morning. Reeds'll want staking.
MAGS: Let them lean the once. Come and eat.
[Tam is curled on the coping with his bandaged hands tucked against his chest. Teodor has put his own coat over him.]
TEODOR: Ah. Mrs Pike. Your sprat has fallen asleep on a wall.
HESTER: He's done it since he was six. He calls it mending.
TEODOR: Then he's mended very thoroughly tonight. I should like to buy that habit.
HESTER: It's not for sale, Mr Lisle. Nothing here is, this morning.
TEODOR: No. I'd noticed. It's very restful.
[Out on the breakwater, Jory turns the lamp down to save the oil, without being asked.]
JORY: Lamp lit at dusk. Answered at dawn. Two dips.
JORY: Old keeper'd have written that down.
[He takes out a stub of pencil and writes it on the back of his hand.]
JORY: Not that it's my book.
[Pell stands up off the wall with the net-tag in one hand. The two children are asleep against his legs.]
PELL: Masthead at dusk. Masthead at dawn. Not a hair off.
PELL: Somebody tell the surveyor. Tell her it's exact.
[On the customs house steps, under LOOK UP, Gage has fallen asleep with the minute book open on his knee. Ada Marr leans over and reads it.]
ADA: "Item. Nothing happened. All night. Whole harbor present."
GAGE: ...I wasn't asleep. I was minuting.
ADA: It's the first minute I'd frame, Councillor.
[At the slip, Tam has woken under Teodor's coat. He looks at Nell, still with her board.]
TAM: You stayed in, Skipper. That's a whole night.
NELL: Don't keep score, Rook. Haul.
TAM: There's nothing to haul.
NELL: Then haul yourself up off that wall. Done.
[The church bell strikes seven. Along the harbor wall, one by one, people pick up their cold lamps and stand, and stretch, and don't go home yet.]
[Credits roll over the harbor wall: the lamps going out one by one in the morning, the boats at the slip, the tower on the headland with a lantern in its window, and the sky, empty and pale, holding still.]

### Scene 15 — The lamp room, morning
[Post-credits. The lamp room in full morning light. Thirty-three strokes in chalk on the glass under "bloom". On the cradle step, side by side: the black ledger, the fair copy, and Sera's tide book.]
[Ivo buttons his waistcoat. He picks up his coat from Wren's shoulders, looks at her, and leaves it there.]
WREN: Ivo. Your watch.
IVO: Keep it until dusk, Wren. You will need it to know when dusk is.
WREN: I always know when dusk is. It itches.
IVO: Then keep it anyway. It is less trouble than an itch.
[The Keeper holds out the chalk to Sera, the way they used to hand it back at the end of every dusk.]
SERA: Keep it.
KEEPER: Until when?
SERA: Dusk. And after. Don't wipe the glass. I want those strokes where I can see them.
BRAM: Ivo. Breakfast. Hester's. You've not been home.
IVO: I have a council to explain this to, Hollis. What is left of one.
BRAM: You've got Gage. Gage'll have minuted it already.
IVO: That is precisely what worries me.
IVO: The council's book can stay where the council last sat. I will fetch it at dusk.
SERA: Dusk.
IVO: Dusk. If the surveyor permits.
IVO: It may come tonight. It was late, Surveyor. Nobody said it was cancelled.
SERA: I know.
IVO: If it comes, we will count it moving.
SERA: Then we'll count it moving. Both books. Out loud.
IVO: And if the town is on the wall again?
SERA: Then you'll have to buy nine more days of oil.
IVO: Eight. I have already done the sum. Hester will not charge me for the tables.
BRAM: She will. She'll put it under "took".
SERA: Bring your octant. The third bar's yours.
IVO: The second is better.
SERA: The second's mine.
[Ivo goes to the stair head. He stops there, and looks down into the turn, where the haze stood last night. It's only a stair now.]
IVO: If there is anything on the stair, Surveyor—
SERA: Call it.
IVO: ...Thank you.
[He goes down. On the ninth step, the stair creaks. He keeps to the left of it, as if he knew.]
[Pair bark unlocked (post-Act III): Sera, "Call it." / Ivo, "...Thank you. Left."]
[Bram stands, stretches the arm that held the lantern all night, and winces. He counts heads with a pointing finger: Sera, the Keeper, Wren. Then he does it again.]
BRAM: Everyone still here? Good. Breakfast's on me.
[Sera opens the back of her tide book and makes one short stroke there. She closes it before anyone can see.]
[Wren has gone back to the glass. She looks east, past the strokes and the word, into the pale sky where the Belt was and where something else is, a moment too long.]
WREN: It's still there. It's only waiting.
WREN: ...So are we. Aren't we?
[Objective: Return at dusk.]
