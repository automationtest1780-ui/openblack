## Script

### Scene 1 — The breakwater, dusk
[The new breakwater light, squat and bright against a red sky. The Keeper opens the logbook. The last page is full of the same line: "Lamp lit at dusk."]
[A young man sits on a bollard at the far end of the breakwater with his collar up. He is looking at the lamp, not the sea.]
[Objective: Light the lamp.]
JORY: Keeper. Light's late.
KEEPER: It's not dusk yet.
JORY: It's dusk on the west slip. Sun's off the water there already.
JORY: Not my business. Light it how you like.
[The Keeper lights the lamp. The wick catches. Jory watches it the whole time.]
JORY: You trim it too short. It'll gutter by two.
KEEPER: Show me?
JORY: No.
[He gets up anyway, crosses, and turns the wick up a quarter turn without touching anything else.]
JORY: There. Now it'll gutter by four. Better than two.
KEEPER: Who taught you?
JORY: The old keeper. He trimmed it every dusk for nine years.
JORY: Then the herring didn't come, and nothing else did either.
[He glances at the lamp again, as if checking it's still lit.]
JORY: You've seen the notice? On the customs house door?
KEEPER: Fog-madness.
JORY: Fog-madness. Big letters. The magistrate's clerk did it in red.
JORY: Half the harbor's seen the tall things in the reeds. Now it's official that they haven't.
JORY: Old Pell read it out loud on the quay. Then he spat on the step.
JORY: Clerk had to scrub it. Funniest thing I've seen since I got off the boat.
KEEPER: Do you believe it? The notice?
JORY: I believe the council likes a word it can write down.
JORY: Took them two weeks to write my family down. "Gone south." Three words.
[He looks north, past the harbor, to the dark line of the marsh.]
JORY: You're going up the old light. To her.
KEEPER: Every dusk.
JORY: Then go. I'll watch it.
[Choice a: "Thanks."]
[Choice b: "Why?"]
[Choices converge.]
JORY: Not for you. Don't make a thing of it.
JORY: Somebody ought to watch it who knows how it gutters. That's all.
JORY: Go on. Your surveyor's lamp is already up. Look.
[High on the headland, a small light climbs window by window inside the dark tower.]
JORY: She never misses. Town hates that about her.
JORY: Town hated it about the old keeper, too.
[Objective: Climb to the lamp room.]
[Skip summary: At dusk on the breakwater, Jory Flint, the last keeper's son, fixes the keeper's wick and offers to watch the lamp. The council's notice on the customs house calls the marsh sightings fog-madness.]

### Scene 2 — The lamp room, dusk
[The lamp room. The chalk wall is nearly covered. Sera kneels at the bottom of it with the sextant in one hand. Her newest marks are half the size of the old ones.]
[Bram sits on the top step with a covered pot. From far down the stair, a small voice is counting.]
SERA: Lamp room. Dusk. You're on time. Hold the chalk.
[The Keeper holds the chalk stub. Sera sights through the glass, over the headland.]
SERA: Thirty-six. Mark it there. Under the last one. Small.
BRAM: You're writing smaller. I thought I'd say, before you're writing in ants.
SERA: The wall's the size it is.
BRAM: Walls usually are. That's the trouble with walls.
BRAM: There's more stone downstairs. A whole tower of it. I've oiled every hinge on it.
SERA: Down there's not where I sight from.
BRAM: No. It's where I live, mostly. Never mind.
WREN: Eighty-nine! Ninety! Ninety-one!
[Wren comes up the last turn of the stair in Bram's coat, sleeves rolled four times, out of breath.]
WREN: Ninety-one! Bram, it's ninety-one tonight! Yesterday it was eighty-eight!
WREN: Did the stair grow? Can stairs do that? Is it because you oil it?
BRAM: It's the same stair, love. You're just counting with more feeling.
WREN: I counted with all my feet! Did you count it, Sera? Did you count me coming up?
SERA: I counted the Belt. Sit by the cradle. Don't stand in the window.
WREN: Because I'm blocking it? Is the Belt shy?
SERA: Because you're in my light.
[Wren sits by the empty lamp cradle, very fast. Then she gets up again.]
WREN: Keeper! You came up too! Did you count your stairs?
KEEPER: I lost count at the ninth.
WREN: The ninth's the grumpy one! Bram says it had a hard life! Did it grumble at you?
KEEPER: It wobbled.
WREN: That's how it says hello.
BRAM: Fish and potato. Hester's fish, my potato, the stair's dust. Bowls, everyone.
SERA: After the count.
BRAM: The count's done. You said thirty-six. I heard you.
SERA: I say it twice. Once when I see it. Once when it's written.
BRAM: Then say it twice with a spoon in.
[Sera doesn't take the bowl. She chalks the number beside the Keeper's mark, small.]
SERA: Thirty-six.
KEEPER: Does anyone read it? Besides you?
SERA: The council read me once. Then they wrote haze.
SERA: Now they've written fog-madness. That's two words for one thing. That's how you know they're lying.
BRAM: Sera.
SERA: I'm not shouting. I'm counting.
[She turns back to the wall. The conversation is over. Bram looks at the Keeper and shrugs, the way a man shrugs about weather.]
BRAM: She's like this every night. You should see her at breakfast. Oh, wait, you can't. She doesn't eat it.
[Wren is at the glass now, nose against it, looking north over the dark marsh.]
WREN: Sera? The crater's sparkly.
SERA: It's glass. Glass catches the last light.
WREN: But it's moving sparkly. Is it meant to move? Is that a thing glass does at suppertime?
[Sera lifts the sextant north. She holds it there.]
SERA: Bram.
BRAM: I'm eating. Whatever it is, it can wait for potato.
SERA: Bram. Come and look.
[Bram puts the bowl down. He goes to the glass. For a moment he says nothing at all.]
SERA: Something's climbing out of the bowl. Over the lip. Low.
SERA: Four. Seven. Eight. Four legs each.
WREN: Are they coming to visit? Do they know we're up here?
SERA: They're on the marsh road. Heading south.
BRAM: So. Funny thing about the marsh road.
BRAM: It goes straight past the pans. And Mags has twenty people on those pans at dusk.
[Bram stands. He takes the storm lantern off its hook and checks the wick by feel.]
BRAM: Right. Wren, coat buttoned. Keeper, you're with me. Sera—
SERA: I'm coming. I'll count from the road.
BRAM: You'll count from behind my lamp, is where you'll count.
SERA: Chalk, Keeper. Bring it.
[Objective: Reach the salt pans.]
[Skip summary: In the lamp room at dusk, Sera chalks the Belt at thirty-six, writing small because the wall is nearly full. Wren spots the crater sparkling. Through her sextant, Sera sees four-legged shapes climbing out of the crater and heading down the marsh road toward the salt pans.]

### Scene 3 — The pan-house lane
[The salt pans at the edge of dark. Square white beds, a low pan-house, a rutted lane out to the marsh road. A woman stands on the pan-house step with a lamp held high, shouting names.]
[Objective: Find the pan folk.]
MAGS: Abel! The Dunn boys! Ferris! Me!
MAGS: Corra! Corra Vey!
[Silence from the pans. An old man with a reed-hook leads a shaggy pony up to the step.]
ABEL: She's not in, Mags. She went back for an evening pick.
MAGS: I told her dawn. Dawn picks. Who picks glass in the dark?
ABEL: Someone who's counted what a handful buys.
BRAM: Mags! How many have you got?
MAGS: Bram Hollis. Thank the tides. Everyone but one. Everyone but Corra.
MAGS: My feet have run the whole lane twice. They want a word with her.
SERA: Where was she picking?
MAGS: North pan. Nearest the crater. Where else.
[Footsteps on the marsh road, coming from the town side. A tall man in a magistrate's coat walks out of the dusk, gloved, alone.]
BRAM: Magistrate! Evening walk again?
IVO: From the customs house window one sees a great deal of marsh, Hollis. I came to see the rest of it.
[Ivo's eyes go to the Keeper and stay there a moment.]
IVO: Keeper. We keep meeting on this road.
IVO: People will talk. Fortunately, the council has ruled that nobody believes them.
[Sera does not look at Ivo. She is looking north with the sextant.]
SERA: Bram. Tell anyone who's interested they're four hundred paces off. Coming down the road.
BRAM: He's standing right there, Sera.
SERA: Then he's interested.
[On the road, something catches the last of the light: a low shape, the size of a big dog, made of greenish glass. It walks with its head down. It has no face.]
[Behind it, more. They make no sound on the road at all.]
WREN: Dogs! Bram, glass dogs! Are they somebody's? Can I pet one?
BRAM: No petting. Behind the lamp, love. All the way behind.
ABEL: Not dogs.
ABEL: Dogs come when you call.
ABEL: Those are hounds. Nose down. Something's been set running.
[The pony stamps and backs into the pan-house wall. Abel puts a hand on its neck.]
ABEL: Easy, Patch. There's my girl. Easy.
ABEL: Marsh gives, marsh keeps. I said bury it. I said it the first morning.
MAGS: You did, Abel. You said it every morning. Not now.
[Out on the north pan, a lantern swings, running. A girl's voice, high and furious.]
CORRA: Mags! Mags, they're behind me! They came out of the bowl!
MAGS: Corra Vey, you run, and you run straight, and you don't drop that apron!
CORRA: I wasn't going to drop it!
[Corra comes over the pan wall at a sprint, apron clinking. Three hounds turn off the road behind her, heads down, following.]
IVO: Three are off the road. They are following the girl, not the road.
IVO: Hollis. Your lamp between them and the step. Everyone else behind it.
BRAM: I know where my lamp goes, Ivo.
SERA: Keeper. Mark where they turned off. Exactly.
[Objective: Get the pan folk behind Bram's lantern.]
[Battle — Stage 1-4-3: The Pan-House Lane. Enemies: Glass Hounds (new). Win condition: no pan worker falls.]
[Bram plants the storm lantern on the step. Its light spreads over Mags, Abel, the pony, two lanky boys and an old man in a nightcap.]
[A hound reaches the edge of the light and stops. It turns its faceless head as if listening. Then it comes on.]
IVO: Their legs are glass. Glass breaks across the grain. Strike low and sideways.
[Sera sights. A hound slides across the salt toward the point she pinned, scattering crust.]
SERA: That one. Off the pan.
[Wren steps out from behind Bram. She is glowing, faintly. Every hound's head turns toward her at once.]
BRAM: Wren! Behind me!
WREN: They're looking at me! Is that rude? Should I look back?
[The first hound breaks across the legs and falls into a heap of warm sand. The others keep coming, silent.]
[Skip summary: At the salt pans, Mags counts her workers and comes up one short: Corra went back to pick glass. Ivo arrives alone on foot. ### Scene 4 — The pan-house, after
[Inside the pan-house: salt sacks, a scale, a long table, one lamp. Everyone is crowded in. Corra sits on a sack with her apron in her lap, shaking. Small glass shards clink in it.]
[Objective: Count everyone in.]
MAGS: Right. Names. Abel.
ABEL: Here.
MAGS: The Dunn boys. Both of you. Don't both say it at once. Ferris.
MAGS: Corra.
CORRA: Here. Fine. I'm here! Fine!
MAGS: Me. That's all of my lot. Feet, you can stop now.
MAGS: Corra Vey, you're docked a half-share for frightening me. Fair's fair.
CORRA: A half-share! That's a week of bread!
MAGS: Then it's a week of bread well spent. I've got a heart. It's in my feet now.
[Corra pulls a shard out of her apron and looks at it in the lamplight. It is warm. She can tell without touching it.]
CORRA: Mags. Did they come for this? Is it my glass?
CORRA: I've been picking since the first morning. Two handfuls a day. Did I take theirs?
ABEL: Marsh gives, marsh keeps. Meaning, empty your apron, child.
CORRA: It's not the marsh's. It fell on my pan.
ABEL: It fell on everybody's pan. You're the one with your apron out.
MAGS: Abel.
ABEL: Reeds leaned again tonight. Before they came out. All of them, toward the road.
MAGS: I know, Abel. I saw. Cut them anyway, in the morning.

WREN: Is that your glass? Is it the same as my glass?

CORRA: We don't know anything.
TEODOR: Ah. Then nothing's changed. How restful.
[He stands, and makes Hester a small bow.]
TEODOR: Mrs Pike. If you'll allow me, tonight's suppers, all of them, I should be glad to—
HESTER: Nobody pays. That's not how the book works.
TEODOR: Of course. Forgive me. Where I'm from, we pay for things.
HESTER: Where you're from, you're not on my slate. Sit down.
[Teodor sits, mildly delighted. At the door, Tam ducks in with the net still over one arm.]
TAM: Hester. Can I sit by the fire? My hands want something to do.
HESTER: You've got a net, you daft sprat.
TAM: I know. I want them to do it warm.
[He sits by the fire and mends without looking at it. He glances once at Wren's sleeves, then back at his knots.]
[Mags sits down heavily on the end of a bench. She pulls the pencil from behind her ear and taps it on her pay-tin.]
MAGS: Bram. Something I want said, and I want it said in front of people.
MAGS: Those things came down the crater road at dusk. Right past my pans.
MAGS: If they come once, they'll come again. Somebody's got to walk that road at dusk, every dusk, and see.
MAGS: My lot can't. Not with rakes. Not Corra, not the Dunn boys. Not Abel, whatever he says.
BRAM: We'll walk it.
MAGS: You've a tower to climb every night already.
BRAM: Then I'll climb it after. I've got good knees. Well. I've got knees.
MAGS: Keeper?
[Choice a: "Every dusk."]
[Choice b: "After I've lit the lamp."]
[Choices converge.]
MAGS: Fair's fair. I'll put you in the pan book. Under "road."
MAGS: Nobody's ever been in the pan book under "road." You'll be the first.
[Unlocked: Crater Patrols. Walk the crater road at dusk.]
[Bram stands with Sera's pot in both arms.]
BRAM: Right. I'm taking this up before it's cold. Keeper, bring the little one when she's eaten.
WREN: I've eaten! I ate a whole bit of pie! Is a bit of pie a supper?
HESTER: It's a start. Here. Take another bit for the stair.
[Skip summary: At the smokehouse, Mags counts everyone in and learns Wren's name, and Hester complains that nobody ate. Teodor tries to pay for everyone's supper and Hester won't let him. Mags asks for someone to walk the crater road every dusk from now on, and Bram and the keeper take it on: Crater Patrols unlocked. Bram carries a pot up to Sera.]

### Scene 13 — The lamp room, late
[The lamp room, late. One lamp on the cradle step. Sera kneels at the bottom of the wall with Pell's tag beside her, writing very small.]
[Wren comes up the last turn with a bit of pie in each hand, yawning. She curls up under the window in Bram's coat almost before she's sat down.]
[Objective: Bring the counts up.]
SERA: Keeper. Hand.
[The Keeper holds out their hand: 36, and 32 under it, smudged. Sera copies both onto the wall.]
SERA: Your count. Mine. Pell's.
SERA: Thirty-six at dusk. Thirty-two at dark. Three counts that agree.
KEEPER: Same as night three.
SERA: Same as night three. Three counts. Same four degrees.
SERA: The only difference is a whole town watched this time.
[She sits back on her heels and looks at the wall. There is very little clean stone left: one row of it, along the very bottom.]
SERA: And not one of them can say where it was.
[Bram comes up the stair with the pot, out of breath. He sets it down beside her and takes off the lid.]
BRAM: Stew. My name's on the lid, so it's mine to give away. Eat.
SERA: In a minute.
BRAM: You said in a minute at dusk. That was six hours of minutes.
[Sera doesn't move. Bram sits down on the step.]
BRAM: They saw it, Sera. Every one of them.
SERA: I know.
BRAM: You were right in front of the whole harbor. Nell said it out loud. Nell. On a barrel.
SERA: She said I was right. Then she asked me where it was.
SERA: They saw it, and they wanted me to tell them what it was. Like the council does. Like a story.
SERA: Seeing isn't counting. By market day it's beads on a string. By winter it's a bit of weather.
BRAM: Then I'll tell it right. I'm good with stories.
SERA: You'll tell it with a joke in.
BRAM: Only at the front. That's where the jokes go.
[A pause. Bram looks at Wren under the window. In her sleep, she is scratching at her arm through the coat sleeve.]
[Bram goes quiet. He watches her for a long moment.]
SERA: What?
BRAM: Nothing. She's itchy. Hester's pie, probably. Too much pepper.
[Sera looks at Wren too. She looks for longer than Bram does. Then she turns back to the wall.]
SERA: You were angry. On the bridge.
BRAM: I was.
SERA: You've never been angry at me.
BRAM: I have. I just eat it, usually. Like stew.
SERA: Why not tonight?
BRAM: Because you stood out of the light with your back to fifteen glass dogs.
BRAM: And I couldn't follow you out there, because then nobody's holding the lamp.
BRAM: That's all. That's the whole of it. Eat your stew.
[Sera looks at the stew. She picks up the spoon. She eats.]
[Bram watches, as if it's a thing he's never seen.]
BRAM: ...Well.
SERA: It's hot.
BRAM: It's meant to be hot. That's the trick of it. Most nights it isn't, by the time you get to it.
SERA: I came into the light. At the bend.
BRAM: I saw.
SERA: I could see the chimney from there.
BRAM: I know. He told you so.
SERA: I worked it out. He only said it first.
[Bram laughs, once, very quietly, so as not to wake Wren.]
BRAM: Eyes up there, feet down here. Deal?
SERA: Deal. Stay in the light.
[Skip summary: Late in the lamp room, Sera copies the keeper's hand and Pell's tag onto the wall: three counts, four degrees, the same as night three. One row of clean stone is left. She tells Bram the town saw it but nobody counted it. Bram watches Wren scratch her arm in her sleep and blames the pepper. He tells Sera why he was angry on the bridge. She eats the stew while it's hot, and they make their deal again.]

### Scene 14 — The lamp room, morning
[Grey morning on the glass. The Keeper wakes on the top step. Sera is asleep sitting up against the wall, chalk in her hand. Bram is gone; his lantern still burns on the cradle step.]
[Under the window, Wren stirs, sits up, and stretches. Both her hands come all the way out of the coat sleeves.]
WREN: Keeper! Keeper, look! My hands are out!
WREN: The coat's let go of them. Did the coat get smaller in the night? Is it that kind of wool?
KEEPER: Stand up.
[Wren stands up. The coat's hem, which dragged on the floor yesterday, hangs above her ankles. Her head is higher on the glass than it was at dusk. Not much. Two fingers.]
WREN: Am I standing taller? Is that a thing you can do by accident? I didn't do it on purpose. Is it bad on purpose?
[Footsteps on the stair. Bram comes up with a loaf under one arm and a paper in his hand.]
BRAM: Bread, fresh, and news, stale. Customs house has a new notice up—
[He sees Wren. He stops on the top step.]
WREN: Bram! Look! My hands came out!
[Bram puts the loaf down on the cradle step. Very carefully, as if it might break.]
BRAM: So they did.
[He kneels in front of her. He takes the left sleeve and rolls it. Once. Twice. Three times. Her hand is out. He stops.]
[He does the right. Three times. He stops.]
WREN: Three! Is three good? Is three better than four?
[Bram doesn't answer. He keeps hold of the cuff a little longer than he needs to.]
[Sera is awake now, watching from the wall. She looks at Wren the way she looks at the Belt.]
SERA: She's taller.
BRAM: Don't.
SERA: Two fingers. I'm only saying what's there.
BRAM: I know what's there. Don't.
[Sera's hand lifts, with the chalk in it, toward the wall. It stops. She puts the chalk down.]
WREN: Bram? Did I do something? Is it bad?
BRAM: No, love.
BRAM: Three's grand. Three's a good number. Ask Sera, she loves a number.
WREN: Sera? Is three a good number?
SERA: It's exact.
WREN: Exact! That's better than a lot! You said!
[She bounces once in the too-short coat. Then she looks at the underside of the stair, a moment too long, before she turns back.]
KEEPER: What's the notice say?
BRAM: Oh. That.
[He unfolds the paper. It's a copy in a clerk's red letters.]
BRAM: "Weather. The council is watching it." Ivo's words. Clerk's ink.
SERA: Weather.
BRAM: Not haze. Not fog-madness. Weather. It's something, Sera.
SERA: It's one word for it. Last week it was two.
[She looks out through the glass, down at the customs house roof in the grey light.]
SERA: He's moved a finger too.
[Bram folds the paper and puts it in his coat. He picks the loaf back up and starts to tear it.]
BRAM: Right. Here's what's happening. Tonight, supper. Up here. A proper one, sat down.
BRAM: Pie. Hester's crust. I'll carry the oven up the stair if I have to.
SERA: Not him.
BRAM: I never said him.
SERA: You were going to.
BRAM: I was going to say pie. Pie's all I said. Here. Bread.
[Wren takes the bread with both hands, free of the sleeves, and looks at them as if they're new.]
[Bram goes down the stair. He doesn't joke on the way. Halfway down, he starts whistling, too loud.]
[Sera goes to the wall. She kneels by the bottom row: one clean course of stone, end to end, and nothing else.]
SERA: One course left. I'll write small.
[She stands and looks down at the harbor. The morning tide is already at the slips, lapping one step higher than the tide table nailed beside them.]
SERA: Tide's in early.
SERA: Eleven minutes early.
KEEPER: Is that a lot?
SERA: It's eleven minutes.
SERA: Hold the chalk.
[The Keeper holds the chalk. Sera writes "11" in the last clean course, very small.]
[Objective: Return at dusk. Supper in the lamp room.]
[Skip summary: In the morning Wren's hands are out of the coat sleeves; she has grown two fingers in the night. Bram rolls the sleeves three times, not four, and goes quiet. Sera starts to mark it and stops. The council's new notice says "Weather. The council is watching it." Bram plans a proper supper in the lamp room tonight, not for Ivo. One course of clean stone is left, and Sera chalks the morning tide in it: eleven minutes early.]
