## Planning sheet (gacha 14.2)

**Player fantasy:** you were on the pans the night the walking ones came, and up in the lamp room when Sera laid two records side by side and saw the sky hold still where both had looked.

**Featured heroes: want / obstacle / cost**
- **Wren** wants to see the crater "with nobody at it", lying down, looking up. Obstacle: she can see in the dark now, and what she sees east of the Belt doesn't match any word she has. Cost: she gives it a word anyway, Bram's flower word, and has to say it out loud to someone. She remembers cold, wide, quiet, and a crowd of lights standing still. At the end she sits by the thrift that has finished opening and says she liked the knot better.
- **Sera** wants tonight's count somewhere exact, and wants not to read Ivo's figures. Obstacle: the only clean paper in the lamp room is the fair copy's margins, and the fair copy is the ledger's sky. Cost: she reads it, the parts she can bear, and the two weeks before her first mark are in it. She finds that his figures go almost flat on every night her wall has a mark, and that the drift walks on the nights only one of them looked. She writes a word on the glass, not a number, and works out the next shift is due tomorrow night, the soonest yet.
- **Corra** (featured NPC) wants to keep a secret and to be let up the stair. Obstacle: she follows Wren to the bowl and is afraid Wren remembers the reeds. Cost: she hears the word first, says "I believe you" and means it, and then takes Wren's hand on the pans, which she swore she'd never do. She counts the stair aloud, 108, and Sera lets her in.
- **Mags** (featured NPC) wants every name home. Obstacle: the walking ones cross the pans in straight lines and walk through anything. Cost: she counts heads under them, short by two, then counts Wren by her name for the first time.
- **Bram** wants Wren in bed and no side picked. He takes supper up, tells the keeper he's "on the stair", and at the end hears his own word come back to him.

## Script

### Scene 1 — The lamp room, night
[Night of day 26, near eleven. The lamp room after supper. The chalk wall is full from ceiling to floor. One storm lantern on the cradle step, and beside it a thick parcel of paper tied with string, labelled in a clerk's hand: "For the Surveyor. Sky figures. Fair copy."]
[SERA sits against the wall with "16" and "12" in chalk on the back of her hand. BRAM is gathering empty bowls into the pot. WREN stands at the dark glass in Bram's coat; the sleeves end well up her forearms. In the dim room she gives off a faint light of her own, like a coal through ash.]
[Objective: Take supper down.]
BRAM: Bowls. Everybody's. Hand them over. Hester counts them back in like sheep.
BRAM: Keeper, that's yours. Sparrow, you've not touched yours. Sera, that one's—
SERA: Take it. It's empty.
BRAM: Empty, she says. Right. Empty. Empty's a place I know.
[He takes her bowl. She doesn't look at him. She doesn't look away, either.]
SERA: Keeper. Twelve at dusk. It's on your hand?
KEEPER: Twelve.
[The Keeper shows the back of their hand. 12, smudged, under the older marks.]
SERA: Same. Two hands. That's two.
BRAM: Two hands and still no stone. Pell's still got money on the floor.
SERA: Pell's chalking his own door now. Hester says. Pell can keep his money.
[Wren hasn't turned from the glass.]
WREN: Bram. I can see the reeds.
BRAM: In the dark, love? From up here?
WREN: One at a time. All the way to the north bed. They're all looking at the town.
WREN: And Abel's stakes. In rows. Like little Plumbs that don't mind.
BRAM: Eat your soup, Sparrow.
WREN: I can see the bowl. The crater. There's spades on the rim. Seven. And a hat.
WREN: Somebody left a hat. Is that sad? It looks sad.
BRAM: That'll be Ferris. He leaves his hat everywhere. He's left it in church.
[Bram laughs. It's short. He watches her back while he does it.]
WREN: Keeper. Go in the dark corner. By the stair. Hold up some fingers behind you.
KEEPER: How many?
WREN: Don't tell me! That's the whole game. Go on.
[The Keeper steps into the black corner by the stair head, turns away, and holds up a hand behind their back.]
WREN: Three. And your thumb's bent. You've got chalk on the middle one.
[The Keeper turns round. Three fingers, thumb bent, chalk on the middle one.]
BRAM: Do another. Do two hands.
WREN: Bram, I can see you doing two hands behind the pot. Seven. That's cheating, it's a pot.
BRAM: ...Well. That's a trick for Hester's. We'll make our fortune.
WREN: Is it a trick? It doesn't feel like one. It's just how the dark is now.
WREN: It's not dark any more. It's only less.
SERA: She's been at that glass since dusk.
BRAM: She's been brighter since dusk, and all.
SERA: I know what she's been.
[Wren turns round. Her face is calm, slower than it used to be, as if it takes a moment longer to arrive.]
WREN: I'm not doing it on purpose. The bright. It's just on. Like a kettle that's been boiled.
WREN: Does it bother you? I can stand in the corner. The corner's very dark.
SERA: Stand where you like.
WREN: Then I'll stand by the soup.
[She sits on the cradle step beside the parcel, not touching it.]
KEEPER: Will you read it?
[The Keeper nods at the tied parcel.]
SERA: I've read the label. That's as far as I've got.
SERA: You carried it up the stair. You know what it weighs.
BRAM: Ivo's sky. On paper. He'd be pleased with himself, if he were the kind to be pleased.
SERA: He's not here.
BRAM: No. He's in the customs house with the water. I'm up here with the bowls.
BRAM: Supper's side. I said. Supper's side's going home now, with the pot.
[Sera says nothing. But she watches him stack the bowls, all the way to the last one.]
WREN: I remembered another bit. At dusk. When it went.
BRAM: Did you, love.
WREN: Not the ticking. A new bit. A corner of one. I'll tell you when it's a whole one.
SERA: Tell me now. A corner's something.
WREN: It's cold again. A different cold. That's the corner. I'll do the rest when I've got it.
[Bram looks at Sera. Sera is looking at Wren. Neither of them says anything.]
WREN: Bram. Can I go out? Just to look.
BRAM: Look at what?
WREN: The bowl. There were so many people at it today. Shouting. Spades.
WREN: I want to see it with nobody. Is that a strange thing to want?
BRAM: It's a midnight thing to want. And it's not midnight yet, and you're not going.
WREN: I'd be back by the time your soup got cold.
BRAM: Mine's already cold, love. Yours is in the pot. That's how I know you didn't eat it.
SERA: Wren. Bed. On the step.
WREN: On the step. Facing the glass?
SERA: Facing whatever you like.
[Wren lies down on the top step, half in the room, and faces the glass.]
[Skip summary: Late on the night after the seventh shift, the Belt stands at twelve. Bram collects the supper bowls; Sera has the count on her hand, and the clerk's fair copy of Ivo's sky figures still tied, read only as far as the label. Bram is on "supper's side", not anyone's. Wren is brighter, can see in the dark from the window, and asks to go and look at the crater "with nobody". Bram says no.]

### Scene 2 — The stair and the quay
[The spiral stair, going down. Bram goes first with the storm lantern. The Keeper follows. Far above, the lamp-room door stands open, and faint gold light lies across the top steps.]
[Objective: Walk Bram down.]
BRAM: Ninth step. Mind it. It's in a mood.
KEEPER: It's always in a mood.
BRAM: Then it's consistent. More than I can say for the council.
[They go down a turn in silence.]
BRAM: Three seats walked out, Keeper. Three. I've seen boats lose crew slower.
BRAM: Half the quay says get in a boat. Half says fill the bowl. Then there's the half that says count.
KEEPER: That's three halves.
BRAM: It's Saltreach. We've always had more halves than wholes.
BRAM: And every one of them asked me which I am. Hester asked me. Nell asked me twice.
BRAM: Ivo asked me without asking. With his face.
KEEPER: What did you tell them?
BRAM: Supper's. Supper comes up the stair. So I'm on the stair.
BRAM: Stair's not a side. Stair's how you get between them.
[Choice a: "Can you stay there?"]
[Choice b: "Sera needs you up there."]
[Choices converge.]
BRAM: I don't know. I know I've not fallen off it yet. That's a start.
BRAM: Nell wanted me on *Patience*. South, the thirtieth. She asked twice. Once with a pie.
KEEPER: You said no.
BRAM: I said not that one. I've never been past the bar for longer than a night. My mam said Hollises get seasick on dry land.
BRAM: And who'd carry soup up a tower from Bellwater? It'd be cold by Tuesday.
BRAM: She finished the bowl tonight. Cold, but she finished it. Did you see?
KEEPER: I saw.
BRAM: Don't say anything about it. I'm not.
KEEPER: Did she say anything to you?
BRAM: "Take it. It's empty." Four words, tonight. I'm counting them like she counts the sky.
BRAM: Earlier she asked had Ivo told me what he said to her. That's more. I've lost count. That's a first for this tower.
BRAM: Hester says that's a thaw. Hester says a lot of things, at the moment, mostly to me.
[At the bottom of the stair he stops at the tower door. He doesn't open it straight away.]
BRAM: So. Funny thing about the dark.
[He waits for the rest of the joke. It doesn't come.]
BRAM: She named me every reed on the north bed tonight, Keeper. From the window. In the dark.
BRAM: I can't see my own boots down here. I've got the lantern, and I can't see them.
KEEPER: She's brighter.
BRAM: She's further. That's the word I keep wanting. Further.
BRAM: Not taller. I'm used to taller. I've let down hems for taller.
BRAM: She stood at that glass and looked a long way off. And she was quiet about it.
BRAM: She used to tell me everything she saw. Before she'd finished seeing it.
BRAM: I measured her on the stair tonight. With my thumb, against the rail. Don't tell Sera.
KEEPER: How much?
BRAM: Coat's past the middle of her arm. Near enough the elbow, the left one.
BRAM: I'm not writing it down. I'm just saying it the once. To you. Then it's said.

### Scene 3 — The breakwater, midnight

### Scene 4 — The crater, night

### Scene 5 — The pan-house, small hours

### Scene 8 — The stair

WREN: Sixty-one's the best landing. You can see the slip from the slit. I sit there when I'm in the middle.
CORRA: In the middle of what?
WREN: Of them. When they're cross. You can hear both from sixty-one.
CORRA: Seventy-three. Are they cross a lot?
WREN: They're cross quietly now. That's worse. Quiet goes further up a stair.
CORRA: Seventy-four. Mum's cross quietly. About the rent. She does it at the stove, so it's warm at least.
CORRA: Seventy-five. Don't say anything. I'm counting.
[She climbs. Seventy-nine. Ninety. A hundred. Faint light above, from the lamp-room door.]
CORRA: Hundred and five. Hundred and six. Hundred and seven.
[One more step. The lamp-room floor.]
CORRA: A hundred and eight.
CORRA: A hundred and eight. Keep. Did you hear? Say you heard.
KEEPER: A hundred and eight.
CORRA: Out loud. With somebody listening. That counts. Sera said that counts.
WREN: I got a hundred and twelve. Going up. I wasn't trying, though.
CORRA: That's because you skip and then you add them back on. I watched you.
WREN: Do I? I think I do it so the stair doesn't feel left out.
[Corra stands in the doorway and doesn't go further. Past Wren's shoulder she can see the wall: every stone of it chalked, ceiling to floor.]
CORRA: That's all hers. All of it. Every stone.
WREN: Every stone. And me, at the bottom, lying down. A long line. That's me on day twenty-one.
CORRA: I've got six numbers on a door. She's got a whole tower.
WREN: Your door's the same numbers, though. Sera says same numbers is what matters. Not how many stones.
CORRA: Sera says that?
WREN: Sera says it with her face. I can read her face now. In the dark, even.

### Scene 9 — The lamp room, small hours
[The lamp room near three in the morning. The storm lantern on the cradle step has burned low. SERA sits awake against the full wall, the tied parcel beside her now, 12 on her hand. She looks up as Wren comes in, then the Keeper, then Corra in the doorway.]
SERA: You went out.
WREN: I went out. I came back. That's the important half, isn't it?
SERA: It's half. Where?
WREN: The bowl. I lay in it. I looked up.
SERA: And the walking ones on the pans? I saw the gold. From here.
WREN: That was only a little. Mags said my name, after. Not long one. Wren.
SERA: Keeper?
KEEPER: Everyone's home. Mags counted.
SERA: Then that's counted. Wren. You went on your own.
WREN: I asked Bram. He said not yet. Then it was yet, and you were asleep.
SERA: I wasn't asleep.
WREN: You were sitting very still with your eyes shut. That's what asleep looks like from the step.
SERA: Next time you wake me.
WREN: Even if you're counting with your eyes shut?
SERA: Especially then.
[She looks at Corra in the doorway.]
SERA: Corra Vey.
CORRA: Hundred and eight. Out loud. Keep heard me.
SERA: Did you.
KEEPER: A hundred and eight.
SERA: Then come in. Don't stand on anything written.
CORRA: Everything's written.
SERA: Then stand lightly.
[Corra comes in. She stands as close to the wall as she dares, and looks at it the way people look at the sea.]
SERA: Wren. What did you look at, in the bowl?
[Wren goes to the glass. She looks east, past the three stars of the Belt.]
WREN: There. East of the Belt. In the dark.
WREN: It's opening. Slow. Like the thrift on the headland. By the door. Bram's pink one.
WREN: It's the bloom. That's what I called it. I told Corra first. She was there.
[Sera doesn't say anything. She looks at Corra.]
CORRA: She said bloom. On her back in the bowl. Pointing.
CORRA: I believe her.
SERA: Why?
CORRA: I don't know why. I don't believe anybody. I believe her.
[Sera gets up. She takes the sextant from her hip and goes to the glass beside Wren.]
SERA: Where? Exactly.
WREN: Where?
SERA: Don't tell me what it looks like. Tell me where it is.
WREN: Left of nothing. Right of more nothing. Under the bit that's darkest.
SERA: That's not a where.
CORRA: There. Past the end star. Two fingers past. Then up, a hair.
[Everyone looks at her. Corra folds her arms.]
CORRA: I saw where. I wasn't just looking at her.
[Sera sights along Corra's two fingers, past the end of the Belt, into the dark.]
[A long pause. The sextant doesn't move.]
SERA: There's nothing there.
WREN: Not yet. It's still mostly knot.
SERA: I can't see a light. I can't take a bearing on nothing.
WREN: I know. It's not a light yet. It's more of a... where a light would go. If it went.
WREN: Is that a thing a sextant can do? It looks like a very clever sextant.
SERA: It does lights. It doesn't do "would go".
[She lowers it. She keeps looking at the dark.]
SERA: You hear it?
WREN: A bit. Not with ears. Like hearing bread rise.
WREN: Corra says Hester's teasing about bread. I think Hester's right anyway.
SERA: And what does it say?
WREN: It doesn't say. It just gets bigger. Things don't have to say, to get bigger. I didn't.
SERA: The corner you had at dusk. Cold. Is it a whole bit yet?
WREN: Nearly. Cold. And wide. And quiet. And lights.
WREN: A crowd of them. All standing very still. Like Mags's lot at two o'clock, when she counts.
SERA: Standing.
WREN: Very. Like they'd been asked to. And nobody counting them, up there.
WREN: That's the bit I keep thinking about. All that standing, and nobody to say they'd done it.
SERA: Were there three in a row?
WREN: There were rows of everything. I don't know which three. Is that a bad answer?
SERA: It's an honest one.
[Sera looks at her then. At Wren, not the sky. A long look.]
SERA: Twelve, at dusk. You know where it was before.
WREN: Forty-one. Before me. Everybody says.
SERA: And you think there's something in the dark it's moving out of the way of.
WREN: I don't think anything. I just saw it.
WREN: Is that allowed? To see a thing and not think it?
SERA: It's the only honest way to start.
[Skip summary: In the lamp room near three, Sera has been waiting up. Corra has counted the stair, so Sera lets her in. Wren tells Sera what she told Corra: the dark east of the Belt is opening like the thrift on the headland, and she calls it the bloom. Corra says she believes her and points out exactly where. Sera sights there and finds nothing she can measure. Wren says it's still mostly knot.]

### Scene 10 — Paper
[The lamp room. Sera stands at the glass, then turns to the wall: full. She looks at the 12 on the back of her hand, half rubbed off now.]
SERA: Keeper. Hand.
[The Keeper holds out a hand. Their 12 is a grey smear.]
SERA: Gone. And mine's going.
SERA: Two hands, two nights running. Hands wash. Hands get used.
CORRA: Write it on the door. I've got the pan-house door. Forty-one to twenty-four.
SERA: I can't sight from your door.
CORRA: You can walk to it.
SERA: Three miles in the dark to write a number.
CORRA: I'd walk it.
SERA: I know you would.
WREN: Write it on my sleeve. The inside. Nobody looks at the inside of a sleeve.
SERA: No.
WREN: I knew you'd say no. I wanted you to say it out loud, though. It's nice when you say it.
CORRA: The floor? There's a bit by the stair with no writing.
SERA: That's where Bram stands.
CORRA: Then he can stand somewhere else.
SERA: He says it's a round room. There's nowhere else.
[Corra looks round the room, at the full wall, the glass, the stair, the floor. She has nothing to price.]
[Sera kneels by the tied parcel. She doesn't untie it. She puts her hand flat on the label.]
WREN: That's paper.
SERA: It's his paper.
WREN: It's paper, though. And it's the only paper. I looked. Twice.
CORRA: What's in it?
SERA: The magistrate's sky. Copied. Every night for years.
CORRA: Copied by who?
SERA: A clerk. Clerk's hand. Not his.
SERA: He didn't carry it up himself. He sent it with the keeper.
CORRA: That's two shillings of paper, that. Good paper. You can tell from here.
CORRA: You could write on the backs. Mum writes on the backs of the rent notes.
SERA: The backs are written. Both sides. He was thorough.
WREN: Are the edges written? Edges are always spare. Like the corner of the corner.
[Sera unties the string. She lifts out the first sheet, unfolds it on the floor and holds it flat with her hand. Columns, in a clerk's neat hand, down the page. There's a margin down the left, a finger wide, clean.]
SERA: The edges are spare.
[She sits back on her heels. She looks at the clean margin a long time.]
SERA: If I write in this, my number's on his page.
CORRA: So?
SERA: So it's one page. His and mine. Side by side, a finger apart.
CORRA: That's not so bad. Mags writes the whole pans on one page. Abel's next to me and we hate it.
SERA: You don't hate Abel.
CORRA: I hate being next to him in the book. It's different. It's the ink.
KEEPER: Ivo said paper.
SERA: I know what he said.
WREN: Four times. Then he said it again on purpose. Then this came up the stair, and that was five.
CORRA: Who's counting?
WREN: He was. He said someone had to.
SERA: He's not here.
WREN: Can we tell him? After?
SERA: Not tonight.
[The Keeper kneels and holds the sheet flat for her, one hand on each corner.]
[Sera takes out her tide book. She opens it past the leaf in the front, to a clean page. She writes, small, square: "Day 26. Dusk. 12."]
[Then she turns to the fair copy, finds the line in the clerk's hand for the same night, and writes in the clean margin beside it, in her own: "12. S.V."]
SERA: There. Book and copy. Same number. That's two.
CORRA: It's on paper.
SERA: It's on paper.
WREN: Ivo's going to be so pleased. He'll do his face.
SERA: He won't know.
WREN: He'll know. He always knows. He'll just not say. Like a cat.
[Skip summary: With nowhere left to write and the counts rubbing off their hands, Sera has only one piece of paper in the lamp room: the clerk's fair copy of Ivo's sky figures. She unties it to use its clean margins. She writes tonight's twelve in her tide book and beside the same night in the fair copy, in her own hand. The count is on paper at last, and Ivo isn't there to see it.]

### Scene 11 — The figures
[The lamp room, after three. Sera unfolds more of the fair copy and lays the sheets along the floor at the foot of the full wall, one after another, all the way round toward the door. The keeper holds the corners down with bowls and the sextant case. Wren has curled up on the cradle step and is asleep, glowing faintly. Corra kneels by the sheets, careful not to touch.]
SERA: Keeper. Read me the top of each. The date. I'll find mine on the wall.
KEEPER: The first one's before your first mark.
SERA: Before. How far before?
KEEPER: Two weeks.
[Sera's hand stops on the sheet. She doesn't look at it.]
SERA: Fourteen nights. I said that to his face. I didn't need it in a column.
SERA: Two weeks of his. Before my first mark.
SERA: I knew it was there. I heard it read in the customs house. I didn't want to see it in a column.
CORRA: You don't have to read that bit.
SERA: I'll read the numbers. I won't read the dates.
CORRA: He was counting too? All along? Before you?
SERA: All along. Before me.
CORRA: Then why'd he stand up and say haze?
SERA: Ask him.
CORRA: Did you?
SERA: In the customs house. With the water still in it.
CORRA: What did he say?
SERA: Sorry.
CORRA: Ivo Thane said sorry? Out loud? To you?
SERA: Once. It didn't help. It's not a number.
[She bends over it. Her finger goes down the clerk's figures.]
SERA: He reads to the minute. Degrees and minutes. Navigator's habit.
SERA: I chalk whole degrees. The minutes stay in the sextant.
CORRA: What's a minute? In the sky?
SERA: A sixtieth of a degree. A hair. You'd not see it with your eye.
CORRA: So it's like a farthing.
SERA: It's exactly like a farthing.
[She checks one line against the wall first: a night from the middle of the wall, his figure against her chalk.]
SERA: Thirty-two. His. Thirty-two, mine, in the chalk. Same night.
SERA: Twenty-eight and twenty-eight. Twenty-four and twenty-four.
KEEPER: He agrees with you.
SERA: Every night. Every night I was right, he was right. In a different room.
SERA: I stood in council with these numbers. And his were in a drawer, saying the same.
CORRA: Doesn't that feel good? Being right?
SERA: It doesn't feel like anything.
[She goes down the first sheet. Then the second. She says the figures under her breath, the way Ivo does. Then she stops.]
SERA: It walks.
KEEPER: Walks?
SERA: Between the shifts. Every night, a little. Night on night. Eight minutes. Eleven. Nine. Ten.
SERA: It creeps. Not a step. A creep. Nobody would see it. He saw it.
[She moves along the wall to her own first marks, and lays the next sheet under them.]
SERA: Here's mine starting. My first mark for the Belt, up there by the ceiling.
SERA: Keeper. Read me his creep from here.
KEEPER: Two. One. Nothing. Two.
SERA: Again.
KEEPER: Nothing. One. One. Nothing.
[Sera sits back on her heels.]
SERA: That's not eight. That's not ten.
CORRA: Is that good?
SERA: It's different. Good's not a number.
[She runs her finger down the sheet. She stops at a line.]
SERA: Day six. He's got nine minutes.
SERA: Day six I have nothing. Cloud on the tower at dusk. I sat up here and saw nothing.
SERA: He had it at ten. Clear, from the customs house. Nine.
[Her finger goes on.]
SERA: Day eleven. Cloud again, up here. My wall's empty for eleven. He has eight.
SERA: Day twelve. I have a mark. He has one.
[She goes back up the sheets. Down again. Faster. Her lips move.]
SERA: Every night there's a mark on my wall, his creep is next to nothing.
SERA: Every night there's no mark, his creep is eight. Nine. Eleven.
SERA: His two weeks, alone. My two clouds. It walks.
SERA: When we've both got it, it hardly moves.
SERA: Shift nights I leave out. Those are steps. That's not what this is.
[The lamp room is quiet. Wind on the glass.]
CORRA: So what does it mean?
SERA: I don't know what it means.
KEEPER: What does it mean, Sera?
SERA: It means write both columns.
[She looks along the sheets on the floor, at the foot of her wall, the two records side by side all the way round.]
SERA: It means I've had half of something for weeks. And so has he.
CORRA: Half's not nothing. Half-share's what Mags gives. People live on it.
SERA: People get by on it.
[Corra looks at the sheets, then at the wall, then at the sleeping Wren.]
CORRA: Sera. Can I say something stupid?
SERA: You'll say it anyway.
CORRA: It's like when Mags counts us. Nobody's ever lost on a night she counts.
SERA: Don't.
CORRA: I'm only saying.
SERA: I know. Don't say it here. Not yet. I've only got figures.
CORRA: Fine. Fine. It's still stupid. Even if it's right.
[Sera doesn't answer that. She takes the tide book out again, and on the clean page under "Day 26. Dusk. 12." she rules a line down the middle. Two columns. At the head of one she writes "S.V." At the head of the other, after a moment, "I.T."]
[Skip summary: Sera lays the fair copy of Ivo's figures along the foot of her full wall and reads them to the minute. Between shifts the Belt creeps a little every night: eight, nine, eleven minutes. But on every night her wall also has a mark, his creep is almost nothing. It walks on the nights only one of them looked: his two weeks alone before her first mark, and two cloudy dusks she missed. Nobody explains it. Sera says it means "write both columns", and rules two in her book, hers and his.]

### Scene 12 — The glass
[The lamp room, three o'clock. Corra has gone to the stair head to watch the sky from the door, out of the way. Wren sleeps. Sera stands at the glass with the chalk in her hand.]
SERA: Keeper. Two fingers past the end star. Then up a hair.
KEEPER: Where Corra said.
SERA: Where Corra said. She's got a good eye. Don't tell her.
[She lifts the chalk to the glass, to the pane beside the second window bar, where the dark east of the Belt shows through.]
KEEPER: You said the glass was no good. You'd be sighting through your own numbers.
SERA: It's not a number.
[She writes, small, in square letters, on the glass over the empty dark: bloom.]
[The chalk squeaks once. Wren stirs on the step and half wakes.]
WREN: Is that my word? On the glass?
SERA: It's on the glass.
WREN: Is it spelled right? I don't know how it's spelled. I've only ever said it.
SERA: It's spelled right.
WREN: Good. I'd hate it to be up there wrong.
[She's asleep again before Sera can answer.]
KEEPER: A word.
SERA: A word.
KEEPER: On the crater rim you wrote "all". You said you hated it.
SERA: I hate "all". "All" is a number that gave up.
SERA: This is a place. There's nothing in it I can read yet. It still wants a name.
SERA: She gave it one. It's a plain word. It's a flower word. Bram's.
SERA: It'll do until I've got a bearing.
KEEPER: And if you never get one?
SERA: Then it's the only word on the glass, and everybody who looks out of this window reads it.
SERA: Bram will. He'll see it from the stair head with the pot in his hands.
KEEPER: What will he say?
SERA: Something about soup.
[She steps back. Through the glass the word sits over the dark, just past the Belt, like a label on a jar nobody has filled.]
[Down on the quay, far below, a lantern comes along slowly and stops at the top of the slip. It goes down the slip steps, one at a time, and stops. Three o'clock.]
CORRA: Somebody's on the slip. With a lamp.
SERA: Bram. Three o'clock.
CORRA: What's he doing down there?
SERA: Looking at the water. Which step it's up to.
CORRA: Does he write it?
SERA: No. He looks.
[Sera opens her tide book. Under the two columns she writes, small: "3. Slip."]
KEEPER: You're writing him down?
SERA: I'm writing the time.
[Choice a: "Answer him."]
[Choice b: (Say nothing.)]
[Choices converge.]
SERA: There's no lantern on the hook. He's got it.
SERA: It's his to dip. Not mine.
[On the slip, the lantern stays a long time. It doesn't dip. Then it goes back up the steps and along the quay and out of sight.]
CORRA: He didn't wave.
SERA: He doesn't wave at three. He only looks. I told you.
CORRA: You know a lot about what he does at three.
SERA: Go home, Corra. Your mother counts at six.
CORRA: Can I come back?
SERA: Learn. Then come.
CORRA: I learned. Hundred and eight.
[A pause. Sera looks at her.]
SERA: Then come.
[Corra goes to the stair. On the top step she stops.]
CORRA: Sera. The word. On the glass. You believed her too.
SERA: I wrote it down. That's different.
CORRA: Is it?
[She goes before Sera can answer. Her voice comes back up the stair, counting down, out loud: "One. Two."]
KEEPER: She counts like you.
SERA: She counts like someone who's been told it doesn't count in her head.
SERA: That was me. On the crater rim. Weeks ago.
KEEPER: She remembered.
SERA: She remembered the stair, too. A hundred and eight. First try.
SERA: Wren's never got it once. Bram's had it since he was four.
KEEPER: What does that make Corra?
SERA: Somebody who counts out loud. If she wants to be.
[On the stair, Corra's count goes round and round below them, getting smaller, and doesn't stop.]

It's a word, not a number, for a place she can't yet measure. Below, Bram's lantern goes down to the slip to look at the water; Sera writes the time in her book but doesn't answer it. She sends Corra home, and tells her she can come back.]

### Scene 13 — The gaps
[The lamp room before first light. The window is just beginning to grey. Sera sits on the floor among the sheets with the tide book open on her knee. The keeper sits on the cradle step. Wren is still asleep beside them.]
SERA: Keeper. The shifts. All of them. His times, and mine.
KEEPER: Before night three. Forty-one to thirty-seven.
SERA: He has the hour. Of course he has the hour.
SERA: Then dusk on thirteen. Then two in the morning, the night of the bar. Then dusk, twenty. Dusk, twenty-two.
KEEPER: About midnight, twenty-four. Dusk, twenty-six.
[Sera writes them in a column. Under them, she writes the gaps.]
SERA: Sixteen days. Then four.
SERA: Then two and three-quarters. Then two.
SERA: Then two and a quarter.
KEEPER: That's longer.
SERA: That one's long. I don't know why. It's written.
KEEPER: Does it matter?
SERA: Everything's written matters. That doesn't mean I know how.
SERA: Then a day and three-quarters. Dusk, yesterday. Twelve.
[She looks at the column a long time. She draws a short line under it.]
SERA: It comes sooner. Every time but one. Each gap's three parts of the one before it. Near enough.
SERA: A day and three-quarters, then a day and a quarter. Less, if it keeps on.
SERA: From dusk yesterday.
[She counts it on her fingers once, the way a child would, and doesn't seem to mind.]
SERA: Tomorrow night. Late. After midnight. Before light.
KEEPER: Tonight, now.
SERA: Tomorrow night. I haven't slept. It's still yesterday up here.
KEEPER: How bad?
SERA: The soonest yet. In the dark. With the town asleep.
SERA: And the one after that'll be sooner. And the one after that.
SERA: It doesn't need walking ones to frighten me, Keeper. It's frightening in a column.
[Wren wakes on the step. She doesn't sit up. She looks at the grey window.]
WREN: Is it going to step? Tonight?
SERA: Late tonight. You'll feel it first. You always do.
WREN: I can feel it a bit already. Not the itch. Further back than the itch.
WREN: Like hearing a door you haven't got to yet.
SERA: How far back?
WREN: I don't know. I'm not a sextant. I'm only a Wren.
SERA: You're the best one I've got for what you know.
WREN: ...Was that a nice thing? It sounded like a nice thing, said like a number.
[Sera looks at her. She doesn't answer. She writes "Due: night 27, late" in the book, and underlines it once.]
WREN: Will you count it? Tonight?
SERA: I count every one.
WREN: Up here? With the keeper?
SERA: Up here.
WREN: And the paper? His paper?
SERA: The paper's up here. It's not going anywhere.
WREN: That's not what I asked. Is it?
[Sera closes the book on her finger, keeping the page.]
SERA: No. It's not what you asked.
WREN: I'll be in the middle. If there's a middle. I'm good at the middle now. Ask the stair.
KEEPER: Will you sleep?
SERA: After the log.
WREN: She always says after. After's her favourite word. After and twelve.
SERA: Keeper. When the breakwater log's open, I want the night's water. Before Hester's open.
KEEPER: I'll fetch it.
SERA: No. I'll come. I read it there now. Every morning.
SERA: And Keeper. Bram. Tell him the hour. Late. After midnight.
KEEPER: Tell him yourself.
SERA: He's on the slip at three and I'm up here. Tell him today.
KEEPER: And Ivo?
[Sera looks at the sheets on the floor, the clerk's hand and hers in the margin.]
SERA: Not yet.
SERA: I've only had his figures one night. I want one more look before I say anything to anybody.
KEEPER: There isn't one more night.
SERA: I know how many nights there are. I just did the sum.
[Skip summary: Before first light, Sera lists every shift and the gaps between them: sixteen days, four, two and three-quarters, two, two and a quarter (a long one she won't explain), then a day and three-quarters. Each gap is about three-quarters of the last. The next shift is due tomorrow night, late, after midnight and before light, the soonest yet. Wren wakes and says she can already feel it, further back than her itch.]

### Scene 14 — The breakwater, morning
[Day 27. The breakwater in grey morning light. The water is high on the stones. JORY sits on the step of the lamp housing with the breakwater log open on his knees. SERA comes along the wall with her tide book and the Keeper.]
[Objective: Read the log.]
JORY: Surveyor. It's open at the night. Your keeper's hand, and mine at the end.
SERA: Read it me.
JORY: You can read.
SERA: I can. You read logs. Read it me.
[Jory looks at her a moment, then down at the book.]
JORY: Dusk. Water at the third mark, rising. Wind west. Lamp lit.
JORY: Midnight. Water at the fifth. Light seen walking north on the marsh road.
SERA: You wrote that?
JORY: It walked. That's what the book's for.
JORY: Three o'clock. Water at the sixth mark. Lamp on the slip. Hollis.
SERA: You put Bram in the book.
JORY: He was on the slip. With a lamp. At three. That's a thing that happened.
SERA: Go on.
JORY: Six. Water falling off the sixth. Fog lifting off the pans.
[Sera copies each line into her tide book as he reads it. Small, square, quick.]
JORY: You're copying it.
SERA: Into my book. Paper.
JORY: The log's paper.
SERA: The log's yours. This is mine. Two books.
JORY: ...That's what the old keeper used to say. Two books, one sea.
SERA: He was right.
JORY: Town said he was a liar. Now they read his log out in the customs house.
SERA: I heard it read.
JORY: So did I. Didn't stay for the end. Not my business.
[He closes the log. He doesn't put it down.]
SERA: Jory. Tonight's water. When?
JORY: Spring tide. The big one, after the flood. And the drift on top of it.
JORY: By the marks, it's coming earlier every tide. High water's on it near midnight.
SERA: Near midnight. Tonight.
JORY: Near enough. Can't swear the minute. Nobody can now.
SERA: Then it's both.

### Scene 15 — The headland, afternoon
[Day 27, late afternoon, before dusk. The headland outside the old tower door. Brown grass flattened by wind. The sea loud below. By the doorstep, in the lee of the wall, one clump of thrift: a round pink head, wide open.]
[WREN sits on the grass beside it with her knees up. The Keeper comes out of the tower door.]
WREN: Keeper. Look. It's all the way open now.
KEEPER: The thrift.
WREN: It was a knot. Two days ago. Then it was opening. Now it's this.
WREN: It went from the middle out. I watched all of it. Is that rude, watching?
KEEPER: I don't think so.
WREN: It felt rude. It didn't seem to mind.
[She touches the round pink head with one finger, very lightly. She doesn't pick it.]
WREN: If I pick it, it'll be in a cup. And then it won't be anywhere.
WREN: I'd rather it was here. Being open. Where it opened.
[She looks up, east. In daylight there's nothing there but white sky.]
WREN: You can't see it in the day. Up there.
WREN: But it's there. Still opening. Like this, only slower. And no smell.
KEEPER: Can you hear it?
WREN: A bit. It's louder than this morning. Not loud. Just more.
WREN: Like a room where somebody's moved all the chairs. And you're the only one who noticed.
[Footsteps on the path up from the harbor. BRAM comes up with a covered pot under one arm and the storm lantern, unlit, in his other hand.]
BRAM: Sparrow. Supper's at five now, remember. I've brought it early. Hester's orders.
BRAM: Keeper. She sent you some bread and a look. I've left the look at the bottom of the stair.
WREN: Bram. Do you remember this one? You showed me. The first week.
[He sets the pot down. He crouches, slowly, knees cracking, and looks at the thrift.]
BRAM: Sea pink. Thrift, my nan called it. Late one, that.
BRAM: Should be brown by now, like the rest. It's got no sense of time.
WREN: You said when it opens, it's a bloom.
BRAM: Did I?
WREN: On the path. I asked what it was doing. You said blooming. Then you said "bloom" on its own, when I asked again.
BRAM: Sounds like me. I'm a font of flowers. Ask anybody.
WREN: I used it. Last night. For the sky.
[Bram goes still.]
WREN: The dark bit past the Belt. It's opening, like this did. I called it the bloom.
WREN: Corra heard. She believed me. And Sera wrote it on the glass.
BRAM: On the glass. Sera did.
WREN: Small. In the corner. Not a number.
[Bram looks up at the lamp-room window, high above them. Then back at the flower.]
BRAM: That's my word.
BRAM: I gave you that one. For a flower. On a path.
WREN: I know. I kept it. I didn't have a better one.
WREN: You gave me sky, and gull, and smoke. That was the first day.
WREN: Then supper. Then thrift. Then bloom. I keep all of them. In order. Like Sera does numbers.
BRAM: You never told me you kept them.
WREN: You never asked. You just kept giving them. That's how giving works, isn't it?
BRAM: I'd have given you a better one, love. If I'd known where you'd put it.
WREN: It's a good one. It's yours. That's why it's good.
[A long pause. The wind lies the grass down flat and lets it up again.]
BRAM: Sera says tonight. The keeper told me. Late. After midnight.
WREN: Before light.
BRAM: Before light.
WREN: Where will you be?
BRAM: Same as always. Supper up. Then the stair.
WREN: Can the stair be in the lamp room? Just tonight?
BRAM: That's not how stairs work, Sparrow. They go up to rooms. They're not in them.
WREN: The top step's in it. Half in. I sleep on it. I know.
BRAM: ...Aye. The top step's half in.
WREN: Bring the lantern. The big one. Your one.
BRAM: I always bring it.
WREN: Bring it all the way in, though. Not on the step. In.
[Bram looks at the unlit lantern in his hand as if it's said something to him.]
BRAM: So. Funny thing about tonight—
[He stops. He waits for the joke. It doesn't come. He doesn't pretend it did.]
WREN: You don't have to do one. I know what you mean without the funny.
[She looks at the thrift again: wide open, every small flower in its round head turned out.]
WREN: Bram.
BRAM: Sparrow.
WREN: I liked the knot better. Is that allowed?
[Bram doesn't answer. He sits down beside her, on the cold grass, and doesn't go anywhere.]
[Objective: Return at dusk.]
[Skip summary: On the afternoon of day 27, Wren sits by the clump of thrift at the tower door, now fully open. She tells Bram she used his flower word, "bloom", for the opening dark in the sky, and that Corra believed her and Sera wrote it on the glass. Bram says he'd have given her a better word if he'd known where she'd put it. The shift is due late tonight. Wren says she liked the knot better, and Bram sits down beside her.]
