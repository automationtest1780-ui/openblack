**Player fantasy:** you saw something fall out of the sky, and four strangers let you stand with them.

**Leads: want / obstacle / cost**
- Sera wants a second count that isn't hers. Obstacle: the town calls it haze. Cost: her first proof turns out to be a child.
- Bram wants everyone home, and supper eaten. Obstacle: the thing that fell can't be carried home. Cost: his coat, and from now on someone small to keep safe.
- Ivo wants nobody to see this tonight. Obstacle: four witnesses. Cost: he has to lie to the council again, and now the keeper knows it.

**Spine link:** the Belt moved three nights ago; tonight something fell out of it.

**Recap blurb (written first)**
> The Ploughman's Belt has slipped east, and the council calls it haze.
> On the third night, something fell into the salt marsh: a barefoot girl, and tall figures that came down after her.
> Bram named her Wren. Sera counted her. The magistrate said nothing.

## Scene cards (gacha §11.2)

**1. Breakwater.** Pell wants someone to believe his pots were set right. By the end, the keeper is carrying his proof up the hill. Only-him lines: "Six pots, set where I've set them forty years." / "Council says haze. Autumn haze." Skipper gets: the stars moved; take the bearing to the tower.

**2. Stair.** Bram wants the new keeper fed and Sera to look down. By the end, the keeper has a friend they didn't expect. Only-him lines: "Nobody loves it, but it's brighter." / "If you get her to look at her feet for one whole minute, I'll owe you a fish." Skipper gets: Bram's family kept this light; Sera is upstairs.

**3. Lamp room.** Sera wants a count that isn't hers. By the end, she has one, and the sky answers. Only-her lines: "That's not haze. That's four degrees." / "Don't stand in the window. You're blocking the Belt." Skipper gets: two readings agree; something fell north.

**4. Marsh.** Sera wants the angle before she sees the thing. By the end, the reeds tell them it's close. Only-her line: "It came down twelve degrees off the Belt." Skipper gets: we're heading to where it fell.

**5. Crater.** Bram wants the thing to be anything but a person. By the end, it's a child asking him questions. Only-them lines: BRAM "So. Funny thing about falling stars." / GIRL "What's hurt? Is it a kind of cold?" Skipper gets: the fallen thing is a girl with no name.

**6. Rim.** Ivo wants her gone before anyone sees. By the end, the obvious rescue has called down the Plumbs. Only-him lines: "I walk in the evenings, Hollis. It is still legal." / "They move when she moves." Skipper gets: lifting her brings the figures.

**7. Fight.** Everyone wants the girl alive. By the end, she saves them. Lines stay under two per beat. Skipper gets: she glows and wins the fight.

**8. After.** Bram wants her to belong to someone, and Ivo wants silence. By the end, she has a name and the keeper has been handed a lie. Only-them lines: BRAM "You'll grow into it." / IVO "You will not tell them otherwise." Skipper gets: she's Wren; Ivo will call it haze.

**9. Lamp room, dawn.** Sera wants to log three counts. By the end, Wren asks a question that makes the numbers feel small. Skipper gets: come back at dusk.

## Script

### Scene 1 — The breakwater, dusk
[The new breakwater light, squat and bright. An old fisherman drags a line of cut floats onto the stones and sits down hard beside them.]
[Objective: Talk to the fisherman.]
PELL: You're the new keeper. Light's on early. Good. Keep it on late, too.
KEEPER: Bad catch?
PELL: No catch. No pots. Six pots, set where I've set them forty years.
PELL: Ropes cut clean through. That's rock, that is. There's no rock where I set.
PELL: I set them by the Belt. Those three, see? In a row, over the headland.
[Three bright stars in a line hang above a dark stone tower on the headland.]
KEEPER: The Belt?
PELL: Ploughman's Belt. Hasn't shifted in three hundred years. Every boat here steers by it.
PELL: Three nights back, it slipped a finger's width east. My pots followed it onto the rocks.
KEEPER: Does the council know?
PELL: Council says haze. Autumn haze.
PELL: Haze doesn't cut rope.
[He nods at the dark tower. A small light climbs inside it, window by window.]
PELL: The surveyor doesn't say haze. She's up the old light every dusk, counting it.
PELL: Take her my bearing, would you? I'm too old for that stair, and she won't come down.
[He presses a wooden net-tag into the Keeper's hand. Numbers are scratched into it.]
PELL: Tell her it's exact. Tell her Pell said so. She'll know what that's worth.
[Objective: Take Pell's bearing to the old lighthouse.]
[Skip summary: The new keeper meets old Pell, who lost his pots when the Ploughman's Belt slipped east; the council calls it haze. Pell sends the keeper up the old lighthouse with his bearing.]

### Scene 2 — The old lighthouse stair
[A spiral of salt-worn stone. A big man in an oilskin sits on the steps with a covered pot beside him, oiling a hinge that holds no door.]
BRAM: Evening! Mind the ninth step. It's had a hard life.
BRAM: You're the new keeper off the breakwater. I'd know that council coat anywhere. They order them by the dozen and none of them fit.
KEEPER: You know the lights?
BRAM: My family kept this one. Four of us, one after another. Grandad, his dad, his dad's dad, then me.
BRAM: Then the council built yours, and the magistrate signed a paper, and up here went dark.
BRAM: Don't look like that. Yours is brighter. Nobody loves it, but it's brighter.
KEEPER: Then why do you climb up?
BRAM: Hinges. Nobody uses them, so somebody has to.
[He taps a key on a string around his neck. He doesn't say what it opens.]
BRAM: Truth is, there's a woman up top who forgets to eat.
KEEPER: Who is she?
BRAM: Sera Vant. Harbor surveyor. Every tide table nailed up on every slip, she drew.
BRAM: She climbs up at dusk to count the sky, and I climb up after with supper. Fish stew, tonight. Somebody has to eat it.
KEEPER: I've got a bearing for her. From Pell.
BRAM: Pell's pots? Ah. Poor old sod. Forty years and never lost one.
BRAM: Up you go. If you get her to look at her feet for one whole minute, I'll owe you a fish.
[Objective: Climb to the lamp room.]
[Skip summary: Bram Hollis, whose family kept the old light, welcomes the keeper on the stair with stew and sends them up to Sera.]

### Scene 3 — The lamp room
[A round room of salt-stained glass around an empty lamp cradle. One stone wall is nearly covered in chalk marks. A narrow woman in a long coat sights the sky through a brass sextant, then adds one mark.]
SERA: Don't stand in the window. You're blocking the Belt.
KEEPER: Pell sent his bearing. He says it's exact.
[Sera takes the net-tag and reads it. She does not look at the Keeper.]
SERA: It is. Pell's never been off by more than half a degree.
SERA: He set at forty-one. The Belt sat at forty-one for three hundred years.
SERA: Tonight it sits at thirty-seven. Four degrees east. His pots went where the Belt told them.
SERA: That's not haze. That's four degrees.
KEEPER: Why doesn't anyone believe you?
SERA: I stood in council with these numbers. The magistrate called it haze, and they wrote that down instead.
SERA: Every dusk since, I've come up here and counted. That wall is every dusk.
SERA: Tonight I have a reading that isn't mine. Hold the chalk.
[The Keeper holds the chalk steady. Sera marks Pell's bearing beside her own. The two marks touch.]
SERA: Two counts. Same place.
SERA: I'm going to find what's pushing it. Then they can write that down.
[Bram comes up the last steps with the pot and two bowls.]
BRAM: Stew. You've been up here an hour. Don't argue, I counted.
BRAM: Eyes up there, feet down here, deal?
SERA: Deal. Stay in the light.
[She takes the bowl and does not eat. A streak of white light crosses the glass and drops behind the headland. The panes ring.]
BRAM: Was that the soup? Tell me that was the soup.
SERA: North. The marsh.
BRAM: Right. I'm faster. You two keep up.
[He is already on the stair, lantern swinging.]
[Objective: Follow the light into the salt marsh.]
[Skip summary: Sera's count and Pell's bearing agree: the Belt is four degrees east. Bram brings supper. Then a light falls into the north marsh.]

### Scene 4 — The salt marsh
[Reed beds and black channels. Bram's lantern is far ahead, bobbing. Sera stops every twenty steps to sight the sky.]
SERA: We're in the north marsh. Bram's ahead. Keep his lantern in front of you.
KEEPER: You're stopping to measure? Now?
SERA: It came down twelve degrees off the Belt. I want that number before I see what it is.
SERA: Afterward, everyone will say what it looked like. Nobody will know where it was.
[The reeds around them all lean the same way, toward a faint glow.]
KEEPER: The reeds.
SERA: All leaning. All one way. There's no wind.
SERA: Stay out of the channels. Bram can carry one of us. Not two.
[Skip summary: Sera and the keeper follow Bram into the marsh. Every reed leans toward the glow.]

### Scene 5 — The crater
[A bowl of glass where there was sand an hour ago. It is still warm. Bram kneels at the bottom with his lantern. A barefoot girl of about ten is curled in the glass.]
[Objective: Reach the fall.]
BRAM: So. Funny thing about falling stars.
BRAM: This one's got feet.
[The girl opens her eyes. She looks up at the sky first, a moment too long. Then at Bram.]
GIRL: Is that a light? Is it yours? Why do you keep it in a box?
BRAM: It's a lantern, love. Keeps the wind off. Are you hurt?
GIRL: What's hurt? Is it a kind of cold? I have a kind of cold.
BRAM: Hang on. We'll fix the cold. Can you sit up for me?
GIRL: I'm sitting! Is this sitting? The ground is very hard and very down.
[Sera reaches the rim and lifts the sextant at the girl.]
SERA: Don't move it.
BRAM: Her.
SERA: Don't move her. She came down twelve degrees from the only thing I've been counting.
GIRL: Out of where? Is it far? Was I in it?
KEEPER: What's your name?
GIRL: I don't know! Is that bad? Is "what" a kind of name?
BRAM: It's not bad. It's just not finished yet.
[Skip summary: Bram finds a barefoot girl with no name in a crater of glass. Sera wants her left where she fell.]

### Scene 6 — The crater rim
[Footsteps on the glass. A tall man in a magistrate's coat walks out of the reeds alone, on foot, gloved, with no lantern.]
BRAM: Magistrate! Long walk for a bit of haze.
IVO: I walk in the evenings, Hollis. It is still legal.
[Sera does not look at him. She chalks a mark on the glass instead.]
SERA: Bram. Tell the magistrate the angle was twelve.
BRAM: He's standing right there, Sera.
SERA: Then he heard it.
IVO: I did. Thank you, Surveyor.
[Ivo looks at the girl for a long time.]
IVO: That child cannot stay in a marsh.
IVO: Carry her to the customs house. Quietly. Nobody needs to see this tonight.
[Bram lifts the girl. The haze thickens around the crater. Tall, thin, faceless figures step down out of it and stand at the rim, very still.]
KEEPER: What are those?
[Bram says nothing. He sets the girl back down on the glass. The figures stop leaning in.]
SERA: Dead straight. Like plumb lines.
SERA: Plumbs, then. Bram, lift her again. Slowly.
[Bram lifts her an inch. Every figure tilts toward her.]
IVO: They move when she moves.
SERA: Then we don't carry her past them. We move them.
[Objective: Protect the girl.]
[Skip summary: Ivo, the magistrate, arrives alone on foot. When Bram lifts the girl, tall faceless figures (Plumbs) come down out of the haze and lean toward her.]

### Scene 7 — The crater, the fight
[Tutorial battle. The four stand around the girl in the glass.]
SERA: That one. Off its line.
[Sera sights the sextant. A Plumb drags across the glass toward the point she pinned.]
BRAM: Behind the lamp! Everyone behind the lamp!
[Bram raises the storm lantern. A blow lands on its light and stops there.]
IVO: The knee joint. Strike there. Now.
[The girl stands up in the glass. She is glowing. With every blow, she is a little taller.]
GIRL: Is this a game? I think I know this game!
[Light falls out of the sky in a line, across every Plumb at once.]
[The Plumbs step back up into the haze the way they came. The marsh goes quiet.]
GIRL: Did I do good? I did good!
[Skip summary: The four fight the Plumbs. The girl glows, grows and drops light on all of them. The Plumbs withdraw.]

### Scene 8 — The crater, after
[The glass cools. The girl shivers. Bram takes off his coat and wraps her in it. The sleeves hang past her hands, and he rolls them back four times.]
BRAM: There. Four rolls. You'll grow into it.
GIRL: Will I? Is that a promise? What's your name? Do I get one?
BRAM: Bram. And yes. Everybody gets one.
BRAM: We'll call you Wren. You chatter.
WREN: Wren! Is it a kind of bird? Is it a good one?
BRAM: Small, brown and loud. Best bird there is.
[Ivo says nothing. He looks at Wren for a long moment, then up at the sky.]
[Sera writes a chalk mark on the back of her own hand.]
KEEPER: What's that for?
SERA: The wall's two miles off. This one's hers.
[Wren toddles up to Ivo in the huge coat and peers at him.]
WREN: Are you cross? Is that your face? Can it do other faces?
IVO: It can. It rarely sees the need.
[Ivo turns to the Keeper.]
IVO: Keeper. Your light went unwatched tonight. The council will ask why.
IVO: I will tell them there was haze. You will not tell them otherwise.
[He walks back into the reeds alone.]
WREN: Did he almost smile? I think he almost smiled.
BRAM: Don't mind him. That's his kind face.
[Skip summary: Bram gives the girl his coat and names her Wren. Sera marks her hand. Ivo tells the keeper he'll call their absence haze, and leaves.]

### Scene 9 — The lamp room, before dawn
[Grey light on the glass. Wren is asleep under the window in Bram's coat. Bram is asleep sitting on the top step, the cold stew beside him. Sera is at the wall.]
[Objective: Rest. Return at dusk.]
SERA: We're back in the lamp room. She slept. I didn't.
SERA: Pell's bearing. My bearing. And you saw it fall from the breakwater. That's three.
SERA: Three counts in one night. I've had one count for three nights.
[Bram stirs, sees the stew, and sighs.]
BRAM: You didn't eat it.
SERA: I held it. That's nearly eating.
BRAM: It's nowhere near. I'll warm it.
SERA: Come back at dusk, Keeper. Hold the chalk.
[Wren half-wakes and looks up through the glass, a moment too long.]
WREN: Sera? The three in a row. Is that where they live?
[Sera lifts the sextant to the Belt. She doesn't lower it.]
SERA: It's where they lived.
[Skip summary: Dawn in the lamp room: three counts on the wall. Wren asks if the Belt's stars live there. Sera: "It's where they lived."]

### Rejection pass (gacha §11.3)
- Can a skipper state today's goal? **Yes.** Every scene opens on its place and goal, and every objective matches the spoken goal (§3 beat 2).
- Does each lead want something that isn't the protagonist? **Yes.** Sera wants proof, Bram wants everyone fed and home, Ivo wants it unseen, and the girl wants a name.
- Is there a cause between each scene? **Yes.** Pell → bearing → Sera → the fall → the marsh → the crater → Ivo's rescue calls the Plumbs → the fight → the naming → dawn.
- More than one new term? **Three in the chapter, within §6's budget:** the Belt (glossed by Pell), haze (glossed by Pell), and Plumbs (coined and shown by Sera). One term per scene at most.
- Did someone change a habit, not just receive information? **Yes.** Sera starts marking on her own hand, away from the wall, and she lets someone else hold the chalk. Bram now has someone to keep safe.
- Read-aloud test: Sera speaks in numbers, Bram in food and family, Ivo in formal full sentences, and the girl in questions. No two could trade lines.
- Is the button a feeling or a fact, not a trailer? **Feeling.** Wren's innocent question makes Sera's proof feel like a loss.
