**Featured heroes: want / obstacle / cost**

- **Bram** wants everyone home, and supper eaten. Obstacle: the thing that fell can't be carried home. Cost: his coat, and from now on someone small to keep safe.

## Script

### Scene 1 — The breakwater, dusk
[The new breakwater light, squat and bright. The Keeper writes the first line in a new logbook: "Lamp lit at dusk. Wind nil."]
[An old fisherman drags a line of cut floats onto the stones and sits down hard beside them.]
[Objective: Talk to the fisherman.]
PELL: You're the new keeper. First night on the breakwater, is it?
KEEPER: First night.
PELL: Then here's the whole job. Light it at dusk. Write the wind. Write every boat that comes in.
PELL: Nobody reads the book. Write it anyway. That's Saltreach for you.
PELL: Where'd the council find you, then?
KEEPER: Up the coast.
PELL: Well. Nobody here wanted it. You'll do, if you stay the winter.
PELL: Light's on early. Good. Keep it on late, too.
KEEPER: Bad catch?
PELL: No catch. No pots. Six pots, set where I've set them forty years.
PELL: Ropes cut clean through. That's rock, that is. There's no rock where I set.
[Choice a: "Maybe the tide moved them."]
[Choice b: "Maybe you set them wrong."]
[Choices converge.]
PELL: Tide doesn't walk a pot that far. And I don't set wrong. Ask anybody on the slips.
PELL: I set them by the Belt. Those three, see? In a row, over the headland.
[Three bright stars in a line hang above a dark stone tower on the headland.]
KEEPER: The Belt?
PELL: Ploughman's Belt. Hasn't shifted in three hundred years. Every boat here steers by it.
PELL: Three nights back, it slipped a finger's width east. My pots followed it onto the rocks.
KEEPER: Does the council know?
PELL: Council says haze. Autumn haze.
PELL: Haze doesn't cut rope.
KEEPER: What do you think it is?
PELL: Me? I think it's the sky's business. My business is pots.
PELL: But I'd like my forty years on the record, all the same.
[He looks out to sea, where one boat's lantern is still a long way off.]
PELL: Nell Garrow's still out on the Patience. Last big boat we've got.
PELL: If the Belt's wrong, she'll be steering home on your lamp tonight. Funny old job, yours.
[He nods at the dark tower. A small light climbs inside it, window by window.]
PELL: The surveyor doesn't say haze. She's up the old light every dusk, counting it.
PELL: Take her my bearing, would you? I'm too old for that stair, and she won't come down.
KEEPER: I can't leave the lamp.
PELL: It's a council lamp. New wick. It'll burn till dawn whether you watch it or not.
PELL: Longer than I'd last on that stair, anyhow.
[He presses a wooden net-tag into the Keeper's hand. Numbers are scratched into it.]
PELL: Tell her it's exact. Tell her Pell said so. She'll know what that's worth.
[Objective: Take Pell's bearing to the old lighthouse.]
[Skip summary: On the keeper's first night at the breakwater light, old Pell says he lost his pots when the Ploughman's Belt slipped east, though the council calls it haze. He sends the keeper up the old lighthouse with his bearing.]

### Scene 2 — The harbor road
[The harbor at the end of the day. Doors are shutting and lamps are going out early. On an upturned boat, two children chant the tide table at each other, the way other children skip rope.]
[Smoke drifts from a long, low smokehouse. Above it, a young man sits on a net-loft roof with a net across his knees.]
[Objective: Cross the harbor to the headland.]
HESTER: You. Council coat. You're the keeper, and you're walking away from your light.
KEEPER: Pell sent me up the old lighthouse.
HESTER: Did he. Then you'll want feeding first. Everybody wants feeding. It goes in the book.
[She is a broad woman in a soot-black apron. She hands over a twist of smoked herring and makes a mark in a small greasy book.]
KEEPER: I can pay.
HESTER: Nobody pays. That's not how the book works. The book is for remembering.
HESTER: Supper's early in Saltreach. Oil costs money, and dark's free. You'll learn.
[The children on the boat get to the end of the table and start again from the top.]
KEEPER: They know the tides by heart?
HESTER: Before their letters. Sera Vant's tables. Nailed up on every slip.
HESTER: Pell's pots, though. You heard? "A bit of weather," they're calling it.
HESTER: That's what this town calls a flood, a fire and a funeral. A bit of weather.
TAM: Hester! Is that the new keeper? Ask if the light's meant to be that bright!
HESTER: That's Tam Rook. Mends nets. Never been past the breakwater, never wants to.
TAM: Why would I? Everything I need comes back through it, torn.
KEEPER: Isn't it too dark to mend?
TAM: Moon's up in an hour. Holes don't wait for the moon.
TAM: Three nets this week, all caught on a rock that isn't there. I'll have them right by Sunday.
TAM: Hester says the town's coming apart. I say it's just a bigger net.
HESTER: And I say get off that roof before you mend yourself to it.
[Tam laughs and doesn't move. Hester glances at the tower on the headland, then at the sky above it.]
HESTER: Tell Bram Hollis he still owes me a pot. And tell Sera Vant to eat something.
HESTER: She won't. But I'll have said it, and that goes in the book too.
KEEPER: Bram Hollis?
HESTER: Carries a pot up that stair every night. Don't ask him why. Some things don't go in any book.
[Objective: Climb the old lighthouse.]
[Skip summary: Crossing the harbor, the keeper meets Hester Pike at the smokehouse, who feeds them on credit and writes it in her book, and Tam Rook mending nets on a net-loft roof.]

### Scene 3 — The old lighthouse stair
[A spiral of salt-worn stone. A big man in an oilskin sits on the steps with a covered pot beside him, oiling a hinge that holds no door.]
BRAM: Evening! Mind the ninth step. It's had a hard life.
BRAM: You're the new keeper off the breakwater. I'd know that council coat anywhere. They order them by the dozen and none of them fit.
KEEPER: You know the lights?
BRAM: My family kept this one. Four of us, one after another. Grandad, his dad, his dad's dad, then me.
BRAM: Then the council built yours, and the magistrate signed a paper, and up here went dark.
BRAM: Don't look like that. Yours is brighter. Nobody loves it, but it's brighter.
KEEPER: Do you hate him? The magistrate?
BRAM: Ivo? No. He did the sums, and the sums were right. Doesn't make them nice sums.
BRAM: I forgave him years back. He's never known what to do about that.
KEEPER: Then why do you climb up?
BRAM: Hinges. Nobody uses them, so somebody has to.
[He taps a key on a string around his neck. He doesn't say what it opens.]
KEEPER: Hester says you owe her a pot.
BRAM: This is her pot. It's got stew in it. She can hush.
BRAM: Last keeper on your breakwater went south in the bad year. Last year, when the herring didn't come.
BRAM: Wages didn't come either, after that. So. You've got his boots. Mind they don't pinch.
BRAM: Truth is, there's a woman up top who forgets to eat.
KEEPER: Sera Vant?
BRAM: Harbor surveyor. Every tide table nailed up on every slip, she drew.
BRAM: First night, I came up to oil the hinges, and there's a woman in my lamp room. Chalking my wall.
BRAM: I thought, well. Somebody ought to feed her. Been bringing supper every night since.
BRAM: Fish stew, tonight. Somebody has to eat it.
KEEPER: I've got a bearing for her. From Pell.
BRAM: Pell's pots? Ah. Poor old sod. Forty years and never lost one.
BRAM: Up you go. If you get her to look at her feet for one whole minute, I'll owe you a fish.
[Objective: Climb to the lamp room.]
[Skip summary: On the stair, Bram Hollis, whose family kept the old light until the council closed it, welcomes the keeper with stew and sends them up to Sera.]

### Scene 4 — The lamp room
[A round room of salt-stained glass around an empty lamp cradle. One stone wall is nearly covered in chalk marks. A narrow woman in a long coat sights the sky through a brass sextant, then adds one mark.]
SERA: Don't stand in the window. You're blocking the Belt.
KEEPER: Pell sent his bearing. He says it's exact.
[Sera takes the net-tag and reads it. She does not look at the Keeper.]
SERA: It is. Pell's never been off by more than half a degree.
SERA: He set at forty-one. The Belt sat at forty-one for three hundred years.
[She tilts the sextant so the Keeper can see its arc. One notch has been filed off.]
SERA: My mother filed that notch. Forty-one. Where the Belt sat when she taught me.
SERA: Tonight it sits at thirty-seven. Four degrees east. His pots went where the Belt told them.
SERA: That's not haze. That's four degrees.
KEEPER: Why doesn't anyone believe you?
SERA: I stood in council with these numbers. The magistrate called it haze, and they wrote that down instead.
SERA: Every dusk since, I've come up here and counted. That wall is every dusk.
SERA: You're the breakwater keeper. What did you see from it at dusk?
KEEPER: Three stars in a row. Over this tower.
SERA: A finger's width east of where they sat all summer. Write that in your book.
[She taps the oldest mark, low by the door, then tonight's, high by the window.]
SERA: First dusk. Tonight. Same star. Count the stones between, then tell me it's weather.
KEEPER: Hester sent herring. She says eat.
SERA: Hester says that to the whole harbor. Put it on the cradle.
SERA: Bram's on the stair, I suppose. With a pot.
KEEPER: Fish stew.
SERA: He's been on that stair every night since the first. I've never once asked him to.
SERA: Tonight I have a reading that isn't mine. Hold the chalk.
[The Keeper holds the chalk steady. Sera marks Pell's bearing beside her own. The two marks touch.]
SERA: Two counts. Same place.
SERA: I'm going to find what's pushing it. Then they can write that down.
[Bram comes up the last steps with the pot and two bowls.]
BRAM: Stew. You've been up here an hour. Don't argue, I counted.
BRAM: And Hester's herring, I see. That'll be on my tab as well. Everything is.
BRAM: She'll tell you she ate at noon. She didn't. Hester saw.
SERA: Hester sees everything. It isn't evidence.
BRAM: It is in Saltreach.
BRAM: Well, Keeper? Did she look at her feet?
[Choice a: "Not once."]
[Choice b: "Nearly."]
[Choices converge.]
BRAM: Then I keep my fish. Eyes up there, feet down here, deal?
SERA: Deal. Stay in the light.
[She takes the bowl and does not eat. A streak of white light crosses the glass and drops behind the headland. The panes ring.]
BRAM: Was that the soup? Tell me that was the soup.
[Sera already has the sextant up, following the streak down.]
SERA: North. The marsh.
BRAM: Right. I'm faster. You two keep up.
[He is already on the stair, lantern swinging.]
[Objective: Follow the light into the salt marsh.]
[Skip summary: In the lamp room, Sera's count and Pell's bearing agree: the Belt is four degrees east. Bram brings supper. Then a light falls into the north marsh, and Bram runs for it.]

### Scene 5 — The harbor, running
[Night. The whole harbor is standing in its doorways. Bram's lantern is already far ahead on the north road. Sera does not slow down.]
SERA: North road, through the harbor, out to the pans. Don't stop for anyone.
SERA: Afterward, everyone will say what it looked like. Nobody will know where it was.
[Hester stands in the smokehouse door. There is white ash all down her apron.]
HESTER: Came straight through my vents! Whole house went white as milk.
HESTER: And Bram Hollis went by like his boots were lit. I'm writing that down.
HESTER: Sera Vant, you've not eaten. I can see it from here.
SERA: Later.
[Old Pell is on the quay, still beside his cut floats, staring north.]
PELL: Keeper! Did you give her my bearing?
KEEPER: She says it's exact.
PELL: Course it is. Go on, then. Run.
[On the net-loft roof, Tam has not moved. His needle is still going.]
TAM: It went over the pans! Big as a door! I didn't drop a stitch!
KEEPER: You kept mending?
TAM: Hole was there before it fell. Still there after.
TAM: Hester! Put it in the book! Not one stitch!
HESTER: Already have, love. Get down off that roof.
[Past the breakwater, a boat comes in hard with one lantern at the bow. A woman in a sou'wester stands at the rail.]
NELL: Keeper! You're the one off the breakwater?
NELL: Something came down right over my bow. Lost my bearing. First time in thirty years.
NELL: The Belt's not where my hands know it. Came home on your lamp instead.
NELL: Mind you keep it lit. There's not a herring in my hold, but I'd like to keep the boat.
NELL: Sera Vant! Is it the Belt? Is it coming down in bits?
SERA: I'll tell you where. Not what.
NELL: That's no answer!
SERA: It's the only true one I've got.
[Skip summary: Racing north through the harbor, Sera and the keeper pass Hester at the smokehouse, Tam still mending on his roof, and Nell Garrow, who lost her bearing and steered the Patience home on the keeper's unwatched lamp.]

### Scene 6 — The salt marsh
[Reed beds and black channels. Bram's lantern bobs far ahead. Sera stops every twenty steps to sight the sky.]
SERA: We're in the north marsh. Bram's ahead. Keep his lantern in front of you.
KEEPER: You're stopping to count? Now?
SERA: It came down twelve degrees off the Belt. I want that number before I see what it is.
[The reeds around them all lean the same way, toward a faint glow. An old man stands among them with a reed hook, very still. Something small is buttoned inside his coat.]
ABEL: Mind your feet, Surveyor. The channels have shifted too.
SERA: Abel Thorn. How long have the reeds been leaning like that?
ABEL: Since before it fell. Long enough for two prayers. I said three, to be safe.
SERA: How long is a prayer?
ABEL: Depends who's listening.
ABEL: Reeds lean to water. To the sun, a little. Never to a hole in the ground.
[A small bird peeps inside his coat. He covers it with one big hand.]
ABEL: Marsh hen. Ran out of the reeds and into my boots. Everything's running out of them tonight.
KEEPER: Will it be all right?
ABEL: She will. I've a box by the stove. She can have it till she stops shaking.
ABEL: Whatever came down there wants burying, Surveyor. Not looking at.
SERA: Then I'll look first. You can bury it after.
ABEL: You'll count it, and they'll call it haze, and I'll be out here living next to it.
SERA: Then count it with me.
ABEL: I don't count. I pray. It's slower, but it's never once been wrong.
KEEPER: The reeds. There's no wind.
SERA: All leaning. All one way.
SERA: Stay out of the channels. Bram can carry one of us. Not two.
[Objective: Reach the fall.]
[Skip summary: In the marsh, Sera stops to fix the angle of the fall. Abel Thorn, an old reed-cutter, says the reeds leaned toward it before it fell, and that it should be buried.]

### Scene 7 — The crater
[A bowl of glass where there was sand an hour ago. A girl of about seventeen sits on the rim in a salt-crusted smock, a rake across her knees, blinking. Far down in the bowl, Bram kneels with his lantern.]
SERA: Corra Vey. You're on the rim. Are you hurt?
CORRA: It knocked me flat. I was raking by lantern, and then I was lying down.
CORRA: You're Sera Vant. You drew the tide table on our pan-house door.
SERA: That copy's two years old. Tell Mags to take it down.
CORRA: Can I come up your light one night? I can count. I count salt all day.
SERA: Can you count to four hundred without stopping?
CORRA: I can learn.
SERA: Then learn. Then come.
CORRA: I touched the glass, before Bram got here. It's warm, like bread.
CORRA: I didn't go down. I didn't see what's in there. I swear I didn't.
BRAM: She was sat up like a seal when I came. Good girl. Stay sat.
BRAM: So. Funny thing about falling stars.
BRAM: This one's got feet.
[In the glass, a barefoot girl of about ten lies curled. She opens her eyes and looks up at the sky first, a moment too long. Then at Bram.]
GIRL: Is that a light? Is it yours? Why do you keep it in a box?
BRAM: It's a lantern, love. Keeps the wind off. Are you hurt?
GIRL: What's hurt? Is it a kind of cold? I have a kind of cold.
BRAM: Hang on. We'll fix the cold. Can you sit up for me?
GIRL: I'm sitting! Is this sitting? The ground is very hard and very down.
GIRL: Why is everybody so tall? Is tall a kind of standing?
BRAM: That's me sitting down, love. Wait till you see me stood up.
[Sera lifts the sextant at the girl.]
SERA: Don't move it.
BRAM: Her.
SERA: Don't move her. She came down twelve degrees from the only thing I've been counting.
SERA: Keeper. Mark where she's lying. Exactly.
BRAM: Sera. She's cold.
SERA: I know. Two minutes.
GIRL: Out of where? Is it far? Was I in it?
KEEPER: What's your name?
GIRL: I don't know! Is that bad? Is "what" a kind of name?
BRAM: It's not bad. It's just not finished yet.
[Far off across the salt pans, a lamp swings. A woman's voice carries over the reeds.]
MAGS: Corra Vey! You answer me, or I'm docking your share!
CORRA: That's Mags. She counts heads whenever anything happens. Even a cow calving.
BRAM: Go on, then. Let her count you. Tell her you're whole.
BRAM: How's the hand?
CORRA: Not burnt. It ought to be burnt. Why isn't it burnt?
BRAM: Ask Mags. She'll know, or she'll pretend. Off you go.
[Corra looks down at the girl in the glass, then at her own palm. She goes.]
[Skip summary: At the crater, Corra Vey from the salt pans, knocked flat by the fall, says she touched the glass first, then goes home to Mags. Bram finds a barefoot girl with no name in the glass. Sera wants her left where she fell.]

### Scene 8 — The crater rim
[Footsteps on the glass. A tall man in a magistrate's coat walks out of the reeds alone, on foot, gloved, with no lantern.]
BRAM: Magistrate! Long walk for a bit of haze.
