## Planning sheet (gacha 14.2)

**Player fantasy:** the night after the sky held still, you're in the room when two people who counted the same sky alone for a season finally read each other's records, and you get to hear who breaks the silence.

**Featured heroes: want / obstacle / cost**
- **Sera** wants Ivo's own hand, not the clerk's copy, laid against her wall night by night. Obstacle: reading it means reading the two weeks he had before her, and every night since that he matched her and said nothing. Cost: she gets proof of everything and it feels "like twelve"; the warm things she does are to stop herself measuring Wren's memory ("It doesn't need a number"), copy his first two weeks into the front of her own book ("I'll write small"), and tell him to bring it back tomorrow.
- **Bram** wants a supper eaten hot by everyone in the room. Obstacle: nobody picks up a bowl, and he won't take a side. Cost: he has to find words for something that isn't a joke, and he gets the first attempt wrong ("Broth's gone cold."). He still won't pick a side: "I picked supper. It's a side."
- **Ivo** wants to hand over his record without asking for anything back. Obstacle: he's lost "the face" and has no costing for being thanked. Cost: Sera asks him to call the count, which he isn't ready for; his pie remark is read aloud (by Sera); the ledger shows he wrote "clear" on the night he told the council haze, and the keeper says so; Wren gets an "almost", near the top; and he has to say "Good."
- **Wren** wants to know whether she's in the book. Obstacle: she isn't, except as "it" on night three. Cost: she remembers what before her name was like (all outside, nobody anywhere), and decides "Wren" was a promotion because it came with a coat.

**Recap blurb (written first)**

## Script

### Scene 1 — Called
[Dusk in the lamp room. The chalk wall is full, door to cradle. On the glass, in Sera's chalk, one word: bloom.]
[SERA stands at the glass with the sextant, her tide book open on the sill. The clerk's fair copy lies on the cradle step under the chalk tin. The Keeper sits beside it.]
[WREN sits on the floor with her back to the glass, knees up. The coat sleeves end near her elbows. In the dusk there's a faint light at her wrists and throat.]
[Objective: Take the dusk count.]
SERA: Keeper. Up. Arm straight.
KEEPER: Where am I looking?
SERA: Where you looked last night. It hasn't gone anywhere.
SERA: Yet.
WREN: Somebody's on the stair.
[Nobody else hears anything. SERA doesn't lower the sextant.]
WREN: Slow feet. Even. Carrying something heavy, flat against his chest. Is it the book?
SERA: Bram doesn't walk even.
WREN: No. Bram walks like soup.
[A long moment. Then, far down, slow and even, footsteps.]
WREN: I hear things a long way off now. And I can see in the dark.
WREN: It's not as good as it sounds. Mostly it's dust?
[IVO comes up into the lamp room, gloved, buttoned to the throat. The black ledger is held against his chest in both arms. He looks at the wall before he looks at anyone.]
IVO: Surveyor. Keeper. Wren.
WREN: You brought it up! Are you allowed? Isn't it the town's now?
IVO: It is council property. I have signed it out of the customs house. To myself, as chair.
IVO: The clerk asked me to sign twice. He wished to compare the signatures.
WREN: Were they the same?
IVO: Very nearly. He was not reassured.
KEEPER: You signed it out to yourself?
IVO: There was nobody left on the council I would trust to carry it up a stair, Keeper.
IVO: That is a sentence I did not expect to say out loud.
SERA: You're late.
[She says it to him. Not to Bram, not to the Keeper. To him.]
IVO: It is dusk now, Surveyor. I am exactly on time for now.
WREN: Did anybody sleep? I didn't. I watched it not move, all night, till the gulls came.
WREN: It's very tiring, watching nothing happen. Is that what you two do? Every night?
IVO: Every night, Wren. For a great many years.
SERA: Most nights it does something.
[SERA lifts the sextant again. She sights. She holds it. She doesn't ask the Keeper.]
SERA: Call it.
[IVO stands very still, the ledger in his arms.]
IVO: ...Thank you.
IVO: Left.
[SERA moves the sextant a hair to the left. She reads.]
SERA: Twelve.
IVO: Twelve.
[They say it together, or near enough. SERA writes it in her tide book. IVO opens the ledger against his arm, writes one line, and closes it without looking up.]
WREN: Same as last night. Same as all of last night. Is twelve a good number now? It used to be just a number.
SERA: It's the same number. That's all it is.
[On the stair, a heavy tread, a clank, and the creak of the ninth step.]
BRAM: Nobody move! Pot, loaf, the end of a cheese, and the ninth step's been told!
[BRAM backs up the last turn, storm lantern hooked on one finger, Hester's pot in both arms. LAMP ROOM is chalked on the lid in Hester's big letters.]
BRAM: Barley broth. Hester's barley, Hester's bacon, Hester's lid. Look what she's put on it.
WREN: "Lamp room." Not names?
BRAM: She says she's done with names on lids till Saltreach works out who's speaking to who.
BRAM: Might be a while, she says. Might be Lamp Night. Might be never, and she'll go back to numbers like the bakery.
[He looks round at them: Sera, Ivo, Wren, the Keeper.]
BRAM: Look at you all. Nobody's slept. I can tell by the faces. Ivo's is the same as it always is, but I can tell.
IVO: I slept from eleven until a quarter past. It was very refreshing.
BRAM: That's not sleep, that's blinking slow.
[He sets out bowls on the cradle step. One, two, three, four, five. He stops, his hand at his coat.]
[Choice a: "No spare tonight?"]
[Choice b: "Five. That's everyone."]
[Choices converge.]
BRAM: Spare's in my coat. I always bring a spare. Somebody always turns up.
BRAM: Only tonight everybody already has. Funny feeling. Like a stair with one step too few.
IVO: I was not invited, Hollis.
BRAM: You weren't invited. You were counted. It's a different thing altogether.
IVO: Then I stand corrected. Or sit, if that is on offer.
[BRAM looks at him properly for the first time.]
BRAM: You're still not doing the face.
IVO: Which face?
BRAM: The one where you've already won.
IVO: I have looked for it, Hollis. It has not turned up. I shall try again in the morning.
[BRAM ladles broth into five bowls. Nobody picks one up. IVO is looking at the wall. SERA is looking at the book under his arm.]
SERA: That's the book.
IVO: It is.
SERA: Not the clerk's copy. Yours.
IVO: Mine.
SERA: How far back does it go?
IVO: Further than your wall. Further than the bad year.
SERA: I know. I heard it read. Standing in the water.
IVO: Then you have heard the worst of it, Surveyor. The rest is only numbers.
SERA: The clerk makes his sevens like ones. I want your hand.
[IVO looks at her a moment. Then he crosses the room to the oldest mark, low by the door, and kneels.]
[Skip summary: At dusk on the evening after the sky held still, Ivo carries his ledger up the lighthouse stair, signed out to himself. Sera speaks to him directly and asks him to call the dusk count; they both read twelve. Bram brings barley broth and five bowls, one for each of them, and keeps the spare in his coat. Sera asks for Ivo's own book, not the clerk's copy, and Ivo kneels at the oldest mark on the wall.]

### Scene 2 — Side by side
[IVO opens the ledger on his knee at the oldest mark. SERA kneels beside him. On the cradle step, five bowls steam, untouched.]
SERA: Bram. Light.
[BRAM lifts the storm lantern and holds it where she needs it, over the wall and the page both.]
[SERA puts a finger on her first mark. IVO puts a finger on a line in the ledger. She reads his. He reads hers.]
[Neither says anything.]
[IVO turns back a page. Then another. Two weeks of lines in his hand, before her wall begins. SERA reads them one at a time. Her finger doesn't leave her first mark.]
[IVO looks at the wall while she reads. Not at her.]
[WREN sits down on the top step of the stair with her arms round her knees. She doesn't ask anything. BRAM looks at her twice, because she always asks something.]
[They move along the wall. SERA's finger goes from stone to stone. IVO turns the pages to keep up. Sometimes he waits for her. Sometimes she waits for him.]
[On the cradle step, the broth stops steaming.]
[Choice a: (Put Hester's lid back on the pot.)]
[Choice b: (Open the fair copy at the same night, and hold it.)]
[Choices converge.]
[The night the whole town saw: SERA's 36 and 32. IVO's page says the same. SERA's breath goes out, once.]
[The night after: SERA's mark, rubbed out and chalked again a little to the left. IVO's page has the corrected number, and something small down the margin. SERA reads it. She doesn't say anything. BRAM leans in to look, and his shoulders shake once. He bites it back.]
[BRAM changes the lantern to his other hand. He doesn't lower it.]
[The night of the bar: SERA's 28, and beside it, 2.10. The lantern dips a hand's width. Then BRAM lifts it again.]
[WREN's long line on the last course, "W. 21." IVO's finger stops. There is nothing in his column for her. WREN watches the finger from the stair. SERA's finger rests on Wren's line longer than on any number.]
[The last stone: 20, and the Keeper's "1 finger". The wall ends there.]
[The ledger doesn't. IVO turns the page. Sixteen. Sixteen. Twelve. Twelve. And tonight's, still wet: twelve.]
[SERA takes her tide book out of her coat, opens it at the same nights, and lays it on the ledger's page. Her hand and his hand. The same numbers, one above the other.]
[Nobody speaks. Outside the glass, the Belt sits where it sat last night.]
[It goes on a long time.]
[At last, BRAM lowers the lantern.]
BRAM: Broth's gone cold.
[Nobody moves.]
BRAM: That's not what I meant to say. Give me a minute. I'll have another go.
[He sets the lantern on the floor between them, the wall on one side and the book on the other.]
BRAM: My mam carried supper up this stair every night. You know that. My grandad ate it on that step, before he lit.
BRAM: And every night she'd ask him, "Anyone out?" And he'd tell her which boats, and where, and what the sea was up to.
BRAM: Then she'd tell him who'd had a baby, and whose roof was in, and who wasn't speaking to who at the smokehouse.
BRAM: Same night, see. Him from up here, her from down there. And neither one of them had the whole of it.
BRAM: I used to think he kept the light. He didn't. They did. It took the two of them, and one supper.
BRAM: You two have been writing the same sky down every night since summer. A street apart. Him in a book, you on a wall.
BRAM: Each of you sat up on your own, thinking you were the only one awake in Saltreach.
BRAM: I'm not picking. I said I wouldn't and I won't. That's not what this is.
BRAM: Only my mam would've looked at that and said, "Two lamps burning for one sky? Daft. Bring him up and feed him."
BRAM: So. I've brought him up. The broth's cold, and I'm not carrying it down again. I'm not made of knees.
[Silence again. Shorter.]
[SERA looks at the two records, one laid on the other. She touches her first mark on the wall. Then his first line in the book.]
SERA: Two counts. Same place.
[IVO's gloved hand closes on the edge of the ledger, and stays there.]
IVO: Your hand was steadier than mine. At the start.
SERA: I didn't read it for you.
IVO: No. I know.
[SERA leaves her tide book lying on his page. She doesn't take it back.]
[Skip summary: Sera and Ivo kneel at the full wall and read the two records side by side, night by night, while Bram holds the lantern. Nobody speaks for a long time. Ivo's ledger starts two weeks before Sera's wall and goes on past it. At last Bram talks about his mam and grandad sharing one night from two ends, and says nobody needs two lamps burning for one sky. Sera answers: "Two counts. Same place."]

### Scene 3 — Remarks
[The bowls are cold. WREN gets up off the stair and picks up Hester's pot in both hands.]
WREN: I can fix cold. Watch. It's not a shift. I'm only warm.
BRAM: Gently, Sparrow.
WREN: Gently is all I do now. I'm very good at gently. Tam says so. He says it from across the room.
[She holds the pot against her. The faint light at her wrists comes up a little. In a while, the broth begins to steam again.]
WREN: There. Is that useful? I'd like to be the useful kind.
BRAM: That's the most useful thing anybody's done in this tower in years, love. Bowls, everybody. Before she gets bored.
[They eat. SERA eats at the wall with the tide book open on her knee. IVO eats on the cradle step, the ledger beside him.]
SERA: It's hot.
BRAM: It's meant to be.
[SERA looks at him. She nearly says something else. She eats instead. Once, she turns to the back of her tide book, makes a stroke, and closes it.]
BRAM: Every night the same as his, Sera. All the way down the wall. You were right.
SERA: I know.
BRAM: How's it feel?
[SERA thinks about it, properly, the way she'd take a reading.]
SERA: Like twelve.
BRAM: Is that good?
SERA: It's twelve.
[BRAM puts his spoon down and turns to IVO.]
BRAM: Now, then. Something in your book.
IVO: There is a great deal in my book, Hollis. The town has heard most of it.
BRAM: Night after the whole harbor saw. Down the side. In ink.
IVO: The remarks column. Every log has one.
BRAM: Read it out.
IVO: I would rather not.
SERA: "Herring pie. Adequate."
[She says it without looking up from her bowl.]
BRAM: Adequate! In the sky book! Next to the stars, in ink, for ever!
IVO: It was a remark. That is what the column is for.
BRAM: That's not a remark. That's an insult with a ruler under it.
IVO: It was adequate.
WREN: What's adequate? Is it a kind of pie?
WREN: I'm joking. I know what adequate is now. It's what Ivo says instead of good.
IVO: It is a perfectly respectable word, Wren.
SERA: The crust was underdone.
BRAM: Sera!
SERA: The bottom crust. You said mind the bottom. Nobody's oven.
[BRAM looks from one of them to the other. They're agreeing. He doesn't know what to do with his face.]
BRAM: You two have agreed about the sky every night since summer and never said a word.
BRAM: And the first thing you agree on out loud is my pie.
WREN: Am I in it? In the remarks?
[IVO's spoon stops.]
WREN: I watched your finger. On my line. There wasn't anything. But maybe down the side?
[IVO opens the ledger and turns back a long way, to night three. He reads the line the way he reads everything, as if it might be minuted.]
IVO: "Clear. Something fell in the marsh. Hollis named it."
KEEPER: Clear? You told the council haze.
IVO: I did, Keeper. And I came home and wrote down clear.
IVO: Both are in my hand. That is the trouble with keeping a record. It keeps you as well.
WREN: It. I was an it.
BRAM: For about a minute, love. Then you were a Wren.
WREN: I remember before. Being an it. It was all outside. No in, anywhere. And nobody was anywhere.
SERA: How far?
[She has the pencil up. She stops. She puts it down on the tide book.]
SERA: No. Never mind. It doesn't need a number.
WREN: Good. I haven't got one. It just went on, all the way out.
WREN: Then Bram said "Wren", and everything got small. In a good way. Like a room?
[BRAM goes very quiet. Then he finds his voice.]
BRAM: Like a room. That's it exactly, Sparrow. Rooms are for who's in. I told you that.
WREN: You did. I kept it. Not on my arm. Inside, where the soup goes.
WREN: So is "it" a promotion, or is "Wren"? I think Wren. Wren came with a coat.
[IVO looks at the line in the ledger for a long moment.]
IVO: It was accurate, for that night. It would not be accurate now.
WREN: That's a nice thing. In a cold voice. Is that how you do nice?
[At the left corner of IVO's mouth, something moves. Very little.]
WREN: There! You did it! The corner went!
IVO: It did not.
BRAM: It did a bit.
SERA: Left corner. One degree.
[Everyone looks at SERA. She turns a page of her tide book.]
IVO: Noted.
[IVO takes out his plain notebook, writes one line, and closes it without looking down.]
WREN: Under "almost"?
IVO: Under "almost." Near the top.
WREN: Near the top! Bram, near the top! Is near the top the same as nearly?
BRAM: It's nearer than he's been since I've known him, Sparrow. And I've known him a long time.
[SERA has slid the ledger's first pages next to her tide book. She is copying his two weeks into the front of it, small.]
IVO: You will want the tide column as well.
SERA: I want all of it.
IVO: It is a great deal of copying, Surveyor.
SERA: Then I'll write small.
[IVO watches her pencil a moment. He doesn't take the book back.]
[Skip summary: Wren warms the cold broth by holding the pot, and they eat it hot. Bram finds Ivo's ledger called his herring pie "adequate", and Sera agrees the crust was underdone, the first thing she and Ivo agree on out loud. The ledger called Wren "it" on night three, and called the night clear when Ivo told the council haze. Ivo says "it" wouldn't be accurate now, and nearly smiles. Sera starts copying his first two weeks into her tide book.]

### Scene 4 — The stair head
[Later. Five empty bowls on the cradle step. WREN has gone to the glass. SERA is still copying, a page and a half in.]
[IVO stands and buttons his coat to the throat. He holds out his hand for the ledger.]
SERA: I'm not done.
IVO: It is signed out to me until morning, Surveyor. The clerk will be on the customs house step with a pen.
SERA: Then bring it tomorrow. Dusk.
IVO: I will be late.
SERA: You'll be on time for then.
[IVO stands with his hand still out. Whatever he means to say, he doesn't say it.]
IVO: ...Yes.
[SERA closes the ledger on her pencil to keep the place, and gives it to him. He takes it in both arms.]
[BRAM picks up the storm lantern and walks him to the stair head. The Keeper goes with them.]
BRAM: Take the light. That stair's black as a boot below the first turn.
IVO: One gill a house, Hollis. I wrote it myself.
BRAM: My lantern counts as a house. I decided weeks ago. Nobody's fined me.
IVO: Nobody has noticed.
BRAM: You noticed.
IVO: I failed to. I have had a great deal of practice.
[He doesn't take the lantern. He looks back once into the room: Sera at the wall, Wren at the glass.]
WREN: Ivo? Will you come up tomorrow? Even if it moves?
