## Planning sheet (gacha 14.2)

**Player fantasy:** the night after the worst quarrel of their lives, you're the one everyone talks through, and you get to see them find the next place to write.

**Featured heroes: want / obstacle / cost**
- **Sera** wants tonight's count written down somewhere exact. Obstacle: there's no stone left, and every place anyone offers is wrong (boots, glass, a wet stair, a pot lid, the magistrate's paper). Cost: when Wren offers her arm, Sera sees what she did to Wren on the wall. She can't write on her, and has to say "I know" to Bram, the first thing she says to him tonight.
- **Bram** wants supper eaten and the room warm again. Obstacle: Sera won't speak to him, and last night's bowl is still full. Cost: his idea (the pot lid) gets turned into an accusation: he'd like a record that washes off. Ivo tells him he'd have done the same, which is no comfort.
- **Ivo** wants the only lit window in Saltreach dark, and a look at the full wall. Obstacle: nobody listens to him about paper. Cost: he tells Bram the truest thing he says all act ("One gets used to it. That is the worst of it.") and has to leave without anyone hearing it but the keeper.
- **Wren** wants to help, and to belong in Sera's counts. Obstacle: she has grown past her own height mark and there's nowhere left to be written. Cost: she offers herself as the record, and nobody can tell her plainly why that's wrong. She keeps her sleeve up, just in case.

## Script

### Scene 1 — Out of wall
[Dusk in the lamp room. The chalk wall is full, every stone written, down to the last one low by the door. SERA stands at the glass with the sextant raised.]
[By the wall sits last night's bowl of stew, untouched, a skin on it. The Keeper sits on the cradle step with their chalk stub.]
[Objective: Take the dusk count.]
SERA: Keeper. Chalk.
SERA: Twenty. A finger east of the bell post. Same as last night.
KEEPER: Where do I write it?
[SERA lowers the sextant and turns to the wall with her own chalk raised. She stops.]
[She looks along the bottom course, stone by stone. Then up. Then along again.]
SERA: Hold it.
KEEPER: Hold the number?
SERA: You've got a head. Hold it in that.
[On the stair: a heavy tread, and the creak of the ninth step. No shout. No joke. BRAM comes up the last turn with a covered pot.]
BRAM: Evening.
[He says it to the room. SERA doesn't turn. BRAM looks at last night's bowl, full and cold.]
[He picks it up without a word. He sets a fresh bowl in its place and fills it from the pot. It steams.]
SERA: Keeper. Tell him the ninth step's worse.
BRAM: Keeper, tell her I know. Four generations of Hollis have known about that step.
[Choice a: "Tell him yourself."]
[Choice b: "He heard you."]
[Choices converge.]
SERA: Then he doesn't need telling twice.
BRAM: Never did, Keeper. That was always the trouble with me.
[Quick feet on the stair, two steps at a time. WREN comes up holding Bram's storm lantern high over her head.]
WREN: Bram! Look! I'm bigger than the lantern now!
BRAM: Then you'd better stay where I can see you, Sparrow.
WREN: You can always see me! I'm the brightest thing on the stair. After the lantern. Most nights.
[She sets the lantern on the cradle step and holds out her arms. The coat sleeves stop short of her wrists. There is nothing left to roll.]
WREN: Look. No rolls. It fits! It more than fits. Is more than fits a thing?
[BRAM looks at her wrists, then away. He takes the lid off the pot.]
BRAM: Fish soup. Hester's fish, Hester's pot, my knees on the stair, and the ninth step getting its opinion in as usual. Bowls, everybody.
[WREN peers at the cold bowl still in Bram's other hand.]
WREN: What's that one? It's got a skin on. Is soup allowed to grow a skin?
BRAM: That's last night's. Sera wasn't hungry.
SERA: Keeper. Tell him I can say what I was.
BRAM: Keeper, tell her she didn't. Not last night. Not one word, start to finish.
[A pause. WREN looks from one to the other, and back.]
WREN: Why's everybody being so careful? Did the stair say something rude?
WREN: Sera's talking to the Keeper, and Bram's talking to the Keeper. Is the Keeper the wall now?
KEEPER: Apparently.
WREN: Can I talk through the Keeper too? Keeper, tell Bram the soup smells like a boat.
BRAM: Keeper, tell her that's the good part.
WREN: It works! It's slow, though. Is it supposed to be slow?
SERA: Wren. Away from the wall. You'll smudge it.
WREN: There's nowhere to smudge. It's all written. Even the corner. Even the corner of the corner.
SERA: I know where it's written.
[SERA stands at the wall with the chalk up, holding the number in her head.]
SERA: Keeper. Read me the last stone.
KEEPER: "Twenty."
SERA: That's last night. That's the last of it.
[She looks at the stone a long moment.]
SERA: Tell him I'd have put the tide under it. If I'd had the tide.
[BRAM stops with the ladle over a bowl. He puts the ladle back in the pot.]
[He says nothing. He goes quiet, the way he does when he's frightened.]
WREN: So where does twenty go? It can't just float about. Can it?
SERA: The floor.
[SERA kneels and sets the chalk to the floor stones. She starts a two.]
BRAM: Keeper. Tell her that's where I stand.
SERA: Tell him to stand somewhere else.
BRAM: Tell her there isn't anywhere else. It's a round room. I've been all the way round it. Twice, tonight.
WREN: If you write the sky on the floor, we'd all be standing on it! Is that allowed? It feels rude.
SERA: Boots smudge. Salt comes in on them. A month, and it's mud.
[She rubs the half-made two out with her thumb, and gets up.]
SERA: Not the floor.
WREN: The glass! Write it on the glass, right where the stars are!
[Before anyone can stop her, WREN takes the chalk out of the Keeper's hand and writes a two on the window. The chalk shrieks on the glass.]
BRAM: Ow. My teeth. My grandad's teeth, and he's been in the churchyard nine years.
WREN: Sorry! It's a loud number! Twos are always loud. Is it the corner?
[SERA lifts the sextant to the glass and sights. The chalk two sits right across the Belt.]
SERA: Now I'm sighting through my own numbers.
WREN: Is that bad? I like it. It's like the stars have got labels on.
SERA: Another month and I can't see out. Rub it off.
[WREN rubs it off. Her sleeve won't reach, so she uses her wrist.]
[Skip summary: The evening after the quarrel, the chalk wall is full and Sera has tonight's count, twenty, with nowhere to write it. Bram takes away last night's untouched supper and sets down a fresh bowl. Sera and Bram speak only through the keeper. They try the floor, then Wren tries the glass; neither will do.]

### Scene 2 — Paper
[Slow, even footsteps on the stair. No lantern light comes up with them.]
BRAM: That's Ivo.
WREN: How do you know?
BRAM: Nobody else climbs a dark stair like it owes him money.
[IVO comes up into the lamp room, gloved, buttoned to the throat. He looks at the lit glass first, then at the full wall.]
IVO: Hollis. Wren. Keeper. Surveyor.
IVO: This is the only lit window in Saltreach after supper. It can be seen from every door on the quay.
BRAM: It's one lantern, Ivo.
IVO: It is one lantern. It is also forty people at their doors, looking up at it, when the council has asked them not to.
IVO: The harbor fund has oil for eleven weeks. Less, if Saltreach decides to sit up admiring a tower.
BRAM: "Don't look up after supper." I know. I heard Hester say it to a gull this morning.
BRAM: We're above supper up here, though, aren't we? We're not looking up. We're the up.
WREN: We're the up! Ivo, we're the up! Does that make us after supper?
IVO: It makes you a matter for the council, Wren.
SERA: Wren. Tell the magistrate people only look where they're told not to.
WREN: She says people only look where—
IVO: I heard her.
[He stands before the wall. He reads along the last course, stone by stone, the way Sera did.]
IVO: You have run out of wall, Surveyor.
[SERA doesn't answer.]
IVO: Paper is cheap. It lies flat. It does not require a tower, or a stair, or a surveyor kneeling in the dark.
[Nobody answers. SERA crosses to the stair head and looks down the first turn.]
SERA: The stair.
IVO: Paper also—
SERA: Keeper. A hundred and eight steps. One count a step. That's three months.
KEEPER: If nothing speeds up.
SERA: Then two.
WREN: Not the stair!
[It comes out fast. Everyone looks at her.]
WREN: I count the stair. Every night, up and down. If there's numbers on it, I'll count those instead and get lost.
WREN: And then I'd be lost on a stair. With a wall on one side. Is that even possible?
BRAM: With you, Sparrow? I'd put money on it.
BRAM: Keeper. Tell her the stair goes down.
SERA: Tell him I know which way a stair goes.
BRAM: Tell her that's all I'm saying. Every count she writes down there, she'll have to look down to read.
SERA: Tell him that's what he'd like.
[BRAM has no answer. He picks up the storm lantern and trims a wick that doesn't need it.]
IVO: I see the surveyor has extended her arrangement to you, Hollis.
BRAM: Don't, Ivo.
IVO: I only meant that I am glad of the company. It is a cold arrangement, for one.
SERA: It's dark below the first turn. I'd need a light to read it.
[BRAM holds the storm lantern out toward her, at arm's length. SERA looks at it. She doesn't take it.]
SERA: Wren. Come and hold the chalk.
[SERA goes down into the dark without a light. WREN goes after her, two steps at a time, and the stair swallows them.]
[At the top: BRAM, IVO and the Keeper. BRAM is still holding the lantern out over nothing. He lowers it.]
IVO: I am told there was a page.
BRAM: Who told you?
IVO: Saltreach. Saltreach tells me everything, in the end. Mostly at Hester's.
IVO: A leaf from the breakwater log is council property, Hollis.
BRAM: Going to fine me?
IVO: I am going to fail to notice. I have had a great deal of practice.
BRAM: You're doing that face where you've already won.
IVO: It is my only face, Hollis.
[A pause. IVO lowers his voice, though there is nobody to hear but the Keeper.]
IVO: For what it is worth, I would have cut it too.
BRAM: I know you would. That's the bit that keeps me up.
BRAM: She'd have gone down and stood in it, Ivo. Out on the bar, up to her knees, with the sextant.
IVO: She would. Which is precisely why one does not tell her.
BRAM: Then why does one feel like this?
[IVO looks at the full wall for a long moment.]
IVO: One gets used to it, Hollis. That is the worst of it.
[From down the stair, WREN's voice, counting: "Eleven. Twelve. Thirteen." Then SERA's: "Enough."]
[SERA comes back up into the lamplight, WREN behind her.]
SERA: It's wet below the twentieth step. Chalk runs.
WREN: And it's dark, and it smells like an old boot! A nice boot. But definitely a boot.
IVO: Paper, Surveyor, does not run, if one keeps it in a drawer—
BRAM: Pot lid.
[Everyone looks at him. He holds up the lid of Hester's pot. BRAM is chalked on it in Hester's big letters.]
BRAM: Hester's chalked names on pot lids for thirty years. Chalk takes to it lovely. And it's warm.
BRAM: Every night you write the count on the lid, and every night there's soup under it. Twenty, and fish soup.
BRAM: I'd carry it up. I'm carrying it up anyway. The sky and the supper in one trip, and nobody's knees the worse.
WREN: That's the best one! The sky on the soup!
SERA: Lids get washed.
BRAM: That's the beauty of it.
[Silence. SERA doesn't look at him.]
SERA: Wren. Tell him he'd like that. Things that wash off.
[BRAM sets the lid down on the pot. Very carefully, as if it might break.]
WREN: Bram? Tell her back. You always tell her back.
BRAM: Not tonight, Sparrow.
IVO: Your last mark is two degrees off, by the way.
SERA: Wren. Tell him it's two degrees closer than his.
WREN: She says it's two degrees closer than yours! Have you got one? Is it on paper?
IVO: It is on nothing at all tonight, Wren. I suggested paper.
[Nobody answers.]
IVO: Four times.
[Skip summary: Ivo comes up to say the lamp room is the only lit window in Saltreach after supper, and suggests paper; nobody listens. Sera tries the stair, which is dark and wet. Alone at the top, Ivo tells Bram he's heard about the cut page and would have done the same, which doesn't help. Bram suggests writing the count on Hester's pot lid, and Sera says he'd like a record that washes off.]

### Scene 3 — The arm
[Five bowls on the cradle step, going cold. SERA stands at the full wall with the chalk raised. Twenty is still only in her head.]
WREN: I know where.
[WREN goes to the wall and stands with her back against it, very straight, the way she stood to be measured. In the last course, at her knee, is a number in Sera's hand: her height, two nights ago.]
WREN: That one's me. Sera put me there. I'm in the last row.
WREN: Only I'm not that any more. I'm past it. Look. I'm out of wall too.
WREN: Isn't that funny? We're both out.
[Nobody laughs. WREN pushes her right sleeve up her arm. It doesn't have far to go.]
WREN: So write it on me.
SERA: Wren.
WREN: I'm here every dusk. I've stopped smudging, mostly. And you don't have to kneel for me any more. I'm the right height.
WREN: And every time the sky goes, there's more of me. I'm the only thing up here that's getting bigger.
WREN: Isn't that handy? I think that's handy.
[IVO's gloved hand goes to his coat, toward the notebook, and stops there.]
[She holds out her bare forearm to Sera, palm up.]
WREN: I'd hold it. I'm good at holding a place. I think it might be the thing I'm best at.
WREN: Better than stairs, anyway. Is that a thing to be good at?
[SERA lifts the chalk. BRAM doesn't move. IVO doesn't move.]
[The chalk stops a finger's width from Wren's arm. SERA looks at the arm. Then at the number on the wall, at Wren's knee. Then back at the arm.]
[BRAM opens his mouth. He shuts it again. This time, he waits.]
SERA: Put your sleeve down.
WREN: It doesn't go down. It stops there now.
[SERA takes the cuff and pulls it toward Wren's wrist. It won't reach. She holds it there anyway, her hand closed round Wren's wrist.]
WREN: Careful. I'm warm. It's not a shift, I'm only warm. I'd tell you if it was.
SERA: Warm. Not hot. I know the difference.
WREN: Tam's hands are getting better. He says they itch now. Hester says itching means mending.
WREN: I don't hold them. I just look at them. Is looking allowed?
SERA: Looking's allowed.
WREN: Then why can't you write on me? I don't mind being a number. I like being in your counts.
[SERA has no answer. Her hand stays where it is.]
BRAM: Because you're not one, Sparrow.
[He says it to Wren. He's looking at Sera.]
SERA: I know.
[She says it to Bram. It's the first thing she has said to him tonight.]
[BRAM stands very still. Then he nods, once, and looks down at the pot.]
WREN: ...So where does twenty go?
[SERA lets go of Wren's wrist. She turns to the Keeper.]
SERA: Tonight it goes on a hand.
[Choice a: "Use mine."]
[Choice b: "Use yours."]
[Choices converge.]
SERA: Both. Two hands, same number. Hold still.
[She chalks 20 on the back of the Keeper's hand. Then on the back of her own.]
SERA: Tomorrow I'll find stone.
KEEPER: Where?
SERA: I don't know yet.
[She looks at the whole wall, door to cradle, every stone of it.]
SERA: I never thought it would fill. I thought the sky would stop first.
IVO: Or paper.
[Nobody answers. WREN giggles.]
WREN: That was on purpose! You said paper on purpose! Was that funny? Did you almost smile?
[IVO takes out his small plain notebook, writes one line, and closes it without looking down.]
IVO: I noted it. Under "almost."
WREN: On paper!
IVO: On paper, Wren. Somebody in this room has to.
[He buttons his coat and goes to the stair head. He stops beside Bram.]
IVO: Good night, Hollis. Put the light out soon. People are looking.
BRAM: Let them look. It's only a lantern.
IVO: It always is.
[He goes down into the dark without a light, slow and even, and then gone.]
[WREN sits down on the cradle step beside Bram, close, but not touching.]
WREN: Bram? If I'm not a number, what am I? On the wall, I mean.
BRAM: You're not on the wall, Sparrow. You're in the room. That's a different thing altogether.
BRAM: Walls are for what's out there. Rooms are for who's in. My grandad would've told you that, and then made you eat something.
WREN: I've eaten! Two bowls! Is two bowls a room?
BRAM: Two bowls is a start. Three's a room. Hester's rule.
WREN: Then I'll have a third. For the room. Not because I'm growing.
BRAM: No. Course not. Here.
[BRAM sits on the cradle step with the storm lantern and trims the wick. He doesn't look at Sera. He's careful not to.]
[SERA picks up the bowl by the wall. The soup has gone cold. She eats it anyway, all of it.]
[Then she opens the back of her tide book, writes something short, and closes it.]
SERA: It's cold.
BRAM: It's meant to be hot.
SERA: I know.
[BRAM's hands stop on the wick for a moment. Then they go on.]
[WREN has gone to the glass. She looks up at the Belt, a moment too long, then turns back to the room. Her sleeve is still pushed up.]
WREN: Sera? Tomorrow, if there's still no stone...
WREN: I'll leave my sleeve up. Just in case. Is that allowed?
[Skip summary: Wren notices she has grown past the height Sera chalked on the wall, and offers her arm as the next place to write. Sera can't do it. She tries to pull Wren's short sleeve down over her wrist, and when Bram says Wren isn't a number, Sera answers him directly for the first time tonight: "I know."
