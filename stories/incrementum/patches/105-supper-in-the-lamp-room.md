## Planning sheet (gacha 14.2)

**Player fantasy:** the fight is over, and you have a seat at the table where four difficult people eat together.

**Featured heroes: want / obstacle / cost**
- **Bram** wants one proper supper with everyone sat down. Obstacle: Sera's counting, Wren's sleeves, and a guest he didn't invite. Cost: he tells Wren the coat shrank and the pie made her grow, and Ivo sees him do it.
- **Sera** wants tonight's mark exact, in the last clean course. Obstacle: Bram shouted on the ninth step and jogged her chalk, and the only one who noticed is the man she won't speak to. Cost: she has to take his number. She does it without taking him.
- **Ivo** wants to see the wall after the whole town saw the sky move, without saying so. Obstacle: he has no reason to be up here, so he invents one (the council wants a report on the tower). Cost: he admits he saw it, to a child, and is told seeing isn't saying.
- **Wren** wants to know what supper is. Obstacle: everyone gives a different answer. Cost: none she can see. She decides supper is somewhere she belongs.

## Script

### Scene 1 — A proper supper
[Dusk in the lamp room. The chalk wall is full except for one clean course of stone along the bottom. SERA kneels at it, sextant in one hand, chalk in the other.]
[The Keeper sits on the step of the empty lamp cradle. Below, on the stair, something heavy clanks.]
[Objective: Sit down to supper.]
BRAM: Nobody move! I've got a pot, a pie and a loaf, and the ninth step's in a mood!
[SERA's chalk jumps as she makes the mark. She looks at it. She doesn't rub it out.]
[BRAM backs up the last steps, lantern hooked on one finger, Hester's pot in both arms.]
BRAM: Lamp room, dusk, everybody still in one piece. That's a supper. A proper one, sat down.
SERA: I'm counting.
BRAM: You're always counting. Tonight you can count with a spoon in your hand.
SERA: One course left. I want it exact.
BRAM: She said that about the last course, Keeper. And the one before.
KEEPER: What's in the pot?
BRAM: Herring pie. Hester's crust, my herring, and nobody's oven, so mind the bottom.
BRAM: The whole harbor was out on the quay last night, necks bent like herons. Nobody ate.
BRAM: Half the pies in Saltreach went to the gulls. Hester was beside herself.
BRAM: So tonight we eat. That's the rule. I just made it.
SERA: And when the wall's full?
BRAM: Then we eat next to a full wall. I'm told it's very restful.
SERA: I'll need more stone.
BRAM: You'll need more pie. One thing at a time.
[He sets out bowls on the cradle step: one, two, three, four. Then a fifth, from inside his coat.]
KEEPER: Five?
BRAM: I always bring a spare. Somebody always turns up.
[Bare feet slap on the stair, fast. WREN comes up at a run in Bram's coat. The sleeves hang over her hands.]
WREN: Bram! I did the whole stair! Up! By myself, with no hands on the wall!
BRAM: That's my girl. How many steps tonight?
WREN: A hundred and eleventeen!
SERA: Eleventeen isn't a number.
WREN: It is on the stair! The stair keeps changing its mind!
BRAM: Did you mind the ninth?
WREN: I said good evening to it, like you said. It didn't say it back. Is that rude, for a step?
BRAM: It's old. Old steps don't talk. They just remember who stamped on them.
WREN: I didn't stamp! I was very soft! Was I soft, Keeper? Did you hear me?
KEEPER: Like a herd of something.
WREN: A herd! Is a herd soft?
BRAM: Come here. Your sleeves are in the gravy before there's any gravy.
[He rolls the left sleeve. Once. Twice. Three times. Her hand is out. He stops with the fourth fold half made.]
[He rolls the right sleeve. Three times. Her hand is out.]
[BRAM is quiet.]
SERA: Four rolls last night. Three this morning.
BRAM: I can count rolls, Sera.
[He smooths the sleeves flat, slowly. Then he grins.]
BRAM: So. Funny thing about coats. Seems this one's shrinking. I'll have words with it.
WREN: Did it shrink? Or did I big? Is "big" a thing you do, like the stair?
BRAM: It's a thing you eat. Pie makes you big. Everyone knows that.
WREN: I want to be a lot big! I want to be lantern big!
[SERA stands up, chalk ready, and looks at the wall, then at Wren.]
SERA: Stand by the wall. I want her height.
BRAM: After supper.
SERA: It'll take a minute.
BRAM: Then it'll take a minute after supper. Sit down, Sera.
[A beat. SERA sits, on the far side of the pot from the wall.]
WREN: Bram? What's a supper?
BRAM: What's a... love, you've had supper every night since the marsh.
WREN: I've had stew! And bread, and one fish with a face! Nobody said supper!
WREN: After the glass dogs, you said "supper's on me." I looked all over you. It wasn't on you anywhere.
WREN: And it always comes when the stars come. Is it a kind of star?
[BRAM laughs so hard he has to put the pie down.]
BRAM: "Supper's on me" means I'm paying, love. It's a thing you say.
WREN: But you didn't pay. Hester wrote it in her book. I saw.
BRAM: That's another thing you say.
[Choice a: "It's food you eat at dusk."]
[Choice b: "Yes. A small one. With a crust."]
[Choices converge.]
WREN: I'm going to watch it very hard. Then I'll know.
BRAM: You'll know by eating it. Spoons, everybody. Before it goes cold and Hester hears.
[WREN takes a bite of pie, and her eyes go wide.]
WREN: It's loud! Bram, it's loud in my mouth! Is hot a kind of loud?
BRAM: Blow on it. Everybody has to blow on Hester's crust. It's tradition.
[Skip summary: The evening after the sky moved where the whole town could see, Bram brings a proper supper up to the lamp room. Wren's coat sleeves need only three rolls now; Bram jokes that the coat is shrinking and won't let Sera measure her until after supper. Wren asks what supper is.]

### Scene 2 — Uninvited
[Footsteps on the stair. Slow, even. No lantern light comes up with them.]
BRAM: That's not Hester. Hester stamps.
[IVO comes up into the lamp room, gloved, his coat buttoned to the throat. He looks at the wall before he looks at anyone.]
IVO: Hollis. Surveyor. Keeper. And the child.
WREN: Wren! I'm a Wren! You know that! You were there!
IVO: I was. Good evening, Wren.
BRAM: Magistrate. You're late for supper, and you weren't asked, so that's two things.
IVO: I am not here for supper. The council requires a report on this tower. Its condition.
BRAM: Its condition is old, and there's pie in it.
[SERA turns her back on Ivo and faces the wall.]
SERA: Bram. Tell the magistrate the tower is standing.
BRAM: Sera, he's four feet away.
SERA: Then he can see it standing.
IVO: Noted.
[He takes a small plain notebook from his coat, writes one line, and puts it away without looking down.]
WREN: What did you write? Was it me? Was it the pie?
IVO: It was the tower.
WREN: Is the tower in trouble? Is that why your face is like that?
IVO: The tower is satisfactory. My face is like this generally.
BRAM: I'll give you your report. Save you the stair next time.
BRAM: Hinges, oiled. Glass, salty. Ninth step, in a mood. Wall, nearly full of somebody's opinions.
BRAM: Lamp room, occupied by four people and a pie. Five people. Five and most of a pie.
IVO: Thank you, Hollis. That is more than the council will read.
[BRAM holds out the fifth bowl.]
BRAM: Sit. I brought a spare.
IVO: Then you were expecting me.
BRAM: I was expecting somebody. You're what turned up.
[IVO looks at the bowl a long moment. He takes it, and sits on the cradle step, as far from the wall as the room allows.]
BRAM: You're doing that face where you've already won.
IVO: It is my only face, Hollis.
[WREN climbs up beside him and holds out her arms to show the sleeves.]
WREN: Look! Three rolls! I ate so much I grew. Bram says pie does it.
[IVO looks at the sleeves for a long time. His hand goes to his notebook, and stops.]
[IVO looks at Bram.]
IVO: Does he.
BRAM: It's a well-known fact about pie.
IVO: You would make a fair magistrate, Hollis.
BRAM: Take that back.
IVO: I will not. It was the kindest thing I have said all week.
BRAM: Then I'd hate to hear the rest of your week.
WREN: Do you like the pie? Is it a good pie? I blew on it for you.
IVO: It is adequate.
BRAM: Adequate! Say that in the smokehouse. Hester will have you hanged off the slip.
IVO: She would need a council vote. I would abstain.
BRAM: Whole harbor saw it last night, Ivo. Hard to call that fog-madness.
IVO: Fog-madness is very catching. By noon I had forty cases at the customs house.
BRAM: And what did you tell the forty?
IVO: That it was a bad night to be out, and a good night to stay in.
SERA: Bram. Ask him what he saw.
BRAM: He's right there.
SERA: Ask him.
BRAM: Ivo. What did you see?
IVO: Weather.
SERA: Tell him weather doesn't move in a straight line.
BRAM: She says weather doesn't...
IVO: I heard.
[IVO looks past her, at tonight's mark in the last clean course.]
IVO: Your mark is two degrees off.
[Silence. BRAM stops with the spoon halfway to the pie.]
SERA: Bram. Tell him it's two degrees closer than his.
BRAM: I'm not telling him that.
WREN: I'll tell him! I can tell anybody anything! She says it's two degrees closer than yours! What's yours? Have you got a wall too?
[IVO is quiet a moment too long.]
IVO: I have a desk, Wren. It is very flat.
WREN: Can I draw on it?
IVO: No.
WREN: Can I draw a face on it? A smiling one, so it can show you how?
IVO: No.
[WREN puts a finger at each corner of her own mouth and pushes them up.]
WREN: Look. It goes like this. Up at the ends. It doesn't hurt. Bram, show him.
[BRAM grins at Ivo, enormous, with pie in it.]
BRAM: Like that, see. It's free. Even the council could afford one.
IVO: Thank you both. I have seen it done.
WREN: You have to do it, though. Seeing it isn't doing it.
[SERA's spoon stops. She doesn't turn round.]
[Somewhere in the pause, the Keeper has caught Ivo's eye. He turns to them, dry as salt.]
IVO: Keeper. You climb a great many stairs, for a breakwater keeper.
[Choice a: "And you came a long way up, for a report."]
[Choice b: "It's shorter than the walk to the crater."]
[Choices converge.]
IVO: Walking is still legal, Keeper. I have checked.
[BRAM glances between them and says nothing. He cuts the pie.]
[Skip summary: Ivo arrives uninvited, claiming the council wants a report on the tower. Bram gives him the spare bowl. Sera won't speak to him and talks through Bram; when Ivo says her mark is two degrees off, Wren carries her reply. Ivo sees Bram telling Wren that pie made her grow.]

### Scene 3 — What supper is
[Five bowls, one pie, the lantern on the floor between them. Outside the glass the Belt is up, and nobody at the pot looks at it except Sera.]
[SERA eats with her tide book open beside her bowl. Once, she turns to the back page, writes something short, and closes it.]
WREN: Nobody said! Everybody's eating it and nobody said what it is!
BRAM: Right. Fair question. Supper's when everybody's in, and nobody's out. You count heads. If they're all there, you eat.
WREN: So it's a counting! Sera's the best at counting!
SERA: Supper is after the mark. Before it gets cold up here.
BRAM: She means it's when I make her stop.
SERA: I mean what I said.
WREN: Magistrate? What's yours? Is yours flat as well?
IVO: Supper is the hour when a town is indoors with its shutters closed, and cannot see the sky.
IVO: I am greatly in favor of it.
SERA: Bram. Tell him some of us eat at the window.
BRAM: She eats at the window, Ivo. When she eats.
IVO: I am aware of the window.
WREN: Did you all have supper when you were small? Before you were big? Were you ever small?
BRAM: Up here, every night, right where you're sitting. My grandad ate his supper on that step, with his boots off, before he lit the lamp.
BRAM: My mam carried it up. Same stair. She minded the ninth step and all.
BRAM: Then he'd wipe his mouth, light up, and say, "Right. Everybody still out there? Let's bring them in."
WREN: Every night?
BRAM: Every night of his life. Nobody up here ever missed a supper. It wasn't allowed.
WREN: Sera? Were you small?
SERA: Briefly.
SERA: My mother ate standing at the window. Bread in one hand, sextant in the other.
SERA: She said the stars were fixed, and supper wasn't, so you ate fast.
WREN: Magistrate? Did you have a mam with a window?
IVO: I had a ship. Biscuit at the change of watch, eaten standing, facing the weather.
IVO: The cook said a man who sits down at sea deserves what he gets.
WREN: What did he get?
IVO: Usually, wet.
WREN: Keeper? What's yours? Where's your supper?
KEEPER: On the breakwater. Herring out of a paper. On Hester's book.
WREN: Everybody's is different! Is it allowed to be different?
BRAM: It's allowed. It's still supper, so long as you eat it.
WREN: So supper's when everyone's in, and you count them, and it's dark, and nobody looks up except Sera?
BRAM: That's... yes. That's about it, love.
[WREN stacks the empty bowls, one inside the next, very carefully. Four. Ivo is still eating. She looks at Sera.]
WREN: Did you see? Did you count it?
SERA: I counted. Four.
[BRAM waits, as if there is more coming. There isn't. SERA goes back to her bowl.]
[SERA reaches into her coat and puts something down by Bram's knee: a coil of new lamp wick, tied with thread.]
SERA: Chandler had wick. Ten inches. You said yours was short.
BRAM: I said that a fortnight ago.
SERA: Twelve days.
[BRAM turns the wick over in his fingers. For once, he doesn't make a joke.]
BRAM: I'll trim it tonight.
[IVO sets his bowl down, empty. He stands and buttons his coat to the throat.]
IVO: Thank you for supper, Hollis.
BRAM: You weren't here for supper.
IVO: No. I was here for the tower.
[He looks at the wall one more time. Then down at Wren.]
IVO: Wren. Tell the surveyor I saw it.
WREN: Sera! He says he saw it!
[SERA doesn't look up from her tide book.]
SERA: Tell him seeing isn't saying.
WREN: She says seeing isn't saying! Is that true? Are they different? They sound the same!
IVO: They are very different, Wren. That is the whole difficulty.
WREN: That was a bit funny. Was that funny? Did you almost smile?
[IVO takes out the notebook, writes one line, and closes it without looking down.]
IVO: I noted it. Under "almost."
[He goes down the stair, his steps slow and even, and then gone.]
BRAM: He ate all of it. He always eats all of it. Never once said it was good.
SERA: He said thank you.
BRAM: Well. That's nearly good.
[SERA picks up the sextant and sights the Belt. Then she looks at tonight's mark. She rubs it out with her thumb and chalks it again, a little to the left.]
BRAM: Was he right?
SERA: He was two degrees right.
BRAM: I'll not say a word.
SERA: Don't. You shouted on the ninth step.
BRAM: So it's my fault?
SERA: It's two degrees your fault.
[BRAM laughs. Then he stops, and watches Wren drift toward the glass in the big coat.]
SERA: Three rolls, Bram.
BRAM: I know.
SERA: It's after supper. I said I'd take her height.
BRAM: Not tonight.
SERA: It's a minute.
BRAM: Not tonight, Sera. Let her have one night that's only supper.
[SERA looks at the chalk in her hand. She puts it in her pocket.]
SERA: Tomorrow.
BRAM: Tomorrow.
[WREN has gone to the glass. She looks up at the Belt, a moment too long, then turns back to the room.]
WREN: Bram? Is supper every night? Even when the sky's moving about?
BRAM: Every single night, love.
WREN: Good. Then I'll be here.
[Skip summary: Over supper, each of them tells Wren what supper is. Sera gives Bram a new lamp wick. Ivo leaves after telling Wren to tell Sera he saw it; Sera answers that seeing isn't saying. Then she quietly corrects her mark by two degrees. Wren decides that supper means she'll be here.]
