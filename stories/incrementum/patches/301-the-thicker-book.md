## Planning sheet (gacha 14.2)

**Player fantasy:** you sat in a back room by candlelight while a man you'd watched kneel at the crater slid the book he'd hidden for years across the table, and then you climbed the stair behind the one person who has to carry it up.

**Featured heroes: want / obstacle / cost**
- **Ivo** wants to read the black book to the council before the town drowns believing a word he gave it. Obstacle: the one person it is owed to won't hear his name, and the town will hear it at the worst time. Cost: he shows Bram everything except one night, says "I do not know" out loud to Ada Marr, refuses to say sorry to the wrong person ("You are not the one it is owed to"), and gives away his last day of choosing to Bram. He doesn't write Wren's joke down; he notes it "under 'later.'"
- **Bram** wants to be the one who tells Sera, straight, from the stair and not from a bench. Obstacle: she isn't speaking to him, the shift comes when he starts, and the second time she won't have Ivo's name in the room. Cost: he asks for the day and spends twelve hours of it failing; Sera can see he's "carrying something again"; he leaves his lantern by Wren and goes down without it. He has written his first line in the light's book, and Sera copied it.
- **Sera** (scene 1) wants the harbor's number back. Obstacle: it was kept for her for six days, in order, and she didn't read it. Cost: she finds Nell knew her tide before she did; she writes her first question mark; from now on the log comes up first, every morning. At the shift she starts to measure Wren's memory and stops.
- **Wren** (scene 14) wants the itch to be the going-somewhere kind that comes back. Obstacle: it steps late and big. Cost: she grows to look Sera in the eye, keeps a faint glow in the dark, and remembers a cold that ticked.
- **Nell** (bench) wants Bram on *Patience*, south, with a reason handed to him. **Hester** (bench) wants to hear nothing she'd have to repeat. **Jory** (bench) wants the light's book honest, and gets thanked by the man who signed it.

## Script

### Scene 1 — The breakwater log
[Morning, day 24. The lamp room in grey early light. The chalk wall is full, ceiling to floor. On the back of SERA's hand, a faded "20".]
[WREN sleeps on the cradle step under Sera's surveyor's coat, her bare ankles out of it. The Keeper comes up the last turn of the stair with the breakwater log under one arm, damp at the corners.]
[Objective: Bring Sera the breakwater log.]
SERA: Keeper. That's the log.
KEEPER: Back from the customs house. Signed.
SERA: Put it on the step. Open. Not by the wall.
[The Keeper lays it open on the cradle step. SERA kneels. She turns to the stub first and runs her thumb down the stitching, the way Jory showed her.]
SERA: "One leaf wanting. Witnessed, I. Thane, chair of council." And the date.
SERA: He's a good hand for what's missing.
KEEPER: Jory asked him to sign it.
SERA: I know who asked. I'm reading who signed.
[She turns the leaf. Her finger goes to the first line after the stub.]
SERA: Four o'clock, the bar night. "Lamp trimmed." I've read that one.
SERA: Then the next night. Three o'clock. "Gauge: two hours forty-five ahead." Your hand.
SERA: Next. "Two fifty." Your hand.
SERA: Next. The night Wren burned Tam. "Three hours five."
SERA: Fifteen minutes in one night. The night before, five.
KEEPER: The sky moved that night.
SERA: I know what moved that night. I'm not saying why. I'm saying fifteen.
[She takes out her tide book, opens it near the front, past the folded leaf, and starts to copy. Small. Exact.]
SERA: Twenty-first. Dusk as well. You started dusk when I asked.
SERA: "Dusk. Water in at the stick. Three hours ten ahead."
SERA: The table said nine. It came in while Hester was ladling.
SERA: Twenty-second. Three o'clock, three fifteen. Dusk, three thirty.
SERA: That's the night the wall filled. Fifteen again. Across the shift.
SERA: Twenty-third. Three thirty-five. Dusk, three forty.
SERA: This morning.
KEEPER: Three fifty.
SERA: I can read it. You don't have to say it.
SERA: You say things now, Keeper. I've noticed.
[She doesn't look up. She keeps copying.]
SERA: You wrote the heights as well.
KEEPER: Feet on the stick.
SERA: Six foot one, the eighteenth. Six four. Six seven. Seven foot. Seven two.
SERA: This morning, seven foot four.
SERA: It's not only early. It's higher. Every three o'clock, higher.
SERA: Springs are tomorrow.
KEEPER: Springs?
SERA: Spring tide. Biggest of the month. Every child on the slip can tell you that before their letters.
SERA: On my table, tomorrow's high water is in the evening. A big one.
SERA: Add three fifty. Add whatever tonight adds.
[She writes the sum in the book. At the end of it she writes something she has never written in that book before: a question mark.]
KEEPER: What does it come to?
SERA: Not evening.
SERA: I'll know closer after tonight's three o'clock. Bring it.
[She counts down the column with the end of her pencil.]
SERA: Nine readings since the leaf. Your hand, every one. In order. Not a line missed.
SERA: I asked for a week. "I'll read the week," I said. Standing right there.
SERA: That was six days ago.
KEEPER: You had the wall.
SERA: I had the wall. You had the tide.
SERA: Only one of us was keeping the harbor's number. It wasn't me.
SERA: Nell's read this. Jory read it to her over a net. Nell knew three forty before I did.
SERA: Nell Garrow knew my own tide before I did.
[She goes back to the column, and stops. Under this morning's reading there is one more line, in big square capitals that lean like a fence in a wind.]
[It reads: SLIP. WATER ON THE FOURTH STEP DOWN. B.]
SERA: That's not your hand.
KEEPER: No.
SERA: Whose?
[Choice a: "You know whose."]
[Choice b: "Bram's."]
[Choices converge.]
SERA: He doesn't write.
KEEPER: He wrote that. At three. I was half asleep at the gauge.
SERA: Fourth step down.
[A long pause. She copies it into her tide book under the keeper's figure, letter for letter, capitals and all.]
SERA: Fourth step down's a foot over the slip ring.
SERA: He's been looking at the right step.
[Under the coat, WREN stirs and sits up. Her hair is flat on one side. She squints at the log.]
WREN: Is that Bram's writing? It looks like a fence fell over.
SERA: It's a reading.
WREN: Bram did a reading? In the light's book? With a pencil?
WREN: Did he do it for you? He did, didn't he.
SERA: He did it in the light's book.
WREN: Everything in the light's book's for you. You said so. On the stair. Twice.
[SERA doesn't answer. She closes the tide book over her thumb, keeping the place.]
SERA: Keeper. From today this book comes up first. Every morning.
SERA: Before the wall. Before I sight. Before anything.
KEEPER: And the count?
SERA: Hands. Still hands. I'm looking for stone.
WREN: Where are you looking?
SERA: Everywhere I'm not.
WREN: Is Bram coming up tonight? Even now?
SERA: He comes every night.
WREN: Even when you're not talking?
SERA: Every night.
WREN: That's a lot of every.
WREN: Is that your coat on me? It's your coat. You've only got your jumper.
SERA: I wasn't cold.
WREN: You're always cold. You've got chalk on your cuffs and your nose goes white.
WREN: My feet were out again. Were my feet out? They're always out now.
SERA: They were out.
WREN: Bram says he'll bring a blanket. He says it every night. Then he brings soup.
WREN: Soup's warmer than a blanket, though. On the inside.
[Skip summary: On the morning of day 24, the keeper brings the breakwater log up the tower, signed by Ivo as a page short. For the first time Sera reads every gauge reading since the leaf, all in the keeper's hand: the tide has gone from two hours forty-five ahead to three hours fifty, and higher every night, with the spring tide due tomorrow. Under this morning's reading is one line in Bram's hand. Sera copies it into her tide book without a word, and says the log comes up first every morning from now on.]

### Scene 2 — The net-loft stair
[Noon. The bottom of the net-loft stair, where two hand-sized scorch marks still darken the steps. The boats are all in. Nets hang to dry along every rail.]
[BRAM carries a heap of net down the stair for Jory. WREN sits on the bottom step, scratching the inside of her arm through the short sleeve.]
[Objective: Find Bram before the afternoon watch.]
BRAM: Keeper! Mind the bottom steps. Hester won't have them scrubbed.
BRAM: Says the harbor ought to have to look at them. She's not wrong. She's never wrong. It's a terrible way to live.
WREN: They're my hands. Tam's hands, and mine on top of his. Look.

BRAM: Walls are different. Walls have opinions.

KEEPER: Every line. She copied it.
BRAM: ...The bottom bit as well?
[Choice a: "Letter for letter."]
[Choice b: "She said you'd been looking at the right step."]

BRAM: Did she.

BRAM: First thing I've written down since I put my name to the net-loft rent. And that was mostly the B.
BRAM: Don't make a thing of it.
KEEPER: She didn't.
BRAM: No. She wouldn't. Not to me.
BRAM: She copied it, though. Into her book.
KEEPER: Capitals and all.
BRAM: I do a big B. My mam taught me. "Make it big, Bram, so they know you were there."
BRAM: Right. Right. Good.
[He folds the same corner again.]
WREN: Bram. My arms are doing it.
BRAM: Doing what, Sparrow?
WREN: The itch. The going-somewhere one.
[BRAM stops folding. He goes quiet.]
BRAM: When?
WREN: Not now. It started at the noon bell. It's further off than last time.
WREN: Like it's taking the long way round the harbor.
WREN: Sera'll say dusk. Two days since the last one makes dusk. She's very good at twos.
WREN: I don't think it's dusk. I think it's later. Is later a time?
BRAM: Later's a time. It's the worst one. Ask anybody on a quay waiting for a boat.
BRAM: Up the tower by four, then. In the room. With Sera. Where I can—
WREN: —see me. I know. You can see me from everywhere. I'm very long now.
WREN: Can I take Tam his net back first? He likes to look at it. He says it's like visiting.
BRAM: Jory's net. Tam's looking. Nobody's touching.
WREN: Jory mends like a man kicking a door. Tam says.
BRAM: Tam says a lot of things for a lad with no hands.
WREN: He's got hands. They're just in Hester's rags. They itch too! Tam itches and I itch.
WREN: Hester says itching means mending. What am I mending?
[BRAM has no joke for that. He looks at her arm instead, where her sleeve stops short of her wrist.]
BRAM: You're mending my temper, Sparrow. Slowly.
WREN: That's not a real answer.
BRAM: It's the one I've got before dinner.
WREN: I've been saving a joke for Ivo. It's the best one yet. It's about paper.
BRAM: Don't tell me. I'll laugh in the wrong place, and you'll have to start again.
WREN: You always laugh in the wrong place. That's how I know it's Bram.

BRAM: Keeper. You're on the breakwater this afternoon?
KEEPER: Till Jory takes the lamp.
BRAM: Then I'll see you at Hester's at five. Supper's at five now. Like my nan.
BRAM: And Keeper. Thank you for not making a thing of it.
BRAM: Sparrow. Tower. Four.
WREN: Four! I'll count the stair so you know where I am. I'll count it right this time.
BRAM: You never count it right.
WREN: Then you'll always know it's me.

### Scene 3 — The breakwater, afternoon
[The breakwater in flat afternoon light. The water is already high against the stones, far higher than any table in Saltreach says it should be. The painted gauge post stands wet nearly to its top marks.]
[JORY sits on the step of the lamp housing with Tam's net across his knees, mending it badly. The breakwater log lies beside him.]
[Objective: Keep the afternoon watch.]
JORY: Keeper. Book's back. She read it?
KEEPER: All of it.
JORY: Good. Not my business. Good.
JORY: Hollis wrote in it. Big as a sign over a shop. Did you see?
KEEPER: I saw.
JORY: First new hand in that book but yours.

JORY: Not saying it wasn't.
IVO: Nor was I.

IVO: Every line.
KEEPER: She copied it. She'll read it every morning now.

KEEPER: She said you've a good hand for what's missing.

KEEPER: What is it?

KEEPER: Hester's at five. The tower at nine.

KEEPER: He walks home. Or he sits on the stair.
IVO: Then tonight he will sit in Mrs Pike's back room instead. At ten.

KEEPER: Why me?
IVO: You watched me kneel at the crater, with no lantern, nine nights after the fall.

IVO: Ask me at ten.

IVO: Seven foot four at three in the morning, Keeper. I used to think a town could sleep through anything if you shut the door for it.
IVO: Ten o'clock. Not a quarter past.

### Scene 4 — The smokehouse, five o'clock
[Hester's smokehouse at five, in daylight. Every bench full and eating fast. HESTER goes down the benches with a ladle.]
[TAM sits by the hearth with his bandaged hands held up off his knees, rubbing them against each other through the linen. BRAM is at the side table, where a lidded pot has SERA chalked on it.]
[Objective: Stop at Hester's before dusk.]
HESTER: Keeper. In or out. My door's for walking through, not leaning on.
KEEPER: In.
HESTER: Sit. Herring, bread. You've the look of somebody who's been talked at by a magistrate.
KEEPER: How can you tell?
HESTER: You've gone stiff at the collar. He does that to people. Like starch.
TAM: Keeper! Look. Look at my hands.
KEEPER: How are they?
TAM: Itching. Something terrible. Hester says itching's mending.
TAM: I've itched since yesterday, so I must be nearly a net.
HESTER: You're nearly a nuisance, you daft sprat. Stop rubbing them.
TAM: I'm not rubbing. I'm visiting them.

HESTER: Pie's for people who are speaking. Fish keeps its own counsel. It'll suit her.
BRAM: That's cruel.
HESTER: It's cheaper. Same thing, this month.

HESTER: And your magistrate's had my back room off me for ten o'clock. Paid in coin.

BRAM: Thank you, Tam. That's very helpful.
TAM: I'm helping with my elbows. Jory says it's all I'm good for.

HESTER: Shut that.

BRAM: It's only weather.
HESTER: Don't you say weather in my smokehouse. That word's been on a door.

HESTER: Nell Garrow's been in three times asking for you, Hollis.

HESTER: She didn't say. Nell never says. She slaps my table and goes out again.
HESTER: Three slaps. That's a big want, for Nell.
BRAM: I'll find her tomorrow.
HESTER: She'll find you first. She always does. That woman could find a herring in a snowstorm.
TAM: Bram. Tell Wren her net's safe. Jory hasn't touched the corner she tied.
BRAM: She'll be glad. She's gone up the tower. She's itching.

TAM: Same as before the stair?
BRAM: Same as.
TAM: Then tell her I'm not cross. Still. Tell her twice, in case once has worn off.

HESTER: Left pocket. Not the other one.
BRAM: There's nothing in the other one now, Hester.
HESTER: Keep it that way.
HESTER: Go on. Walk your road. Pot's here for you at nine. And you're back here at ten.

### Scene 5 — The lanes
[Boss stage.]
[The harbor lanes at dusk. Shutters closed all the way up the hill, lamps out under the ration. The haze lies in the lanes waist-deep and white, and moves uphill against the wind.]
[BRAM walks with the storm lantern lit. The Keeper walks beside him.]
[Objective: Get up the hill to the crater road.]
BRAM: Lanes are quiet. Everybody's in. That's the ration doing its job.
BRAM: I hate it when Ivo's sums work. It's like being beaten at cards by a clock.

ADA: Hollis! Hollis, there's one in my yard!
BRAM: One what, Ada?
ADA: One of them! The tall ones! It walked through my washing!
BRAM: Walked?
ADA: Walked! Through my sheets! Didn't stop! Went straight up the lane like it was late for church!

BRAM: Sera's on the headland. Sparrow's on the headland.

BRAM: It went round me! It went round my lamp like it was a puddle!

BRAM: That's worse. Ivo, tell me that's worse.
IVO: It is different. I will tell you whether it is worse when I have counted it.

ADA: Pip! Pip, get in! Don't look up after supper!
BRAM: He's not looking up, Ada, it's at his window! Lantern! Keeper, with me!

BRAM: Window shut, Ada! Shut, and sing him something!
ADA: I don't know anything to sing!
BRAM: Sing the tide tables! He'll be asleep in a minute! I was!

IVO: The tall one. The others keep pace with it. Keeper, that one.
IVO: Not in front. At the turn of the lane, as it passes the pump. It has to go straight past the pump.

BRAM: They all felt that! Every one!
IVO: Again.
[The Keeper strikes again. The tall one breaks. At the top of the hill the haze thins to nothing over the last roofs, and every Walker still standing walks straight up out of the lanes, into the thinning haze, and is not there anymore.]

BRAM: Gone up.
IVO: Gone up where the white ended. Not where they were going.
BRAM: How do you know where they were going?
IVO: I do not. I know which way they were facing.

### Scene 6 — Ada Marr's yard
[A small yard halfway up the hill. A washing line hangs broken across it. ADA MARR stands in her doorway with the baby, and PIP peers round her skirts. IVO picks a wet sheet up off the cobbles and folds it, corners together, very exact.]

### Scene 7 — The lamp room, nine

### Scene 8 — The black book
[Hester's smokehouse after ten. The lamps are out, the benches empty, and the banked fires make the long room red. HESTER sits at the hearth with her iron fire-hook across her knees.]
[A door at the back stands ajar on a narrow room: a cot made up square, a washstand, a hat on a peg, and nothing else of the lodger's on show. A single candle on a small table. IVO sits behind it, gloved. The black book lies closed in front of him.]
[Objective: Hear what Ivo wants.]
HESTER: In. Both of you. Lisle's out walking. Don't touch his hat.
BRAM: Why would anybody touch a hat?
HESTER: I don't know. That's why I'm saying.
[She looks at Ivo through the door. She wipes her hands on her apron, twice.]
HESTER: I'm at my fires, Magistrate. Fires are loud.
IVO: Thank you, Mrs Pike.
HESTER: Don't thank me. You've paid. Thank people who haven't.
[She pulls the door to. It doesn't quite shut.]
IVO: Sit, Hollis. Keeper.
BRAM: If this is about the leaf, I've been fined by better. My mam, mostly.
IVO: It is not about your leaf.
[He puts two gloved fingers on the black book and slides it across the table to Bram.]
IVO: Open it. Anywhere.
[BRAM opens it near the middle. Columns, in a small, hard, even hand. Dates. Bearings. A star's name at the top of a column. Another. Times.]
BRAM: That's numbers.
IVO: That is the sky. Every clear night.
BRAM: Whose?
IVO: Mine.
[BRAM turns to the front. The ink there is brown, faded nearly to tea.]
BRAM: This is old.
IVO: I came ashore, Hollis. I did not stop being a navigator. A navigator takes a sight every clear night, or he forgets how.
IVO: The Belt. The Nail. The harbor tide, against the surveyor's mother's tables. Every clear night since I came ashore.
BRAM: You never said.
IVO: No.
BRAM: Twenty years I've known you. You never once said you looked up.
IVO: A magistrate who looks up is a man the council has to watch. It was easier to be watched looking down.
KEEPER: The crater. The ninth night. What were you doing?
IVO: You may ask tonight. I said so.
IVO: Taking a sight, Keeper. Over the place where it fell. With my hand on the glass, to know it was real.
IVO: It went in here that night. With the rest. A line, like any other night.
BRAM: A line. A girl fell out of the sky and you wrote a line.
IVO: It is what I had, Hollis. You had a coat.
[He reaches across, turns the leaves forward, and stops on one. He lays a gloved finger under a line.]
IVO: Here. The Belt. A tenth of a finger east of where it has been for three hundred years.
IVO: I wrote "error, mine."
[He turns one leaf.]
IVO: The next clear night, the same. I struck out "mine."
BRAM: When's this?
IVO: Two weeks before the surveyor stood up in the customs house with the same figure.
[BRAM looks at the date. He doesn't say anything for a while.]
BRAM: She stood up in front of the whole council. With her mother's sextant and her chalk on her cuffs.
BRAM: And you'd had it two weeks. In this.
IVO: Yes.
BRAM: And they laughed at her. Half the benches laughed.
IVO: Not all of them laughed. Some of them were frightened. I gave the room a word so that they would not be.
BRAM: Haze. You called it haze. To her face.
IVO: I gave it the council's word. In session. With her standing there.
BRAM: You let her be the mad one.
IVO: Yes.
[BRAM's hand goes to the right-hand pocket of his oilskin. It's empty. His hand stays there anyway.]
[IVO watches the hand. He says nothing about it.]
IVO: Turn back, Hollis. Further. To last year.
[BRAM turns back. The tide column runs down the page, night after night.]
BRAM: The herring year.
IVO: The bad year. Read the tide.
BRAM: I can't read your hand, Ivo. It's like a row of nails.
IVO: Then I will read it. Here, two minutes early. Here, three. Here, two. Here, four.
IVO: Small. Too small for any table to blush at. Before anything I could see in the sky had moved at all.
BRAM: The tide was early. Last year. Before.
IVO: The tide was early. And the herring were not where the tables said they would be.
IVO: I wrote it down. I did not know what I was writing down. I know now.
[BRAM turns another leaf. IVO's gloved hand comes down flat on the page, over a date, and stays there.]
IVO: Not that night.
BRAM: What's that night?
IVO: A night that belongs to someone else. They will hear it read once, aloud, with the rest. Not in a back room first.
BRAM: Who's it belong to?
IVO: Not that night, Hollis.
[He doesn't move his hand. BRAM looks at it a long moment, and lets go of the page.]
[Skip summary: In Hester's back room after ten, Ivo slides a thick black book across to Bram: years of sky and tide sights, taken every clear night since he came ashore. ### Scene 9 — Nell
[The smokehouse door bangs. Boots on the boards. HESTER's voice, sharp.]
HESTER: Garrow! It's private!
NELL: Nothing's private after nine, Pike. There's a ration. Where is he?
[NELL GARROW pushes the back-room door open with one hand. IVO closes the black book under his glove, unhurried, and leaves his hand on it.]

NELL: Four, now.
[She slaps the nearest flat thing, which is the black book under Ivo's hand. She doesn't look at it.]
NELL: Big Coll's done his knee on the slip. He's out a month. I'm a hand short.
NELL: I want you on *Patience*. South.
BRAM: South.
NELL: South. You've hauled for every boat in this harbor but mine. Come and haul for one that's leaving.

NELL: That's not cruel, Hollis. That's a reason. I'm handing you a reason. Take it.

NELL: Magistrate. Didn't see you there. You were being furniture.

NELL: Council's every week. Council's never once been a reason for anything.

NELL: You're a strange pair for a back room. Lisle'll want rent off you.
NELL: After tomorrow, then. And tomorrow's a long time for a boat, Hollis. Think on it.
NELL: Done.

NELL: Put his supper on my slate, Pike.
HESTER: He's had his supper.
NELL: Then put tomorrow's.

BRAM: She'd have read that book upside down if you'd let go of it.
IVO: Skipper Garrow does not read upside down. She reads the sea. It has been a poor year for it.

### Scene 10 — One day
[The back room. The candle has burned down a finger. IVO opens the black book again, flat, and turns it so it faces Bram.]
IVO: Tomorrow the council sits. I will read this to it. In session. Into the minutes.
IVO: All of it. In red.
BRAM: Red.
IVO: Red is for things we are sure of. I have been sure of these for a long while.
BRAM: Why now? You've sat on it a year. More. Why tomorrow?
IVO: The surveyor read the breakwater log this morning. Every three o'clock since the bar, in the keeper's hand. And yours.
IVO: So the town has one honest book of the tide again, and it is hers.
IVO: The springs are tomorrow. The water is at seven foot four at three in the morning, and things are walking in the lanes.
IVO: And I find I have run out of weather.
BRAM: You always said a frightened town drowns faster than a fooled one.
IVO: I did. I was right, for a year. It is in here.
IVO: The fooled one is now drowning at very nearly the same rate. That is in here also.
[BRAM looks at the open book. He doesn't touch it.]
BRAM: Why tell me? Why not just read it tomorrow and let me find out with everybody?
IVO: You forgave me the light, Hollis. I signed the order that put it out, and you forgave me. I never saw when.
IVO: I have never understood it. I wished to see whether it was a habit.
BRAM: It wasn't a habit. It was a lamp.
BRAM: This isn't a lamp, Ivo. This is her.
IVO: Yes.
BRAM: Say you're sorry, at least. To me. In a back room. Nobody'd hear.
[A long pause. The fire spits beyond the door.]
IVO: Not to you, Hollis. You are not the one it is owed to.
[BRAM looks at him. Then he nods, slowly, once.]
BRAM: Then don't do it from a bench.
IVO: Do what?
BRAM: Tell her. Don't let her hear this read out in that room. Not her. Not that room.
BRAM: She stood up in that room once with her numbers, and they laughed, and you gave them the word to laugh with.
BRAM: She'll not hear this in the same room, from the same chair, with Gage sat there picking his teeth.
IVO: She will hear it. The whole town will hear it. That is the point of it.
BRAM: I know. Let her hear it first from somebody who brings her supper.
BRAM: Give me a day. One day. I'll tell her myself.
IVO: You.
BRAM: Me. Straight. Not sideways. I've done sideways. I know what it costs now.
[IVO looks at him for a long moment. His lips move, without sound.]
IVO: The council sits tomorrow afternoon. Until the chair calls the first item, it is yours.
IVO: That is about fifteen hours. I have counted.
BRAM: Course you have.
IVO: You will sleep eight of them.
BRAM: I'll not sleep.
IVO: No. Nor shall I.
IVO: If you have not told her by the first item, Hollis, I read it anyway. I will not wait for a better day. There are no better days in here.
[He lays his gloved hand on the book.]
BRAM: I'll tell her.
IVO: I believe you intend to.
BRAM: And after you've read it? What then?
IVO: Then the room will do what frightened rooms do, Hollis. I have watched one do it before. In a port that is not a port now.
IVO: Some will shout. Some will go home and pack. Some will look at the surveyor.
BRAM: And you?
IVO: I will be standing in the middle of it, holding the book. That is the arrangement.
[BRAM stands. At the door he stops, and turns back, and takes a breath.]
BRAM: Sparrow gave me a joke for you. Made me promise.
IVO: Then you had better tell it.
BRAM: What's flat, and lies still, and doesn't run when it gets wet?
IVO: Paper.
BRAM: ...She said you'd guess. She said that's the joke. That you'd guess, because it's you.
[IVO looks at the open book in front of him. He doesn't take out his plain notebook. He doesn't write anything.]
IVO: Tell her I noted it.
BRAM: Under "almost"?
IVO: Under "later."
[Skip summary: Ivo tells Bram he will read the black book into the minutes at tomorrow's council session, because Sera has her tide back and he has "run out of weather." Asked to say he's sorry, Ivo says Bram isn't the one it's owed to. ### Scene 11 — The fires
[The long room of the smokehouse, red from the banked fires. IVO has gone out the back way, without a lantern. HESTER sits at the hearth. BRAM stands in the middle of the room with his lantern unlit.]
[Objective: Get back up the hill before late.]
HESTER: I heard nothing.
BRAM: Hester.
HESTER: Fires are loud. I said so. I'll go on hearing nothing till somebody tells me something I'm allowed.
HESTER: Sit. Two minutes. You've the face of a man who's been handed a fish he didn't catch.
[BRAM sits on the end of a bench. HESTER puts a cup in his hands. It's hot.]
BRAM: Garrow wants me on *Patience*.
HESTER: I know. Four slaps. I counted.
BRAM: She says nothing's keeping me.
HESTER: Nell says a lot of things on her way out of doors. Half of them she's saying to herself.
HESTER: Drink that.
[She wipes her hands on her apron, twice.]
HESTER: Is it a debt? What he showed you?
BRAM: It's not mine.
HESTER: Then it's not on my slate. I don't write secrets down. Only debts.
HESTER: Whose is it?
BRAM: Tomorrow it's everybody's.
HESTER: Then I'll hear it tomorrow, with everybody, and make a face like I never knew there was a candle in my back room.
HESTER: I'm very good at faces. I've had the practice. Everybody lies in a smokehouse.
BRAM: Is that why they all tell you things?
HESTER: They tell me things because I feed them first. A full man's an honest one, mostly. Mostly's what you get.
[She looks at him over the fire.]
HESTER: Does it touch her? Sera?
[BRAM doesn't answer. That's an answer.]
HESTER: Then you go up that hill and you tell her before you eat a mouthful. Or you'll not eat for a week.
BRAM: I've been told off for not telling her. Now I'm told off for telling her.
HESTER: You're told off for telling her late. It's a whole different crime, you great cod.
BRAM: She's not speaking to me.
HESTER: She ate your supper cold last night. Ate the lot. Bowl came down clean.
BRAM: How'd you know that?
HESTER: Bowls come back to me, Hollis. Every bowl in this harbor comes back to me in the end. I read them like Vant reads a tide.
HESTER: Clean bowl's not nothing. Clean bowl's a woman who's still eating what you bring.
[BRAM drinks. He puts the cup down.]
BRAM: Garrow's put my supper on her slate.
HESTER: She has. First time Nell Garrow's paid for anybody in thirty years. She wants you on that boat badly.
BRAM: And you'd let me go?
HESTER: I never ask anybody to stay. You know that. Not Nell. Not you. Not the herring.
HESTER: I only ask them to come back for their pots.
HESTER: How's the long girl? Tam says she's itching again.
BRAM: Since noon. She says it's coming late.
HESTER: Then go and be there late. That's what late's for.
HESTER: And tell her I've a bigger pot for her name. The old one's too short. Like her coat.
[She takes the lantern out of his hands, lights it from the fire with a spill, and hands it back.]
HESTER: Fires aren't lamps. But they'll light one.
HESTER: Go on. Up. Keeper, mind he doesn't stop on the ninth step and talk to it.

Nell has put Bram's supper on her slate, the first time she's paid for anyone. Hester lights his lantern from the fire.]

### Scene 12 — The headland path
[Half past eleven. The headland path climbs above the dark harbor toward the old tower. The haze lies along the headland, white and low, and moves uphill against the wind.]
[BRAM walks with the lantern up. The Keeper beside him.]
[Objective: Reach the tower before the shift.]
BRAM: Right. Practicing. You're Sera. Stand there and look at the sky like I'm not here.
KEEPER: That's easy.
BRAM: It is, isn't it. Right.
BRAM: "Sera. So. Funny thing about Ivo—"
BRAM: No. No funny thing. That's sideways. That's the whole trouble.
BRAM: "Sera. Ivo's got a book."
BRAM: No. She'll say, "What kind?" and I'll say "Black," and she'll say "That's a colour, not a kind."

KEEPER: I'd say what he showed you.
BRAM: What he showed me. Aye. A book of numbers in a hand like a row of nails.

BRAM: So I can't show her. I can only say. I'm terrible at only saying.
BRAM: "Sera. Ivo knew."
[He stops walking.]
BRAM: That's it, isn't it. That's straight. Three words.
BRAM: I can lift a net full of water. I can't lift three words up a hill.

BRAM: Oh, not now. Not now, you long lot.

### Scene 13 — Before midnight
[The lamp room near midnight, dark but for the stars. SERA stands at the glass with the sextant up. WREN walks up and down by the wall, rubbing both arms, faster than she walks the stair.]
[BRAM comes up the last turn with the lantern, breathing hard. The Keeper behind him.]
BRAM: Everyone still here?
WREN: Bram! It's close now! It's ever so close! It's on the stair!
BRAM: What's on the stair, Sparrow?
WREN: Not the stair. I mean it's near. Like when you're on the ninth step and you know the tenth.
SERA: Keeper. Arm. Sight it with me.

SERA: Twenty. Still twenty.
WREN: Not for long! Not for very long!
WREN: Bram, the tall ones came up the headland. The walking ones. I watched from the glass.
WREN: They walked right up to our door. Then they went up. Like they came to visit and changed their minds.
WREN: Is that rude? It felt rude. I'd have let them in.
BRAM: You'd let a gale in, Sparrow, if it knocked.
SERA: Keeper. Four on the headland path. I counted them from here. One broke. Three went off the edge.
KEEPER: Off the edge and up.
SERA: Up. I saw.

### Scene 14 — The sixth shift
[Midnight. The lamp room. SERA at the glass with the sextant raised. The Keeper beside her, arm straight. BRAM holds the storm lantern high by the cradle, and WREN stands inside its light.]
[Out over the headland, the Belt. Three stars.]
SERA: Twenty.
[WREN's shard glows at her throat. Then her hands. Then all of her: gold, then whiter than gold, so bright that the lantern's light looks brown beside it.]
WREN: Bram. Bram, I'm doing it.
BRAM: I know, love. I've got you. You're in the light.
WREN: I'm not hurting anybody? Is anybody touching me?
BRAM: Nobody's touching you, Sparrow. Nobody's near. Tam's at home with his elbows.
[The Belt begins to slide.]
SERA: Nineteen.
WREN: It's not like the window. It's not the floor going.

SERA: Eighteen.

BRAM: It's you. Stay in the light. That's all. That's the whole job.

BRAM: It's a big one, Sparrow. Biggest yet. You'll be able to reach the top shelf at Hester's.
WREN: She keeps the sugar there!
BRAM: Then that's settled. Something good's come of it.

SERA: Sixteen. Mark it.

### Scene 16 — The ninth step
[The spiral stair, about two in the morning. BRAM sits on the ninth step, the one that creaks, with his cap on his knee and no lantern. The faint glow from the lamp room comes round the turn above, no more than moonlight.]
[The Keeper comes down and sits two steps above him.]
BRAM: Two goes.
BRAM: The sky got the first one. His name got the second.
BRAM: Funny thing about a day, Keeper. It's shorter from this end.
KEEPER: How long?
BRAM: Fifteen hours, he said. That was at ten.
BRAM: Twelve now. Near enough. She'd say to the minute.
BRAM: Eleven and three quarters. There. I can do it too.
[He turns the cap over in his hands.]
BRAM: She said "daylight." She said "where I can see his face."
BRAM: She meant mine. Didn't she. She meant she'd see it on my face.
KEEPER: She can see it now.
BRAM: Aye. She said. "Carrying something again."
BRAM: Again. That's the word that'll keep me up. Not "Ivo." "Again."

BRAM: She counts in her sleep now. Did you hear? Counting stairs she's not on.
BRAM: She's level with Sera now. Eye to eye. Did you see Sera's face, when she turned round?
BRAM: Like she'd looked across the quay and seen somebody she ought to know.
BRAM: She remembered the cold. From up there. A cold that ticks.
BRAM: I found her in the glass and wrapped her in my coat because she was cold, Keeper. That first night.
BRAM: I thought it was the marsh she was cold from.

BRAM: I put it down. I don't say "eat first."
BRAM: I say three words. Straight. Before the bread's cut.
BRAM: Then I hold the lamp, and she does whatever she does. And I don't stop her.
KEEPER: And Ivo?
BRAM: Ivo reads it to the council whatever I do. He said. He'll not wait for a better day.
BRAM: There aren't any in his book. He said that too.
[He stands up. The ninth step creaks under him, loudly, the way it always has.]
BRAM: There. That's the step telling me off. Four generations it's been telling Hollises off.
BRAM: Go and sleep, Keeper. You've a three o'clock.
BRAM: Write it big. She reads it first now.
BRAM: And if I'm on the slip, I'll come out and write mine under it. Fourth step, fifth step. Whatever the water says.
[He goes down into the dark without a lantern, the way Ivo does, and the Keeper hears him all the way to the bottom, and out of the door, and onto the path.]
[Objective: Return at first light.]
[Skip summary: About two in the morning, Bram sits on the ninth step and counts down the hours Ivo gave him: twelve left, near enough. He's failed twice to tell Sera, and she can see he's carrying something "again." He plans to take her bread at first light and say three words straight. He tells the keeper to write the three o'clock big, since Sera reads it first now, and goes down into the dark without a lantern.]
