**Featured heroes: want / obstacle / cost**
- **Sera** wants the number Ivo stole from her: how long he knew, and what it bought. Obstacle: she's been right since the first night, and now that the whole town has heard it, being right weighs nothing. Cost: she speaks to him directly for the first time since the haze, hears "I'm sorry" and tells him it isn't a number. She learns his two-degree correction at the first supper was made against the hidden ledger. She won't take his book ("Then it can lie"), and he sends her the fair copy anyway. - **Ivo** wants the ledger entered as council property and the town kept standing without his chair. Obstacle: three seats walk out, the crowd won't come in when he asks, and Sera finally speaks to him. Cost: he says "I'm sorry" for the first time, to the one person it can't help, and finds out who it's for. He writes "withheld" into the council's own minute book. On the steps at dawn he admits he's surprised by none of it.
- **Bram** wants everyone home and no one asking him to choose. Obstacle: Sera knows he had the ledger a day, and the harbor wants him on a side. Cost: he won't go into the customs house while she's in it, then says to both, separately, that he isn't picking ("Nobody ever asks. I'm saying it anyway."). - **Wren** (Brightening) wants to know whether the crater is still where she came from if they fill it. She itches before the shift, lights the tower brighter than ever, and afterward can see in the dark.
- **Abel** (bench, rate-up) wants the crater filled before it takes something else. He leads the crowd out at dawn, and in the afternoon says the plain half: "A hole's a thing I can fill." It isn't filled.
- **Teodor** (bench, rate-up) mildly offers to buy every piece of glass and take it south. Ivo isn't surprised by any of it.

## Script

### Scene 1 — The customs house, about nine
[Night of day 25, nine o'clock. The customs house, hours after the spring tide came through it. A brown line of silt runs along the whitewash at knee height. The benches stand on end against the walls, dripping. Wet sand and weed lie across the floor in long tongues. The door stands open.]
[The board outside the door is bare. Two nail holes where the notices hung.]
[Seven chairs at the table under the window. Six seats are filled. In the middle chair, IVO, gloved, his coat dark to the knee with seawater. On the table beside one candle lies a thick black book with a red ribbon, open.]
[At a side desk, the CLERK, old and narrow, lays the green minute book's wet leaves out one by one to dry. The Keeper sits in the chair with the bad leg, by the door.]
[Objective: Attend the council.]
IVO: The harbor council is in session. Again. The chair thanks the seats for coming back.
HARBORMASTER: We've not come back, Magistrate. We've come for our coats.
IVO: Then the chair will be brief, while you find them.
IVO: One item. The book on this table.
IVO: The chair moves that it be entered as council property. Kept here, open, for any resident who wants to read it.
CHANDLER: Open. Now it's open.
IVO: Now, Chandler. Yes.
CLERK: The minute book's wet through, Magistrate. Front to the middle.
CLERK: I'll have to write tonight on the dry leaves at the back.
IVO: Then write it at the back. It belongs there.
GAGE: Read the motion again. My ears are still full of harbor.
IVO: That the chair's private record of the sky be entered as the council's. And lie open.
GAGE: Private. That's a nice word for it.
IVO: It is the accurate one, Councillor.
[The HARBORMASTER doesn't sit. He stands behind his chair with both hands on its back.]
HARBORMASTER: Item four. Fog-madness. You remember it, Magistrate?
IVO: I wrote it.
HARBORMASTER: My men wouldn't cross the pans after dusk. I asked if they were right not to.
HARBORMASTER: You asked me what I'd sell in February to buy the oil.
HARBORMASTER: You knew what February looked like. You had it written down. In that.
IVO: I had a great deal written down in that.
HARBORMASTER: My boy's on those pans. Cutting with his uncle. You knew that too.
IVO: You told this council. I minuted it.
HARBORMASTER: I put my hand up last for fog-madness. Minute that. Last, and wrong.
IVO: Clerk.
CLERK: "Harbormaster's hand. Last. Wrong." I've got it, Magistrate.
HARBORMASTER: Garrow's going on the thirtieth. First light. She says so, and this time she's put a date on it.
HARBORMASTER: My two boats go behind her. My boy goes in the second.
HARBORMASTER: I'll not sit in a chair that lied to my boats.
[He pushes the chair in, square to the table, the way you'd leave a pew. Then he walks out through the wet sand without looking back, and leaves the door as it was, open.]
[The CHANDLER has her figures book open in front of her, as she always does. She closes it.]
CHANDLER: I sold you my oil at cost. For the breakwater. Every gill in the shop.
CHANDLER: You said at cost'd look like a great deal of money in February. I wrote that down. I was pleased with it.
IVO: It will. It is still true.
CHANDLER: You'd done that sum a year back, hadn't you? Before the girl. Before any of it.
IVO: I had done most of it.
CHANDLER: I'm not going south. I'm not digging Abel Thorn's hole either.
CHANDLER: I'm going home to count my stock. What's left of it. Half's in the cellar with the sea.
CHANDLER: Somebody in this town should count something they can sell.
[She tucks the figures book under her arm, nods once to Gage, and goes.]
[The SHIPWRIGHT, a heavy man with tar on his cuffs, is already standing.]
SHIPWRIGHT: Abel Thorn's asking for carts at the pan-house. Dawn. I've two carts and a yard full of sand.
IVO: The crater is thirty-two paces across, Shipwright. Two carts of sand—
SHIPWRIGHT: Don't you tell me thirty-two. That's her number. The surveyor's.
SHIPWRIGHT: She paced it at noon with the marsh watching. And you sat in here and let her have it on her own.
[Ivo says nothing. The Clerk's pen stops.]
SHIPWRIGHT: I'll not sit here while a book tells me what's coming. I'll go and stop a bit of it.
[He goes. Three chairs stand empty.]
[Three seats are left: GAGE, old and bent, and two others who have never yet spoken in this room, and don't now.]
IVO: Councillor Gage.
GAGE: I'm stopping, if that's what you're asking.
GAGE: Fog-madness was my grandad's word. I gave it you. You put it on the door with a nail.
GAGE: I'll sit here till I've seen what it was holding up.
IVO: Three seats and the chair. Enough to sit. Just.
GAGE: Then sit. My knees aren't getting up twice in one night.
IVO: Those in favor.
[Hands rise along the table. Gage's comes up first this time, and stays up.]
IVO: Three. The chair does not vote. Carried.
IVO: Clerk. At the back. "Entered: the chair's own record of the sky, kept from the first move, and withheld from this council."
CLERK: Withheld, Magistrate? You want withheld?
IVO: Withheld. Spell it in full.
[The Clerk writes. It takes him longer than any line he has ever written in that book.]
[Through the open door: voices on the harbor road, and lanterns. More lanterns than the town has burned after supper in days.]
GAGE: Hark at them. Nobody's counting gills tonight.
IVO: No. Keeper.
KEEPER: Magistrate.
IVO: The surveyor will hear that it lies open. Anyone may read it.
KEEPER: You could tell her.
IVO: She has not spoken a word to me since the day she brought four degrees into this room.
IVO: I do not expect tonight to be the night she starts.
[Skip summary: On the night of day 25, hours after the flood, Ivo moves that his ledger become council property and lie open to anyone. The Harbormaster walks out to sail south behind Nell on the thirtieth, the Chandler walks out to count her stock, and the Shipwright walks out to give Abel his carts. Councillor Gage stays, and the three remaining seats carry it. Ivo has the clerk write that the record was "withheld".]

### Scene 2 — The smokehouse, ten o'clock
[Hester's smokehouse at ten. For the first time in days every lamp in the long room is lit, and every bench is full. Three tables, three knots of people, and HESTER going between them with a ladle.]
[Objective: Find Bram.]
HESTER: Keeper. In. Everybody else is.
HESTER: Nobody's counting gills tonight, and I'm not asking anybody to.
KEEPER: Is Bram here?
HESTER: Bram's been in twice. Took her pot up. Came down with it full. Took it up again.
HESTER: Sit. Three tables, three arguments, one soup. Sit where you like. Not on Nell.
[At the first table, NELL GARROW has a slate propped against the salt box, half covered in chalked names. She slaps the table.]
NELL: Keeper. Thirtieth. First light. *Patience* and three behind her. Harbormaster's two makes six.
NELL: The bad year was the sky. Not the fish. You heard him read it. The sky.
NELL: You can't follow fish south from a sky. You can get out from under it.
[TAM ROOK sits beside her with his hands in fresh linen, held off the table.]
TAM: It's the same sky down south, Nell.
NELL: It's a different bit of it, Rook.
TAM: It's the same bit. It's all one sky. I've looked.
NELL: Done.
TAM: You can't say done to a sky.
NELL: I can say it to you. Keeper. You coming south?
NELL: Somebody who can read a tide nobody can read. I'd find you a berth.
[Choice a: "I keep the light."]
[Choice b: "Ask me on the twenty-ninth."]
[Choices converge.]
NELL: Hm. Hollis said near enough the same. Only longer, with soup in it.
NELL: I asked him to crew. He's the best hand on a slip I ever grew up next to.
NELL: He said he'd a stair to climb every night. I said there's stairs in the south. He said not that one.

### Scene 3 — The lamp room, eleven

### Scene 4 — The customs house, midnight
[No music. The background track cuts as Sera comes through the open customs house door and does not come back until she leaves. Under the dialogue there is only seawater dripping from the upended benches onto the stone, slow.]

IVO: The word was not even true. It did not need to be.
IVO: Saltreach had lost the herring. Two families had gone. Every house in this harbor was a sum that came to February.

SERA: You bought it with me.
IVO: Yes.
IVO: You were the price. I knew it when I paid it.

SERA: Was it worth it? The month.

IVO: It bought Saltreach thirty more nights of thinking it was weather.
SERA: That's not an answer. That's a list.

SERA: Flint lit his light, and the tide was wrong, and you knew it was wrong.

SERA: Don't.
IVO: No. That was not a joke. I do not know what it was.
SERA: You told Bram.
IVO: Last night. In Mrs Pike's back room. He asked me for a day, to tell you himself.
SERA: He didn't.

IVO: I wanted someone to have tried.

IVO: I did.
SERA: It's nothing.

IVO: It weighs exactly that, Surveyor. Nothing, every night.

IVO: Surveyor.
[She doesn't turn.]
IVO: Sera.
[She turns.]
IVO: I am sorry.
[Nothing. Water ticks off a bench onto the stone, once, twice.]

IVO: All of it.
SERA: All isn't a number.
IVO: No. I know.

SERA: It doesn't change a figure. Not one.

IVO: I wanted to find out what it was for.

IVO: It is not for the one who hears it. I see that now.
SERA: No. It isn't.

IVO: It is not mine to keep. That was tonight's motion.

IVO: I know.
SERA: You had two records, and you chose one nobody could see.
IVO: Yes.

[Ivo stands at the table with his bare hands flat on the shut ledger. He doesn't sit.]

### Scene 5 — The customs house steps

### Scene 7 — The customs house steps, dawn

### Scene 8 — The lamp room, morning
[The lamp room, mid-morning. SERA sits on the floor against the full wall with the breakwater log open on her knees, reading the keeper's dusk and three o'clock lines, one finger moving down the page. She reads it every morning now.]
[WREN is at the glass, scratching at her forearms where the sleeves stop. The light in her is fainter by day, but it's there.]
[The Keeper comes up the last turn with the parcel.]
[Objective: Walk the crater road.]
SERA: Keeper. Three o'clock.
KEEPER: Still ahead.
SERA: Further than yesterday. I can read. I wanted to hear you say it.
WREN: Keeper! You've got a parcel! It's got string. Presents have string. Is it a present?
SERA: Put it there.

SERA: Whose hand?
KEEPER: The clerk's.
SERA: And whose figures?
[Choice a: "The clerk copied them."]
[Choice b: "You know whose."]
[Choices converge.]
SERA: Same thing.
WREN: Is it Ivo's? Is it his book? Is it the whole book in a little book?
SERA: It's figures.
WREN: Figures can be presents. You gave me sixty-one seconds once. I still have them.
[Sera gets up. She crosses to the cradle step and stands over the parcel. She doesn't touch the string.]
SERA: Not the tides.
KEEPER: Sky only. He said you have your own tides.
[Sera's jaw moves. She doesn't answer that.]
SERA: How thick?
KEEPER: Thick.
SERA: Thicker than the wall?
KEEPER: I didn't measure it.
SERA: You always measure. You measured the bowl with me. Thirty-two.
KEEPER: Two fingers. Near enough.

SERA: Leave it there. Don't open it. Don't let Wren open it.
WREN: I wasn't going to! I was only going to look at the string very hard.

SERA: They won't fill it.
WREN: Is that a sum? It sounded like a sum.
SERA: It's a sum.
WREN: Is it the crater? Where Bram found me? Are they filling in where I was?

WREN: If they fill it in, is it still where I came from?
WREN: Or is it just a field then?
SERA: It's still thirty-two paces. Under the sand. Sand doesn't change a width.
WREN: That's nice. That it keeps its size even when nobody can see it.
WREN: I'd like to be like that. Keep my size. Even when nobody's...
[She stops, and looks at her own forearm where the sleeve ends.]
WREN: ...That's a silly thing to want, isn't it? With my sleeves. Ask the sleeves.
SERA: It's not silly.

SERA: It's different.
WREN: I remember it was warm. The glass. It was the first warm thing.
WREN: Before that it was very quiet. Not empty quiet. Full quiet. Like a room with everybody holding their breath.

WREN: My arms are thinking about itching. They're not doing it yet. They're thinking about it.
SERA: Since when?
WREN: Since I woke up. It's the going-somewhere kind. It's going to step. Not now. Later. Is later a time?
SERA: Later's a time. Keeper. Be on the crater road by dusk.
SERA: Sight off the tower top. Arm straight. Bring it back on your hand.
KEEPER: You'll count from here?
SERA: From here. With her.

BRAM: Morning. I'm going to the crater. Not to dig.

WREN: Can I come?
BRAM: No, Sparrow.
WREN: Because it's where I came from?

### Scene 9 — The slip, noon
[The main slip at noon on day 26. *Patience* lies against the quay with her hatches open. Her crew carry nets and salt barrels down the slip. Behind her, two smaller boats with the Harbormaster's paint on their bows. A boy of about twelve carries an oar longer than he is.]

TAM: They do, a bit. That's why you don't make boats of them.

HESTER: You'll not.

NELL: ...Done.

TAM: She didn't slap anything.
NELL: Shut up, Rook.

KEEPER: I saw.

NELL: That's my point.
NELL: Thirtieth. First light. Tell Vant. No. Don't tell her anything. She'll have worked it out before I said it.
HESTER: She'll have it to the minute.
NELL: She'll have it to the minute, and she'll be right, and I'll be gone. Done.

HESTER: Go on, haddock. Bram went up the north road an hour back with a lamp he hasn't lit.

TAM: That's a job. Wren says.
HESTER: Wren's right about more than she lets on.
KEEPER: How's Jory?
TAM: Lit the breakwater on the dot again. Still not talking.
TAM: He ate, though. Hester left bread on the step and it went. That's nearly a word.
HESTER: That's a whole sentence, from a Flint.

### Scene 10 — The crater, afternoon
[The crater in the afternoon. The bowl of fused glass, thirty-two paces across, catches a flat grey light. Round its north lip the crowd works: carts tipped, sand shovelled down over the glass, children carrying it in buckets, women in a line with coal scoops.]

MAGS: Tip it from the lip, Shipwright, or don't tip it.

### Scene 11 — The crater, before dusk

BRAM: Crater patrol. It's dusk. I'd have come if it were raining frogs.

CORRA: Show me. Arm straight how?

CORRA: That's a finger. Is that a finger? I've got thin fingers.

ADA: Walking. That's the ones from my lane. Bram, that's them.
BRAM: Haze-walkers. Everybody in the light. Everybody. Now.
